# Credit Information Law

## Connections

### [[Credit Information Company]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing who is authorised to operate as a credit bureau in KSA, treat 'Companies' as a defined status created and constrained by this Law, not a generic label. Article 1 defines credit information companies as entities 'licensed to collect and maintain credit information ... and provide the same to members,' and Article 12 makes operating without a SAMA licence a violation. Conclude that any entity collecting/exchanging consumer credit data must hold a SAMA licence and that the Law's confidentiality, accuracy and consumer-consent obligations attach to that licensed status.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 7 / Art 12):** "engaging in activities of credit information companies without obtaining a license from SAMA"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 2 / Art 1):** "Companies: Credit information companies licensed to collect and maintain credit information on consumers and provide the same to members upon request"

### [[Credit Record]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When advising on consumer rights over their credit data, anchor them in the Law's defined 'Credit Record' — a report issued by companies containing consumer credit information — because that definition triggers the Article 9 rights (consent to establish, free copy on decline, right to annotate, right to correct errors and escalate to the committee). The Law also imposes maintenance duties on companies under Article 10. Conclude that any dispute over record accuracy, access fees, or unlawful requests is governed by these statutory rights and the committee mechanism, not by contract alone.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 2 / Art 1):** "Credit Record: A report issued by companies containing consumer credit information"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / Art 9):** "A consumer credit record may not be established with companies for the first time except with the written consent of the consumer"

### [[Implementing Regulations]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining the full compliance perimeter under the Credit Information Law, do not rely on the primary Law alone — it repeatedly delegates operative detail (fees, update timelines, retention periods, committee mechanics, licensing controls) to the Implementing Regulations, which SAMA's Governor must issue within 180 days and publish in the Official Gazette. Article 12 further makes breach of the Implementing Regulations itself a violation carrying Article 13 penalties (fines up to one million riyals, suspension, or licence revocation). Conclude that any assessment of update-timing or retention obligations must be verified against the current Implementing Regulations, not just the Law's articles.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 9 / Art 16):** "The Governor of SAMA shall issue the Implementing Regulations of this Law within (180) days ... and ... shall be published in the Official Gazette"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / Art 12(8), Art 13):** "any other violation of the provisions of this Law or its Implementing Regulations ... a fine not exceeding one million riyals"
