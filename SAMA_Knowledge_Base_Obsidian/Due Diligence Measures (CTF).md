# Due Diligence Measures (CTF)

### [[AML Due Diligence Measures]] — `semantically_similar_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** These two due diligence regimes appear closely parallel but govern distinct predicate offences — one targets terrorist financing (CTF), the other money laundering (AML) — so do not assume compliance with one automatically satisfies the other. Both impose risk-based CDD, permit postponed verification for low risk, require refusal/termination plus reporting where DD is not feasible, and allow reliance on third parties, but each rests on its own Law and Implementing Regulation with separate reporting channels. Because this link is inferred from conceptual similarity, verify the specific triggers, thresholds and enhanced/simplified DD rules in each primary text before treating obligations as interchangeable, particularly the tipping-off and third-party reliance conditions that differ in wording.

### [[Beneficial Owner Identification]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a firm's CTF onboarding is complete, treat beneficial owner identification as an integral element of the due diligence obligation, not an optional add-on. Within the same Implementing Regulation, verifying the identity of the person who owns or controls 25% or more of a legal person is one of the prescribed due diligence measures, and the 25% threshold (with fallback to the natural person exercising control) is the operative test. You would conclude that CDD on a legal-person customer is deficient unless the ultimate beneficial owner has been identified and verified from a reliable and independent source per this threshold.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 9 / Art 16(5)):** "shall apply due diligence measures to all business relationships according to risk level... applying measures of due diligence to current customers and beneficial owners"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / Art 16):** "verifying the identity of the person who owns or controls (25%) or more of the legal person... the identity of the natural person exercising control over the legal person shall be verified by all means possible"

### [[Implementing Regulation Combating Terrorist Crimes Financing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a firm's CTF obligations, read due diligence not as a standalone control but as a mandatory component of the internal policies the Implementing Regulation requires under Article 67 of the Law. The Regulation (Articles 17–18) obliges financial institutions and DNFBPs to apply risk-based due diligence at defined trigger points and to embed those measures — including risk management prior to customer verification — into board-approved policies. You would therefore verify that a firm's CTF policy framework operationalises the specific due diligence triggers in Article 17 (account opening, occasional transactions, wire transfers, suspicion), since a policy silent on these is non-compliant with the Regulation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 7 / Art 17):** "Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions shall apply the due diligence measures referred to in Article 64 of the Law"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / Art 18):** "provisions relating to the measures provided in the Law and Regulations, including those relating to risk management procedures of business relationships taken prior to completion of customer verification"

### [[Know Your Customer (KYC)]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping a DTFC's customer-onboarding duties, these two provisions appear to reinforce each other: the DTFC KYC chapter and the CTF Regulations' due diligence measures both require identifying the customer before opening an account, refusing the relationship where verification is not feasible, and reporting to a SAMA/General Directorate of Financial Intelligence channel. However, they are distinct regimes — the DTFC Rules govern deposit-taking prudential/account conduct, while Article 17/18 measures implement the CTF Law across all financial institutions and DNFBPs. Treat this as a lead: verify that the DTFC's KYC program independently satisfies the CTF Regulation's risk-based due diligence and reporting articles, since the account-opening rules do not themselves discharge the AML/CTF obligations.
- **Grounding — this node (SAMA_EN_132 Page 7-9 / Art 17-18):** "apply the due diligence measures ... prior to opening an account ... refuse to open an account ... report the matter to the General Directorate of Financial Intelligence"
