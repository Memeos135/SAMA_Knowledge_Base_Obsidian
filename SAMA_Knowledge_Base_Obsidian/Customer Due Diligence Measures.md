# Customer Due Diligence Measures

## Connections

### [[Guide to Rules Governing Banks' Remittance Centers]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the AML/CTF obligations of a bank remittance center, treat CDD as a mandatory operational requirement of this Guide, not a general principle: the Guide expressly directs centers to apply essential due diligence to all permanent and temporary customers including real beneficiaries, on a continuous, risk-based basis. Note the defined-term interaction — the Guide's 'customer' is the person performing a financial transaction (membership holder or temporary customer), so CDD scope tracks both categories. A compliance reader should conclude that identity verification, beneficial-owner identification, and enhanced measures for high-risk relationships (PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions) are enforceable minimums a center must evidence for each transaction type.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 12 / Section 9.6):** "remittance centers apply the essential due diligence procedures to all permanent and temporary customers, including real beneficiaries, and that these procedures be continuous"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / Section 9):** "taking into account the full compliance with regulations and instructions issued by SAMA, AML/CFT-related instructions, 'Know Your Customer (KYC)' requirements, and Customer Due Diligence (CDD) procedures"

### [[Know Your Customer Principle]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a remittance center's AML/CFT customer obligations, treat KYC and CDD as two limbs of one continuous requirement rather than separable tasks, because the Guide lists them together as mandatory conditions for accepting and maintaining a customer relationship. KYC governs identity verification at membership creation while CDD extends to ongoing monitoring, beneficial-owner identification, and enhanced measures for high-risk customers and PEPs. For a compliance decision you would conclude that satisfying one does not discharge the other — verification at onboarding plus continuous due diligence are both required, and the text appears to make failure to verify identity or sources a ground for terminating the relationship, though you should confirm the exact article wording before relying on it.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 12 / Section 9.6):** "remittance centers apply the essential due diligence procedures to all permanent and temporary customers, including real beneficiaries, and that these procedures be continuous"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / Section 9):** "'Know Your Customer (KYC)' requirements, and Customer Due Diligence (CDD) procedures for customers of various types and categories"
- **Caveat:** Relation is INFERRED; the two concepts are listed together as related requirements but the Guide does not formally define one in terms of the other. Verify section text before relying.
