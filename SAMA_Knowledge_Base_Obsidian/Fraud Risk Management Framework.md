# Fraud Risk Management Framework

## Connections

### [[Fraud Risk Appetite]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When testing governance of fraud risk tolerance, note that Fraud Risk Appetite is a required element operating within the Fraud Risk Management Framework: the FRMF is the overarching structure that must be defined, approved, implemented and aligned to enterprise risk management, and appetite (with its board endorsement, thresholds and crisis-escalation triggers) is one of its constituent controls. This means appetite obligations inherit the framework's approval and alignment requirements. Conclude that a compliance review of Fraud Risk Appetite should verify it sits within, and is consistent with, an approved FRMF rather than existing as a detached statement.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / 4.1 Risk Management):** "A Fraud Risk Management Framework should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organisation's enterprise risk management process."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 30 / 4.1.3 (Fraud Risk Appetite)):** "Fraud Risk Appetite should be reviewed on at least an annual basis and be formally endorsed by the Board."

### [[Fraud Risk Assessment]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the Risk Management (Prevent) obligations, treat the Fraud Risk Assessment as a required component sitting within the broader Fraud Risk Management Framework, which itself must be defined, approved, implemented and aligned to the enterprise risk management process. This means an FRA is not a standalone deliverable — its adequacy is judged as part of whether the overall FRMF is in place and effective. Conclude that reviewing the FRA requires also confirming the governing framework exists and is board/senior-management approved, since the framework is the parent obligation the FRA operates under.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / 4.1 Risk Management):** "A Fraud Risk Management Framework should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organisation's enterprise risk management process."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 28 / 4.1.2 Fraud Risk Assessment):** "A Member Organisation should conduct an enterprise-wide Fraud Risk Assessment as part of its Counter-Fraud Programme."

### [[Intelligence Monitoring]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping the scope of a firm's Fraud Risk Management Framework, treat Intelligence Monitoring as a component sub-process within it rather than a separate obligation: Intelligence Monitoring (4.1.1) sits under the Risk Management principle (4.1) that requires the Framework to be defined, approved and implemented and aligned to enterprise risk management. Conclude that framework-level compliance cannot be evidenced without a functioning Intelligence Monitoring process, and that gaps in monitoring effectiveness (which must be periodically evaluated) reflect directly on the Framework's adequacy.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / 4.1):** "A Fraud Risk Management Framework should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organisation's enterprise risk management process"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 27 / 4.1.1):** "Member Organisations should draw on a variety of internal and external data sources to identify and monitor emerging fraud threats"

### [[Key Risk Indicators]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing the Fraud Risk Management Framework, note that its effectiveness must be measured using indicators, and KRIs (4.1.4) are a defined mechanism for measuring position against Fraud Risk Appetite and providing early warning of increasing exposure within that framework. Both provisions sit under the same Risk Management principle (4.1), so KRIs are part of how the Framework demonstrates it is working, not an optional add-on. Conclude that a firm should be able to show KRIs are embedded in the Framework's measurement and periodic evaluation; a framework lacking defined, methodology-based KRIs would not satisfy the measurement expectation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / 4.1(b)):** "The effectiveness of the Fraud Risk Management Framework should be measured and periodically evaluated using Key Performance Indicators"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 30 / 4.1.4):** "KRIs to monitor exposure against the risks identified in the Fraud Risk Assessment"

### [[Prevent Domain]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping obligations under SAMA's Counter-Fraud Framework, treat the Fraud Risk Management Framework as a mandated sub-domain (4.1) sitting inside the Prevent domain, not as a standalone policy. Section 4 opens the Prevent domain and requires that a Fraud Risk Management Framework be 'defined, approved and implemented' and aligned with enterprise risk management, with Fraud Risk Assessment, Fraud Risk Appetite and KRIs as its minimum components. The consequence: the risk-assessment output is what drives where prevention activity is focused, so a compliance reviewer should verify the FRA feeds the prevention standards and appetite/limits rather than assessing the two in isolation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / Section 4.1):** "A Fraud Risk Management Framework should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organisation's enterprise risk management process."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 26 / Section 4):** "An effective Counter-Fraud Programme includes fraud prevention processes and controls to facilitate the identification of threats and mitigate the risk of fraud occurring."
