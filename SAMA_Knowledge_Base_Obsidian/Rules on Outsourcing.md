# Rules on Outsourcing

## Connections

### [[DBC Outsourcing Requirement]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When advising a DBC Company on outsourcing, do not assume the bank-focused Rules on Outsourcing apply verbatim; treat them as an analogous framework whose principles (board responsibility, materiality assessment, SAMA access, business continuity) inform but do not automatically govern DBC arrangements. The DBC Rules list 'Outsourcing risk' as a required risk-management item and impose parallel duties (records retention, business continuity, information security), but the two instruments are separate regimes under different licensing bases. Verify from the primary DBC text and any express SAMA cross-reference whether the banking Outsourcing Rules are formally imposed on finance/DBC companies before relying on their detailed provisions.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8 / II.G):** "The Board of Directors of the bank retains the ultimate responsibility for the outsourcing policy and all outsourcing arrangements"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / Art 19):** "Such policy shall cover the analysis of at least the following risks:... k) Outsourcing risk"
- **Caveat:** The banking Rules on Outsourcing (2389) address banks; their application to DBC/finance companies is not established by the provided text — confirm any express extension by SAMA before relying on it.

### [[Foreign Bank Branch Outsourcing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping outsourcing obligations for a foreign bank branch, treat this as a stricter sub-regime under the general Rules on Outsourcing, not a lighter one. Section vi imposes branch-specific requirements: KSA business must be booked locally, key management plus compliance and AML/CFT functions may NOT be outsourced at all, and outsourcing to the Head Office or a related party does not diminish the branch's obligations to comply with Saudi laws. You would conclude that even intra-group arrangements attract annual audit and immediate reporting to SAMA, and cannot be relied on to shift AML/CFT accountability off the KSA branch.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8 / Governance F.11):** "The Board of Directors of the bank retains the ultimate responsibility for the outsourcing policy and all outsourcing arrangements, including compliance with all relevant legal and regulatory requirements."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / Section vi Art 45):** "functions such as compliance and Anti-Money Laundering (AML)/Combatting the Financing of Terrorism (CFT) are not allowed to be outsourced... Outsourcing to Head Office or a related party does not diminish the obligations"

### [[Material Outsourcing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When classifying an outsourcing arrangement, the Material/non-Material distinction is the trigger that determines which heightened duties apply, so classify before you assess obligations. The Rules attach specific requirements to Material outsourcing — a business-case analysis before entry, separate contingency plans, immediate SAMA notification on termination, mandatory internal-audit review, and enhanced register entries. You would conclude that mislabelling a function as non-Material strips these obligations away improperly, so document the materiality reasoning and the date of the last assessment as the register requires.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8 / Art 16):** "Banks must be able to demonstrate to SAMA that, in assessing the options for outsourcing a Material business function or activity to a third party, it has: a) Prepared and analyzed a business case"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 13 / Art 37, R.40):** "Where a Material outsourcing agreement is terminated, banks must notify SAMA immediately... Whether it is considered Material or not, the reasons why it is considered as such"

### [[Third-Party Service Provider]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining whether the Rules bite on a given contract, apply the defined term 'Third-Party Service Provider' precisely, because the definition both scopes in and scopes out. A TPSP is any entity undertaking the outsourced activity on behalf of the bank, but the definition expressly excludes Head Offices and related entities of foreign bank branches from being 'Third-Party Service Providers.' You would conclude that intra-group arrangements are still governed by the branch-specific rules (Section vi) even though the counterparty is not a TPSP, and that overseas TPSPs additionally require a written SAMA no-objection.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 12 / Art 33-34):** "Banks are required to ensure that for all outsourcing arrangements, SAMA has unrestricted... access to data pertaining to the outsourcing, if located at the premises of the third-party service provider"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Definitions):** "An entity undertaking the outsourced activity on behalf of the Banks. (Head Offices and Related entities of Foreign Bank Branches operating in KSA are not considered as Third-Party Service Providers)"
