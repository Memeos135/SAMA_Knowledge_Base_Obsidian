# Counter-Fraud Department

## Connections

### [[Fraud Remediation]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When identifying which function owns remediation of fraud incidents, attribute it to the Counter-Fraud Department: its minimum responsibilities expressly include performing comprehensive investigations, identifying root causes, and documenting corrective actions — the substance of Fraud Remediation. This places accountability for closing out incidents within the day-to-day operation of the Counter-Fraud Programme. A reviewer should therefore test the Department's investigation and corrective-action records as evidence of remediation, rather than looking to a separate business-unit process.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 20):** "Member Organisations should establish and maintain a Counter-Fraud Department that has responsibility for the day-to-day operation of the Counter-Fraud Programme."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 20):** "Performing comprehensive fraud investigations, identifying root causes of fraud incidents and documenting corrective actions."

### [[Governance Domain]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping who is accountable for what under SAMA's Counter-Fraud Framework, treat the Counter-Fraud Department as a mandated component of the Governance domain, not an optional operational unit. The Framework's Governance domain (Section 3) requires a Governance Structure and expressly locates the Counter-Fraud Department under sub-domain 3.5, giving it day-to-day operational responsibility for the Counter-Fraud Programme (monitoring policy compliance, fraud risk assessment, SAMA intelligence-sharing, reporting to senior management). A compliance reviewer should therefore verify that the Department exists, is adequately resourced, and reports through an approved line — its absence is a Governance-domain gap, not merely a process weakness.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 20 / sub-domain 3.5):** "Member Organisations should establish and maintain a Counter-Fraud Department that has responsibility for the day-to-day operation of the Counter-Fraud Programme."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 14 / Section 3 Governance):** "a Governance Structure with associated internal and external reporting aligned to the organisation's size and complexity to monitor and oversee fraud risk management."

### [[Head of Counter-Fraud]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping accountability under SAMA's Counter-Fraud Framework, treat the Counter-Fraud Department and the Head of Counter-Fraud as distinct but linked roles: the Department (a function Member Organisations must establish) runs day-to-day operations of the Counter-Fraud Programme, while the Head of Counter-Fraud is the accountable individual owning strategy, policy, the Fraud Risk Assessment, Fraud Risk Appetite and fraud KRIs. This means when assessing whether an organisation meets the framework, you check both an operational function AND a named senior accountability owner — the two cannot be conflated. Conclude that gaps in personal accountability (Head role) are separate compliance findings from gaps in departmental operations.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 20 / section 4 (Counter-Fraud Department)):** "Member Organisations should establish and maintain a Counter-Fraud Department that has responsibility for the day-to-day operation of the Counter-Fraud Programme"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 18 / section (Head of Counter-Fraud)):** "The Head of Counter-Fraud should be accountable for: 1. Developing, implementing, and maintaining: a. Counter-Fraud Strategy. b. Counter-Fraud Policy..."

### [[Supervisory Notifications]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assigning who discharges the SAMA notification duty, note that the Counter-Fraud Department's mandate to 'share Counter-Fraud Intelligence with SAMA' operationalises the separate Supervisory Notifications obligation, which requires immediate notification to the SAMA General Department of Cyber Risk Control of new typologies and significant fraud incidents. These are enforceable reporting requirements triggered even where no financial loss occurred. A compliance reviewer should confirm the Department's remit explicitly covers the immediacy and trigger events specified in section 3.7, and not assume routine intelligence-sharing satisfies the mandatory immediate-notification standard.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 20):** "Sharing Counter-Fraud Intelligence with SAMA and other organisations in the sector."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 22 / Sec 3.7):** "Member Organisations should notify the SAMA General Department of Cyber Risk Control immediately of ... Any new fraudulent typology whether it resulted in financial loss or not."
