# Alternative Liquidity Approaches (ALA) Options

## Connections

### [[Basel Committee]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing the standing of the ALA guidance in Annex 3, understand that these options exist within a Basel Committee framework and that jurisdictional eligibility is externally validated, not self-declared. The document ties ALA use to Basel Committee oversight — notification of transition plans, self-assessment of eligibility, and an independent peer review conducted as part of the Committee's work programme. Conclude that a bank's ability to rely on ALA options in KSA depends on the jurisdiction's continuing eligibility under that Basel-supervised process, and that SAMA-level rules governing usage sit beneath this international framework; verify the current eligibility status rather than assuming the options remain permanently available.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 69 / Annex 3):** "Guidance on standards governing banks' usage of the options for alternative liquidity approaches (ALA) under LCR"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 68 / para 41):** "The Basel Committee will oversee the independent peer review process for determining the eligibility of its member jurisdictions to adopt alternative treatment."

### [[Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a bank's LCR compliance in a jurisdiction with insufficient HQLA, read the ALA options guidance as subordinate to and conditioned by the LCR standard, not as a free-standing alternative. Both live in the same SAMA/Basel LCR document (SAMA_EN_3623): the ALA options exist only to meet the LCR where eligible HQLA is short, and Annex 3 caps their use to the actual shortfall and imposes supervisory reporting. Conclude that a bank cannot use ALA options above the level needed to meet its LCR, must report usage to the supervisor regularly, and that certain options (e.g. Option 1 committed facilities) may require specific central-bank approval.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 69 / Annex 3):** "A bank should not use an alternative treatment to meet its LCR more than its actual need as reflected by the shortfall of eligible HQLA."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 7 / Introduction):** "ensuring that banks have an adequate stock of unencumbered high-quality liquid assets (HQLA)... to meet their liquidity needs for a 30 calendar day liquidity stress scenario."

### [[Option 1 Committed Liquidity Facility|Option 1: Committed Liquidity Facility]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank can rely on a committed liquidity facility to plug an HQLA shortfall, treat Option 1 as one of the ALA options that is only available subject to the general usage rules in Annex 3 — meaning it cannot exceed the actual currency shortfall and usage must be reported to the supervisor. The ALA guidance expressly flags that Option 1 typically requires central bank approval of the committed facility, so the facility is not self-executing. You should conclude that eligibility to count an Option 1 facility in the LCR depends both on SAMA/central bank approval and on the jurisdiction's demonstrated economic capacity to support it, not on the bank's election alone.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 69, Annex 3 / General rules (i) and para 3):** "For example, use of Option 1 will typically require central bank approval of the committed facility."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 61, Principle 2 Criterion (a)):** "For Option 1 ... the jurisdiction must have the economic strength to support the committed liquidity facilities granted by its central bank."

### [[Option 2 Foreign Currency HQLA|Option 2: Foreign Currency HQLA]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating whether a bank may hold foreign-currency HQLA to cover a domestic-currency liquidity need, read Option 2 as governed by the same ALA general rules (usage limited to the actual shortfall, supervisory reporting) plus its own currency-mismatch controls. The framework only permits Option 2 where the shortfall is genuine and the currency mismatch is 'justifiable and controlled within limits agreed by their supervisors,' with a haircut for FX risk. You should conclude that reliance on Option 2 is contingent on supervisor-agreed mismatch limits and freely/reliably convertible currencies — it is not a free substitution of any foreign HQLA.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 69, Annex 3 / General rules):** "A bank should not use an alternative treatment to meet its LCR more than its actual need as reflected by the shortfall of eligible HQLA."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 23, para 59):** "hold HQLA in a currency that does not match ... provided that the resulting currency mismatch positions are justifiable and controlled within limits agreed by their supervisors."

### [[Option 3 Additional Level 2A Assets|Option 3: Additional Level 2A Assets]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When considering whether a bank may hold Level 2A assets beyond the standard 40% cap, treat Option 3 as an ALA option subject to the general usage limits and to a stricter asset-quality gate: only Level 2A assets 'of a quality comparable to that for Level 1 assets' may exceed the cap, and they carry a minimum 20% haircut versus the ordinary 15%. Level 2B assets remain capped at 15% regardless. You should conclude that Option 3 does not relax quality standards — it tightens them (e.g. higher minimum ratings, higher haircut) — so counting excess Level 2A in the LCR depends on meeting those enhanced criteria, not merely on availability of Level 2A paper.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 24):** "These additional Level 2A assets would be subject to a minimum haircut of 20%, ie 5% higher than the 15% haircut applicable to Level 2A assets that are included in the 40% cap."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 64, Principle 2 Criterion (c)):** "the jurisdiction must only allow Level 2 assets that are of a quality ... comparable to that for Level 1 assets in its currency to be used under this option."
