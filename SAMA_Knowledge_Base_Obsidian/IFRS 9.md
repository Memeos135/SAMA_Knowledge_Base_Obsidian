# IFRS 9

### [[Expected Credit Loss Provisioning]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When testing whether a finance company's ECL provisioning is compliant, recognize that the Rules build the ECL concept directly on IFRS 9 mechanics: expected credit loss, its 12-month and lifetime variants, and the PD/EAD/LGD components are all defined 'as prescribed by IFRS 9.' The Rules layer SAMA-specific minimums (annual methodology review, quarterly reassessment, Board reporting) on top of that accounting standard. You would conclude that ECL measurement must satisfy both the IFRS 9 accounting basis and SAMA's governance/frequency overlays—compliance with one does not evidence the other.

### [[Rules Governing Credit Risk Exposure Classification and Provisioning]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When applying these Rules, read the core measurement terms (PD, EAD, LGD, ECL, staging, past due) as anchored in IFRS 9, but note SAMA overlays its own regulatory categories on top. The Rules explicitly define each metric 'as prescribed by IFRS 9' and map IFRS 9 stages to regulatory categories (Regular, Special Monitoring, Substandard, Doubtful, Loss), with sub-stages (2A/2B, 3A/3B) used for regulatory reporting only, not accounting. You should conclude that IFRS 9 supplies the accounting foundation while SAMA's staging/category framework governs regulatory classification, and that the earlier Provisions Guidelines definitions (Circular 381000046342) are superseded and must no longer be used.
