# Banks Investment Rules

## Connections

### [[Approval Requirements and Controls]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing what pre-clearance a bank needs before investing, treat Section 8 as the binding approval gate within these Rules. It requires SAMA non-objection for investments requiring regulatory approval under the Banking Control Law and Ministerial Decree No. 3/2149, with prior non-objection also needed for any change to their structure or limits. Conclude that certain investments and any limit changes cannot proceed on the bank's own governance authority alone; SAMA sign-off is a condition precedent, and MMF investment additionally requires CMA-licensed channels.

### [[Banking Control Law]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** To establish the legal force and scope of these Rules, note they derive authority from the Central Bank Law (Royal Decree M/36) and the Banking Control Law (Royal Decree M/5), and apply to all banks licensed under the Banking Control Law. This makes the Rules subordinate implementing measures, not standalone policy — they inherit their enforceability and their addressee (licensed banks) from that parent law, and Section 8's approval requirement itself rests on Banking Control Law provisions plus Ministerial Decree No. 3/2149. Conclude that compliance is mandatory for Banking-Control-Law-licensed banks and that the underlying law governs where the Rules are silent.
- **Grounding — this node (SAMA_EN_3366_VER1 Page 4):** "These Rules are issued in accordance with the authority vested in SAMA under the Central Bank Law... and the Banking Control Law issued via Royal Decree No. M/5 dated 22/2/1386H"

### [[Central Bank Law]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing the legal force of the Banks Investment Rules, treat them as subordinate exercise of SAMA's statutory authority rather than free-standing guidance, because the Rules are expressly issued under the Central Bank Law (Royal Decree No. M/36) together with the Banking Control Law. This means the enforceability of every 'should/must' obligation in the Rules traces back to SAMA's supervisory mandate under that primary law. In a compliance decision you would treat non-compliance with the Rules as a supervisory breach reviewable under SAMA's charter powers, and read any ambiguity in the Rules against the parent law's objectives.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Sec 1 Introduction):** "These Rules are issued in accordance with the authority vested in Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) under the Central Bank Law issued via Royal Decree No. M/36 dated 11/04/1442H"

### [[Investment Governance Requirements]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping who is accountable for investment decisions, do not read the Investment Rules as merely portfolio-level guidance — Section 6 imposes concrete governance obligations, making the Board ultimately responsible for oversight and for approving the Investment Policy and Strategy, with a management-level committee accountable for approvals, limits and risk assessment. The governance requirements are an integral, mandatory component of the parent Rules, not optional best practice. In a compliance review you would check for a documented board-approved Investment Policy, a functioning investment committee, and (for Foreign Bank Branches) that head/regional office assumes the board's oversight role.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Sec 2.2):** "ensure the alignment with sound risk management and governance practices"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Sec 6.2):** "The Board of Directors of a bank is ultimately responsible for the oversight of the bank's investments. The Board or its delegated authority is responsible for approving the internal Investment Policy and Strategy."

### [[Risk Management and Monitoring]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining the obligations attaching to investment activity, treat Section 7 as binding requirements: banks must align investments with their risk management framework, maintain a system to identify, measure and manage investment risks, conduct annual portfolio stress-testing, and provide results to SAMA on request. Critically, Section 7.3 embeds a cross-regime obligation — banks must satisfy all SAMA AML/CTF requirements before engaging in any investment. In a compliance decision you would treat investment onboarding and counterparty dealing as also triggering AML/CTF duties, not just prudential risk controls.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Sec 2.1):** "Ensure banks put in place a comprehensive internal investment framework, with proper risk management in accordance with SAMA regulatory requirements."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / Sec 7.3):** "Before engaging in any investments, Banks should comply with all relevant Anti Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing requirements issued by SAMA."
