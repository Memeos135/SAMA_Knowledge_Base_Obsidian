# Related Parties Rules for Banks

## Connections

### [[Banking Control Law Article 9]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing the legal enforceability of the Related Parties Rules, treat them as subordinate implementing rules that operationalize a primary statutory obligation rather than as free-standing SAMA policy. The Rules are issued under the Banking Control Law and expressly 'reinforce and provide further guidance to Article 9,' and Section 6 confirms Article 9 is the source of the substantive prohibition on granting unsecured loans, facilities or guarantees to specified parties. For a compliance decision this means a bank cannot satisfy the security requirement by reading the Rules alone; the binding obligation traces to Article 9 of the Banking Control Law, and any dispute over scope of 'parties specified in the law' must be resolved against that statute.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Section 1):** "These rules define related parties... and reinforce and provide further guidance to Article 9 of the Banking Control Law."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / Section 6):** "Article 9 of the Banking Control Law requires banks not to grant, without security, a loan or credit facilities, or issue a guarantee or incur any other financial liability in respect to parties specified in the law."

### [[ExposureTransaction Limits|Exposure/Transaction Limits]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When quantifying permitted related-party exposure, the binding numerical caps live in Section 5 of these Rules, not merely in the general governance provisions. The Rules set hard limits — 5% of eligible capital per non-bank related party (10% if a Saudi-listed company, 25% for a non-bank financial-sector subsidiary) and a 50% cumulative cap — enforceable 'at all times,' with breaches requiring immediate notification to SAMA and an action plan. For a compliance decision you should treat any excess as a reportable breach exposing the bank to punitive supervisory action, and confirm which limit applies based on the counterparty's classification.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 9 / Section 5.1):** "The sum of all exposures/transactions values a bank has to a non-bank related party must not be higher than 5% of the bank's available eligible capital base at all times."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 9 / Section 5.1.iv):** "A cumulative limit on all exposures/transactions to non-bank related parties shall be 50% of the banks eligible capital."

### [[Related Party Definition]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Before applying any exposure limit or governance control, determine whether a counterparty meets the defined term 'Related Party,' because the entire regime turns on that classification. The Rules define Related Party to include substantial shareholders, board members of the bank or its subsidiaries/affiliates and their relatives, and Shariah Committee members, and tie the trigger to a 'Control Relationship' (deemed at >50% voting rights plus qualitative influence tests). For a compliance decision, scope the population first: mislabelling a counterparty as non-related would wrongly exempt it from the 5%/10%/25%/50% caps, arm's-length pricing, and board-approval and recusal requirements.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8 / Section 4):** "A bank is required to have adequate systems and controls in place to identify, measure, monitor and report related party exposures/transactions."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / Section 2.vi):** "Related Party: a. Substantial Shareholders of the bank. b. Board members of the bank or any of its subsidiaries/ affiliates ... and their relatives."
