# Prevent Domain

## Connections

### [[Authentication Standard]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping fraud-prevention obligations, treat the Authentication Standard (sub-domain 4.4) as a mandated Prevent-domain control, so authentication weaknesses are assessed as prevention-control failures under the Framework. The Prevent domain requires proactive controls to stop fraud before harm, and expressly cites authentication among the systems and technology implemented to prevent fraud; the standard must be risk-based, use multi-factor authentication, and be built jointly by Counter-Fraud and Cyber Security with reference to the Cyber Security Framework's identity/access and electronic-banking controls. A reviewer should confirm the authentication standard covers customers, employees and third parties across digital and non-digital channels and is calibrated to the Fraud Risk Assessment — cross-checking the referenced Cyber Security Framework controls (3.3.5, 3.3.13) rather than relying on Prevent-domain text alone.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 40 / Section 4 Prevent):** "Systems and technology implemented to prevent fraud (e.g., identity and access management, authentication, issuance of one-time-passwords, biometrics)."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 37 / sub-domain 4.4 Authentication):** "Member Organisations should define, approve, implement and maintain a standard for the authentication of customer, employee and third party credentials ... This should be risk-based and utilise multi-factor authentication."

### [[Counter-Fraud Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping which controls a Member Organisation must implement under the Counter-Fraud Framework, treat the Prevent domain as one of four mandated structural pillars (Governance, Prevent, Detect, Respond), each carrying its own Principle and Control Requirements. Prevent covers proactive, risk-based controls — segregation of duties, authentication, due diligence, transaction limits, underwriting — that must be documented in fraud prevention standards, periodically reviewed, and calibrated to the Fraud Risk Assessment and Fraud Risk Appetite. You would conclude that preventive controls are not discretionary add-ons but a required domain whose adequacy is judged against the organisation's own risk assessment output.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / 4.1):** "An effective Counter-Fraud Programme includes fraud prevention processes and controls... proactive... stopping a fraudster acting before they can cause harm."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 9):** "The Framework is structured around four main domains, namely: Governance, Prevent, Detect, Respond"

### [[Due Diligence]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping prevention obligations, treat fraud Due Diligence (sub-domain 4.2) as a Prevent-domain control distinct from AML/CTF Customer Due Diligence — here it targets fraud risk across employees, customers and third parties, aiming to prevent relationships outside fraud risk appetite. The Prevent domain lists 'due diligence and integrity checks' among the minimum prevention controls, and 4.2 requires DD standards aligned to the Fraud Risk Assessment, covering employee background checks, customer identity validation and third-party vetting. A reviewer should not treat this as satisfied by AML CDD alone: the fraud DD standard must be separately defined, approved and periodically updated, and must address internal-fraud (employee) and third-party dimensions that AML customer-focused DD does not cover.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 40 / Section 4 Prevent):** "The controls implemented to prevent fraud (e.g., segregation of duties, ... due diligence and integrity checks, ...)."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 31 / sub-domain 4.2 Due Diligence):** "standards for assessing the fraud risk associated with employees, customers and third parties to prevent the establishment of relationships outside risk appetite."

### [[Fraud Risk Management Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping obligations under SAMA's Counter-Fraud Framework, treat the Fraud Risk Management Framework as a mandated sub-domain (4.1) sitting inside the Prevent domain, not as a standalone policy. Section 4 opens the Prevent domain and requires that a Fraud Risk Management Framework be 'defined, approved and implemented' and aligned with enterprise risk management, with Fraud Risk Assessment, Fraud Risk Appetite and KRIs as its minimum components. The consequence: the risk-assessment output is what drives where prevention activity is focused, so a compliance reviewer should verify the FRA feeds the prevention standards and appetite/limits rather than assessing the two in isolation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / Section 4):** "An effective Counter-Fraud Programme includes fraud prevention processes and controls to facilitate the identification of threats and mitigate the risk of fraud occurring."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 26 / Section 4.1):** "A Fraud Risk Management Framework should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organisation's enterprise risk management process."
