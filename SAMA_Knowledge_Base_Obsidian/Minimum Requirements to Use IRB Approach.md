# Minimum Requirements to Use IRB Approach

## Connections

### [[Corporate Governance and Oversight]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping what a bank must satisfy for IRB eligibility, do not treat 'Corporate Governance and Oversight' as separable from the minimum-requirements regime — it is Section 5 within the eleven-section chapter 16 that a bank must meet at outset and on an ongoing basis. This means board/senior-management oversight of the rating system is a supervisory precondition for using IRB, alongside rating system design, use test, risk quantification and validation. For an eligibility or breach assessment you should check governance and oversight as one of the mandatory sections, since failure there undermines IRB approval as a whole.

### [[IRB Disclosure Requirements]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining what a bank must do to rely on its own internal risk estimates, read the IRB disclosure requirements as an integral condition of the minimum-requirements chapter, not an optional add-on — chapter 10 makes IRB approval 'subject to certain minimum conditions and disclosure requirements', and disclosure is listed as Section 11 of chapter 16. This means adequate public disclosure is a supervisory precondition for continued IRB use. For an eligibility assessment you should confirm the disclosure section is met alongside the other mandatory sections, since it is one of the standing conditions of IRB authorization.

### [[Rating System Design]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank qualifies to use the IRB approach for capital purposes, treat rating system design not as an optional design choice but as a component of the mandatory minimum requirements — Section 3 (rating system design) sits inside Chapter 16 ('Minimum requirements to use IRB approach') per the framework's own table of contents. This means a bank cannot claim IRB eligibility while treating its two-dimensional rating structure, multiple-system rationale, and design documentation as merely internal governance; those are enforceable qualifying conditions SAMA can test. Conclude that any IRB approval assessment must verify Section 3 compliance at outset and on an ongoing basis, and that failures in rating system design directly undermine IRB eligibility rather than being a separable issue.

### [[Requirements for Recognition of Leasing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]

### [[Risk Quantification]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating a bank's own estimates of PD, LGD and EAD, read risk quantification (Section 7) as an integral part of the IRB minimum requirements, not a standalone modelling exercise — the framework's structure places Section 7 within Chapter 16 and paragraph 16.61 states it 'addresses the broad standards for own-estimates of PD, LGD, and EAD.' The practical consequence is that a bank on the advanced approach that fails the quantification standards cannot use its own LGD/EAD and is pushed back to supervisory estimates. Conclude that IRB eligibility for a given parameter is contingent on meeting Section 7's estimation standards, and check quantification compliance parameter-by-parameter before accepting own-estimate use.

### [[Supervisory LGD and EAD Estimates]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a bank fails to satisfy the own-estimate requirements for LGD or EAD, this link tells you the consequence is prescribed, not discretionary: the minimum requirements framework mandates fallback to supervisory estimates for corporate, sovereign and bank exposures. ### [[Use of Internal Ratings]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When checking whether a bank genuinely qualifies for IRB, note that the minimum requirements include a 'use test' — Section 6 (use of internal ratings) is listed inside Chapter 16 — meaning internal ratings and estimates must actually drive credit decisioning and risk management, not exist solely for capital calculation. The corpus reflects this same principle in the SEC-IAA context, requiring that an internal assessment 'be used in the bank's internal risk management processes.' Conclude that IRB eligibility can fail if ratings are calculated for regulatory reporting only; verify that the same rating outputs are embedded in real credit and management processes before accepting IRB use.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Ch.16):** "Section 6: use of internal ratings 196"

### [[Validation of Internal Estimates]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When judging whether a bank's IRB estimates can be relied upon, treat validation (Section 8) as a mandatory minimum requirement, not optional model assurance — it is listed within Chapter 16 and paragraph 16.121 requires banks to 'have a robust system in place to validate the accuracy and consistency of rating systems.' The concrete standards include regular back-testing of realized versus estimated PDs (and LGD/EAD for advanced IRB) using long data histories, updated at least annually. Conclude that IRB eligibility depends on demonstrable, documented validation, and that absence or weakness of validation undermines the whole IRB permission regardless of how well the models are otherwise designed.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Ch.16):** "Section 8: validation of internal estimat[es]"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 219 / 16.121-16.122):** "Banks must have a robust system in place to validate the accuracy and consistency of rating systems ... compare realized default rates with estimated PDs"
