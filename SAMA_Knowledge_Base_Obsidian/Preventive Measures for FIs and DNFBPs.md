# Preventive Measures for FIs and DNFBPs

## Connections

### [[Anti-Money Laundering Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining what an FI or DNFBP must actually do under the AML Law, treat the 'Preventive Measures' block (Section III, Articles 5 onward) as the operative source of the customer-facing obligations, not merely as background — the Law's structure carries the risk-assessment, CDD, ongoing-monitoring, and ten-year record-keeping duties into this concept node. The parent Law establishes the duties (risk identification under Art 5, monitoring/EDD under Art 13, records under Art 12) that the preventive-measures concept aggregates. You would conclude that compliance scoping should trace each obligation back to its Article in this Law, and that the Implementing Regulation fills in when and how measures apply.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3-4 / Art 5, Art 7):** "FIs, DNFBPs shall identify, assess, and document their money laundering risks and keep it up to date"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / Art 13-14):** "FIs and DNFBPs shall: Monitor and scrutinize transactions, document and data on an ongoing basis..."

### [[Director of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing who is accountable for the AML/CTF preventive-measures regime, treat the AML Law's institutional obligations and the senior-appointment regime as one accountability chain: the Law imposes CDD, PEP-screening, correspondent-banking and wire-transfer duties on FIs and DNFBPs, while the Senior Positions Requirements make the 'Director of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing' a position that requires SAMA's written non-objection (and must be filled by a Saudi candidate). The link tells you the person owning those preventive obligations sits within SAMA's fit-and-proper gate, so a firm cannot lawfully install its MLRO/AML head without prior SAMA non-objection. Because this connection is inferred across two instruments rather than a stated cross-reference, verify the applicable appendix for your entity type before relying on it, and confirm which preventive duties are assigned to that officer under the Implementing Regulation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Art 7-8):** "Apply due diligence measures to their customers ... Where a higher risk of money laundering was identified, they shall apply enhanced due diligence measures."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 11 / Appendix (1)):** "Director of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing"
- **Caveat:** Link is inferred; the two documents do not cite each other. Confirm the entity-specific appendix and the Implementing Regulation before relying on it.

### [[Due Diligence Measures]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping CDD obligations, treat 'Due Diligence Measures' as a component within the broader 'Preventive Measures' regime, not a separate track — the Law requires risk-based CDD, PEP screening (Art 8), correspondent-banking safeguards (Art 9) and wire-transfer originator/beneficiary information (Art 10), all within Section III. The due-diligence node draws the specific measures from the same articles the preventive-measures node covers, and crucially the Law defers the triggering instances and measure types to the Implementing Regulation. You would conclude that the extent of CDD is risk-calibrated (EDD where higher risk is identified) and that you must consult the Implementing Regulation to know exactly when each measure applies.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Art 7(1)):** "Apply due diligence measures to their customers and the Implementing Regulation shall set forth the instances"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Art 7-8):** "Determine the extent of due diligence measures based on the risks... they shall apply enhanced due diligence measures"

### [[General Directorate of Financial Intelligence]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping who receives the outputs of the preventive-measures regime, note that the General Directorate of Financial Intelligence (Articles 17-22) is the national central agency to which suspicious transactions detected through CDD and ongoing monitoring must be reported, and it holds analytical and information-exchange autonomy under State Security oversight. The link is grounded in the same Law: the preventive duties (monitoring, EDD) generate STRs, and separate provisions prohibit tipping-off and protect good-faith reporters (Art 16). You would conclude that detection obligations and the reporting/confidentiality regime are two ends of one chain — a firm cannot satisfy preventive measures without routing suspicions to the Directorate, and must observe the tipping-off prohibition when doing so.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Art 13(3)):** "increase the level and nature of monitoring... to determine whether the transaction is unusual or suspicious"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Art 17):** "national central agency to receive suspicious transaction reports... to analyze such reports... and to disseminate the results"
