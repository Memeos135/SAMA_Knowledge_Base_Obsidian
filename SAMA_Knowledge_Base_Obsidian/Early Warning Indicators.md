# Early Warning Indicators

## Connections

### [[Contingency Funding Plan (CFP)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank's Contingency Funding Plan meets SAMA's ILAAP expectations, do not treat early warning indicators as a separate optional checklist — the CFP guidance expressly folds them into the invocation and escalation architecture. The CFP must include 'early warning signals using benchmark indicators' with defined triggers (e.g. 30% or 50% decline in collections for three continuous months), and Section B independently lists the indicators (concentrations, currency mismatches, limit breaches, rating downgrade, rising funding costs, deposit outflows). A reviewer should therefore verify that the same benchmark indicators feed the CFP's escalation triggers, and treat an absence of quantified triggers as a compliance gap in the CFP itself, not merely in monitoring.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 25 / Section B):** "An important component of liquidity risk management and the contingency funding plan is the early warning indicators including: Growing concentrations in assets or liabilities."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 26 / Section C (CFP)):** "Early warning signals using benchmark indicators i.e. Availability of credit lines, collection efficiency... These signals should have triggers based on 30% or 50% decline in collections for continuous three months."
