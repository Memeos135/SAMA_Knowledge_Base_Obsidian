# Alternative Liquidity Approaches (ALA)

## Connections

### [[ALA Option 1 Committed Liquidity Facilities|ALA Option 1: Committed Liquidity Facilities]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank may rely on committed liquidity facilities to plug an HQLA shortfall, treat ALA Option 1 as one of three specific alternative treatments authorised only within the general ALA regime, not as a free-standing entitlement. The ALA guidance sets overarching preconditions — usage capped at actual HQLA shortfall, ongoing reporting to the supervisor, and (for Option 1) typically central bank approval of the committed facility — that constrain any use of this option. You would therefore verify that both the ALA general rules and Option 1's specific conditions are satisfied before treating committed facility capacity as LCR-eligible.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 69, Annex 3):** "use of Option 1 will typically require central bank approval of the committed facility."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 61, Principle 2):** "A jurisdiction which intends to adopt one or more of the options for alternative treatment must be capable of limiting the uncertainty of performance"

### [[ALA Option 2 Foreign Currency HQLA|ALA Option 2: Foreign Currency HQLA]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a bank faces a shortfall of domestic-currency HQLA, ALA Option 2 permits holding foreign-currency HQLA instead, but only as a subordinate option within the ALA framework and subject to that framework's usage cap and reporting duties. Option 2 adds its own conditions: currency mismatch positions must be justifiable, controlled within supervisor-agreed limits, and confined to freely and reliably convertible currencies, with foreign-exchange haircuts applied. You would conclude that eligibility turns on both the general ALA constraints and Option 2's currency-specific limits, and would check the supervisor's agreed mismatch limits before relying on foreign-currency HQLA.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 69, Annex 3):** "A bank should not use an alternative treatment to meet its LCR more than its actual need as reflected by the shortfall of eligible HQLA"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 23, para 59):** "Option 2 would allow supervisors to permit banks that evidence a shortfall of HQLA in the domestic currency ... to hold HQLA in a currency that does not match"

### [[ALA Option 3 Additional Level 2 Assets|ALA Option 3: Additional Level 2 Assets]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When considering whether a bank can hold Level 2A assets beyond the 40% cap, treat ALA Option 3 as the third alternative treatment governed by the same ALA general rules (usage limited to actual shortfall, supervisory reporting), plus its own quality and haircut conditions. Option 3 requires the additional Level 2A assets to be of quality comparable to Level 1, subject to a minimum 20% haircut (5% above the standard 15%), while Level 2B assets stay within the 15% cap. You would conclude that exceeding the 40% cap is permissible only where both the overarching ALA limits and Option 3's higher qualifying standards are met.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 24, para 63):** "The usage of any of the above options would be constrained by a limit specified by supervisors"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 64, Criterion (c)):** "the jurisdiction must only allow Level 2 assets that are of a quality (credit and liquidity) comparable to that for Level 1 assets"

### [[Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining whether a bank may rely on alternative liquidity approaches, treat ALA as a conditional supplement that only becomes relevant because the LCR standard itself requires HQLA the jurisdiction cannot supply. Within SAMA_EN_3623 the ALA framework and the LCR are part of one standard: ALA usage is defined and constrained by reference to the LCR (reporting to supervisor, no usage above the shortfall, possible specific approval). Conclude that ALA eligibility flows from the LCR's HQLA requirements and supervisory framework, so any reliance on ALA should be verified against the LCR reporting duties and the jurisdiction's documented supervisory oversight and periodic self-assessment.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 69 / Annex 3):** "general and specific rules governing banks' usage of the options are for the guidance of supervisors in developing relevant standards for their banks."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / Part 1):** "The LCR should be a key component of the supervisory approach to liquidity risk, but must be supplemented by detailed supervisory assessments."
