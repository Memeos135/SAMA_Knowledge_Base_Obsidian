# Payment Systems Classification

## Connections

### [[Financial Market Infrastructure Principles]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a payment system operator's obligations, note that the FMI Principles are incorporated by reference as a compliance standard whose applicability depends on the system's classification. Article 106 requires an unclassified payment system operator to conduct periodic self-assessment against the FMI Principles per its licence, and the Regulation separately structures 'classification of important payment systems' (Part Nine, Chapter One) and 'FMI principles requirements' (Chapter Two). You would conclude that classification (important vs. unclassified) drives which FMI Principle obligations and capital/resource requirements apply, so verify the operator's classification before determining the applicable standard.

### [[Settlement Finality and Insolvency]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a payment system operator's obligations under the Payment and Payment Services Executive Regulation, treat system classification and settlement finality as a single continuum: the regulation's Chapter Nine first classifies payment systems (important vs. unclassified) and then, in its fourth section, sets the finality-of-settlement and insolvency rules. The classification drives which prudential and operational duties (including PFMI compliance and capital adequacy) attach, and finality determines when a settlement becomes legally irrevocable and how it survives a participant's insolvency. Conclude that you cannot assess insolvency protection for settled payments without first fixing the system's classification, because both live in the same instrument and share the same defined terms.
