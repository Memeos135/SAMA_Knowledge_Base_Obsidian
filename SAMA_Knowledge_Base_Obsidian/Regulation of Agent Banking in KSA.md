# Regulation of Agent Banking in KSA

## Connections

### [[Bank (Agent Banking Principal)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When allocating liability in an agent-banking model, the Bank is the principal that bears sole responsibility for selecting Agents and is answerable for all their actions or omissions, with board-level accountability for compliance with the Regulation. The Regulation defines 'Bank' by reference to the Banking Control Law and makes the Bank–Agent relationship direct and contract-governed. You should conclude that the Bank cannot contract away its regulatory responsibility, and check that the Bank's board oversight, Agent-selection controls, and the CEO fit-and-proper declaration are documented before treating the arrangement as compliant.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Article 3 Responsibility):** "It is the responsibility of the board of directors of each Bank to ensure compliance with this Regulation. Banks shall be solely responsible for the selection of Agents."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / Article 3):** "The Bank is responsible for all actions or omissions"

### [[Banking Agent]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping who bears AML/CFT and CDD duties in an agent-banking arrangement, treat the Agent as a defined, SAMA-approved legal entity that carries out KYC and customer due diligence at the point of service, but note the Bank retains overall responsibility for the agency relationship. The Regulation defines the Agent and requires the Agent to hold a commercial register permitting the business, obtain approval from its own supervisor, and comply with AML/CFT laws. You should conclude that outsourcing service delivery to an Agent does not transfer regulatory liability away from the Bank, and verify that the Agent's KYC/AML obligations are captured in the contract and processes.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Article 1 Definitions):** "Agent(s): a legal entity contracted by a licensed commercial Bank and approved by SAMA to engage in Agent Banking activity."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 7 / para 3):** "The Agent shall apply for and receive necessary approval from the relevant supervisory and regulatory entity before the Bank applies for SAMA's non-objection"

### [[Banking Control Law]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When establishing the legal authority and hierarchy behind agent-banking obligations, note the Regulation is issued under SAMA's powers granted by the Banking Control Law (Royal Decree No. M/5) and the SAMA Charter, making it subordinate implementing instruction to that primary law. Banking Agents must comply with the Regulation in addition to the Banking Control Law and its instructions, alongside the AML Law, the Law on Terrorism Crimes and Financing, and SAMA Outsourcing Rules. You should treat the Banking Control Law as the enabling statute — meaning agent-banking requirements sit within, not apart from, the broader banking-supervision regime — and check those parallel laws when assessing the full compliance perimeter.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Article 1):** "The Regulation is issued pursuant to powers granted to SAMA under the following laws and regulations... b. Banking Control Law, issued by Royal Decree No. M/5 dated 22/02/1386H."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4):** "Banking Agents shall comply with this Regulation in addition to relative laws... Banking Control Law and its instructions;"

### [[Consumer Protection Requirements]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a bank's agent-banking programme, treat consumer protection as a non-delegable obligation of the Bank even though the customer-facing conduct occurs at the Agent. Article 24 requires the Bank to put in place a framework implementing the Banking Consumer Protection Principles (BCPP) and SAMA instructions to protect beneficiaries from Agent Banking risks (fraud, loss of privacy, loss of service), and the application stage already demands a description of consumer protection measures. You should conclude that the BCPP regime is imported wholesale into agent banking and that the Bank — not the Agent — is answerable for gaps in complaint handling and disclosure.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 7 / Art 4):** "Description of consumer protection measures, including financial awareness and education strategies related to Agent Banking"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 18 / Art 24):** "Banks must put in place an appropriate consumer protection framework that ensures implementation of all requirements set out in the Banking Consumer Protection Principles (BCPP)"
