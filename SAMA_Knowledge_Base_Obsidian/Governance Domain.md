# Governance Domain

## Connections

### [[Counter-Fraud Department]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping who is accountable for what under SAMA's Counter-Fraud Framework, treat the Counter-Fraud Department as a mandated component of the Governance domain, not an optional operational unit. The Framework's Governance domain (Section 3) requires a Governance Structure and expressly locates the Counter-Fraud Department under sub-domain 3.5, giving it day-to-day operational responsibility for the Counter-Fraud Programme (monitoring policy compliance, fraud risk assessment, SAMA intelligence-sharing, reporting to senior management). A compliance reviewer should therefore verify that the Department exists, is adequately resourced, and reports through an approved line — its absence is a Governance-domain gap, not merely a process weakness.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 14 / Section 3 Governance):** "a Governance Structure with associated internal and external reporting aligned to the organisation's size and complexity to monitor and oversee fraud risk management."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 20 / sub-domain 3.5):** "Member Organisations should establish and maintain a Counter-Fraud Department that has responsibility for the day-to-day operation of the Counter-Fraud Programme."

### [[Counter-Fraud Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assigning accountability for a counter-fraud programme, note the Governance domain is the framework component that fixes where responsibility sits: the Board and Executive Leadership are ultimately responsible, and organisations must establish a Counter-Fraud Governance Committee headed by an Executive Committee member with defined membership. Because Governance is a mandated domain with its own Principle and Control Requirements, these are enforceable structural obligations. Conclude that board/senior-management ownership and the CFGC composition requirements must be evidenced, and check the Governance domain rather than treating fraud oversight as delegable operational detail.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 14 / 3.1 Governance Structure):** "Member Organisations should establish and maintain a dedicated Counter-Fraud Governance Committee (CFGC)... headed by a member of the Executive Committee."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 9 / 2.1 Structure):** "The Framework is structured around four main domains, namely: Governance, Prevent, Detect, Respond."

### [[Counter-Fraud Governance Committee]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping accountability for the Counter-Fraud Programme, the Counter-Fraud Governance Committee (CFGC) is the specific body the Governance domain requires each Member Organisation to establish, so governance obligations cannot be satisfied without it. The Framework mandates a CFGC headed by an Executive Committee member and populated at minimum by the Head of Counter-Fraud, CRO, COO and Head of Digital, and it is the body that approves the CFGC charter and endorses the Strategy, Policy, Fraud Risk Assessment output and Fraud Risk Appetite. You would conclude that the CFGC's composition and its endorsement of these documents are checkable evidence of governance compliance, and their absence is a governance-domain deficiency.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 14 / 3):** "a Governance Structure with associated internal and external reporting... to monitor and oversee fraud risk management."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 14 / 3.1):** "Member Organisations should establish and maintain a dedicated Counter-Fraud Governance Committee (CFGC)... headed by a member of the Executive Committee."

### [[Counter-Fraud Policy]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing governance documentation, treat the Counter-Fraud Policy as one of the mandatory deliverables of the Governance domain rather than a standalone internal document. Section 3 requires the Counter-Fraud Programme to include a Policy 'outlining responsibilities and mandatory requirements', and the Governance controls make the Policy subject to CFGC approval and Board endorsement, with the Head of Counter-Fraud accountable for developing and maintaining it. A reviewer should confirm the Policy exists, has been approved and endorsed through the required governance chain, and defines responsibilities — an unapproved or absent policy is a Governance-domain deficiency.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 14 / Section 3 Governance):** "a Counter-Fraud Policy outlining responsibilities and mandatory requirements."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 18):** "The Head of Counter-Fraud should be accountable for: 1. Developing, implementing, and maintaining: ... b. Counter-Fraud Policy."

### [[Counter-Fraud Strategy]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping governance obligations, treat the Counter-Fraud Strategy as a required Governance-domain output that must be developed, approved and endorsed through the defined chain, not just a management planning artefact. Section 3 states the Programme 'should include a Counter-Fraud Strategy to define organisational objectives', the CFGC endorses it after approval, and the Head of Counter-Fraud is accountable for developing and maintaining it. A reviewer should verify the Strategy exists, sets organisational objectives, and has passed CFGC approval/Board endorsement — its absence signals a Governance-domain non-conformance.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 14 / Section 3 Governance):** "a Counter-Fraud Strategy to define organisational objectives."
