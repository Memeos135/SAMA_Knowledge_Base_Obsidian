# Equity Risk

## Connections

### [[General Market Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When computing the equity charge under the standardised approaches, understand that equity risk is one of the defined risk classes that feeds the overall general market risk measurement, and that under the simplified standardised approach equity, interest rate, FX and commodities charges are summed arithmetically. The framework lists equity risk within the enumerated risk classes and directs that these component risk measures be aggregated for the capital requirement. Conclude that the equity charge is not standalone but part of the summed general market risk total, and check [14.1]–[14.73] for the applicable equity computation.

### [[Simplified Standardised Approach]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the simplified standardised approach for an eligible bank, equity risk is one of the four defined categories the approach measures, and its charge is computed separately and summed with the others rather than netted across risk classes. Paragraph 14.1(1) lists equity risk within [14.3]–[14.73], and 14.75 confirms equities capital numbers are added to the relevant category totals with general and specific market risk incorporated. Conclude that equity positions of an eligible bank must be capitalised under the simplified rules and arithmetically aggregated per 14.1(3); do not assume any diversification offset between equity and other risk classes under this approach.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 134 / 14.1(3)):** "The capital requirement under the simplified standardised approach will be the measures of risk obtained from [14.2] to [14.86], summed arithmetically."

### [[Specific Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When capitalising an equity position, note that the framework treats equity risk as one of the defined risk classes and, consistent with the standardised structure, separates specific (issuer-level) risk from general market risk. The 'Risk class' definition enumerates equity risk alongside credit spread (specific) risk, and the standardised approach requires distinct component measures to be summed. Conclude that an equity holding may attract both a specific-risk and a general-risk element, and verify the equity chapter for how each is derived rather than applying a single blended charge.
