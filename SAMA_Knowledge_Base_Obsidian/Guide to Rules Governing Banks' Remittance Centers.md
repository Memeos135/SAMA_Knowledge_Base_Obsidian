# Guide to Rules Governing Banks' Remittance Centers

## Connections

### [[AMLCFT Rules|AML/CFT Rules]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When operating a bank remittance center or licensed remittance company, do not read this Guide as a self-contained rulebook — it incorporates SAMA's AML/CFT circulars and KYC requirements by reference and subordinates itself to them. The Guide's general provisions require compliance with all AML/CFT and CTF circulars and the Account-Opening Rules, and the customer-acceptance section mandates applying KYC and SAMA AML/CFT rules, rejecting fictitious/anonymous names, terminating relationships where sources cannot be verified, and applying enhanced due diligence for high-risk customers per FATF. You should conclude that the binding AML/CFT obligations for remittance centers reside in SAMA's standing circulars and the KYC regime, and this Guide directs you to them rather than replacing them.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 7 / Section 5):** "All instructions issued in all SAMA circulars regarding outbound and inbound remittances ... for example but not limited to: Anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing circulars"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 11 / Section 9):** "The requirements of KYC, AML/CFT rules issued by SAMA and other relevant regulations and instructions shall be applied"

### [[Banking Control Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping the legal authority behind remittance-center rules, treat the Guide as a subordinate instrument sitting under the banking framework rather than as a standalone source. The Guide governs banks' remittance centers and licensed remittance companies, and banks derive their legal existence and permitted activities from the Banking Control Law's licensing regime; a remittance center 'belongs' to a Bank, so the Guide's obligations presuppose that BCL-authorized entity. For a compliance decision you would confirm the operating bank holds a valid BCL license and that remittance activity falls within its authorized scope before relying on the Guide alone as your regulatory basis.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / Definitions):** "Centers: Banks' remittance centers and licensed remittance companies. Bank: The bank to which the remittance center belongs."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Overview):** "Article 3 of the Banking control law allows Joint Stock Companies to carry on banking business in Kingdom of Saudi Arabia."
- **Caveat:** Link between the two documents is thematic (banking-sector authority); the Guide does not itself cite the BCL in the provided text.

### [[Customer Due Diligence Measures]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the AML/CTF obligations of a bank remittance center, treat CDD as a mandatory operational requirement of this Guide, not a general principle: the Guide expressly directs centers to apply essential due diligence to all permanent and temporary customers including real beneficiaries, on a continuous, risk-based basis. Note the defined-term interaction — the Guide's 'customer' is the person performing a financial transaction (membership holder or temporary customer), so CDD scope tracks both categories. A compliance reader should conclude that identity verification, beneficial-owner identification, and enhanced measures for high-risk relationships (PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions) are enforceable minimums a center must evidence for each transaction type.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8 / Section 9):** "taking into account the full compliance with regulations and instructions issued by SAMA, AML/CFT-related instructions, 'Know Your Customer (KYC)' requirements, and Customer Due Diligence (CDD) procedures"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 12 / Section 9.6):** "remittance centers apply the essential due diligence procedures to all permanent and temporary customers, including real beneficiaries, and that these procedures be continuous"

### [[Financial Action Task Force (FATF)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing risk classification for remittance customers, use FATF as the operative external benchmark this Guide incorporates: the Guide defines FATF and then requires enhanced due diligence for transactions involving jurisdictions the FATF website identifies as not adequately implementing AML/CFT recommendations. This makes FATF listings a live input into the center's risk-based approach rather than merely informational. A reader should conclude that high-risk-jurisdiction and PEP triggers derive from FATF standards and must feed the center's enhanced-measures and rejection decisions (e.g., funds from FATF-warned countries).
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / Section 3 Definitions):** "Financial Action Task Force (FATF): An inter-governmental body that sets standards and promotes effective implementation of legal, regulatory and operational measures to combat money laundering"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 13 / Section 9.6):** "those mentioned on the FATF website as being jurisdictions that do not adequately implement the recommendations related to AML/CFT"

### [[Know Your Customer Principle]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When establishing a remittance membership, treat KYC as a gating condition tied to membership opening, not an optional check: the Guide requires official-ID capture, rejection of fictitious/anonymous names, and manager approval only after verifying customer information against the volume and nature of activity, and mandates termination where sources or identity cannot be verified. KYC here sits alongside CDD and AML/CFT rules issued by SAMA as an integrated acceptance standard. A reader should conclude that a membership cannot lawfully be opened or maintained without documented KYC, and that failure to verify identity or source is an express ground to end the relationship.

### [[SARIE System]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing domestic transfer obligations, note that SARIE is the designated channel for in-Kingdom transfers and carries a specific originator-information requirement: domestic transfers performed exclusively through SARIE must ensure the transferor's name and account number are present. This ties the payments-rail choice directly to the AML/CFT traceability requirements for wire transfers. A reader should conclude that domestic remittances routed through SARIE must satisfy the originator-data completeness standard, and that incomplete-originator handling rules apply where that information is absent.

### [[Saudi Arabia Financial Investigation Unit (SAFIU)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a remittance center forms reasonable grounds to suspect ML/TF, SAFIU is the mandated recipient of the report, so identify it as the reporting endpoint for suspicious-activity obligations under this Guide. The Guide directs centers to report to SAFIU both where funds/transactions appear to be criminal proceeds or ML/TF-related and where a correspondent fails to respond on an incomplete-originator wire, and to retain related decisions and records for ten years under SAMA's AML/CFT rules. A reader should conclude that reporting cannot be discharged internally — SAFIU filing, plus documented rationale and ten-year retention, is the required response.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / Section 3 Definitions):** "SAFIU: The authority authorized to receive and analyze reports of activities suspected of being related to money laundering and terrorist financing"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 18 / item 26):** "When there are reasonable grounds to suspect that customers' funds, operations and transactions represent proceeds of criminal activity ... they must be reported to SAFIU."
