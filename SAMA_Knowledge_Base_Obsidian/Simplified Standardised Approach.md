# Simplified Standardised Approach

## Connections

### [[Commodities Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining capital charges for a bank eligible to use the simplified standardised approach, treat commodities risk as one of the four risk categories that the approach expressly covers and measures with prescribed, fixed percentages rather than sensitivity-based methods. Paragraph 14.1(1) scopes [14.3]–[14.73] to interest rate, equity, FX and commodities risk, and 14.72–14.73 set the concrete charges (15% of net position plus 3% of gross positions) for commodities. So for an eligible bank (non–G-SIB/D-SIB, no IMA, no correlation trading), you would calculate commodities capital using these flat charges and sum them arithmetically per 14.1(3) — but confirm SAMA has not mandated the full standardised approach for sizeable commodity risk under 3.9(2).
- **Grounding — this node (Page 134 / 14.1(1)):** "[14.3] to [14.73] deal with interest rate, equity, foreign exchange (FX) and commodities risk."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 158 / 14.72):** "The capital requirement will equal 15% of the net position, long or short, in each commodity."

### [[Equity Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the simplified standardised approach for an eligible bank, equity risk is one of the four defined categories the approach measures, and its charge is computed separately and summed with the others rather than netted across risk classes. Paragraph 14.1(1) lists equity risk within [14.3]–[14.73], and 14.75 confirms equities capital numbers are added to the relevant category totals with general and specific market risk incorporated. Conclude that equity positions of an eligible bank must be capitalised under the simplified rules and arithmetically aggregated per 14.1(3); do not assume any diversification offset between equity and other risk classes under this approach.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 134 / 14.1(1)):** "[14.3] to [14.73] deal with interest rate, equity, foreign exchange (FX) and commodities risk."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 134 / 14.1(3)):** "The capital requirement under the simplified standardised approach will be the measures of risk obtained from [14.2] to [14.86], summed arithmetically."

### [[Foreign Exchange (FX) Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** This link shows FX risk as a component of the simplified standardised approach across two related SAMA market-risk documents; before relying on it, confirm that the simplified approach is available to the bank at all, because eligibility is gated. SAMA (3553, para 3.9) permits the simplified alternative only for smaller/simpler trading books subject to SAMA approval, excluding G-SIBs/D-SIBs, IMA users and correlation-trading positions, and SAMA may still mandate the full standardised approach. For a bank considering measuring FX risk this way, first verify SAMA approval and the indicative eligibility criteria; the FX-risk module (as detailed in 3487 [14.53]–[14.62]) only applies if the simplified approach is authorised.
- **Grounding — this node (SAMA_EN_3553_VER1 Page 11 / para 3.9):** "SAMA may allow banks that maintain smaller or simpler trading books to use the simplified alternative to the standardised approach as set out in [14]. The use of the simplified alternative is subject to SAMA approval"
- **Grounding — related node (SAMA_EN_3487_VER1 Page 162 / para 14.75):** "added to the capital requirements for the relevant category, ie interest rate related instruments, equities, FX and commodities as described in [14.3] to [14.73]"
- **Caveat:** The two documents appear to be parallel/versioned market-risk frameworks; confirm which version applies to the bank and that eligibility conditions in 3553 para 3.9 are met before relying on the simplified FX treatment.

### [[Interest Rate Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When applying the simplified standardised approach for an eligible bank, interest rate risk is the first of the four defined risk categories covered, and the framework also treats interest rate risk as a component within commodities and options measurement rather than only a standalone book. Paragraph 14.1(1) scopes interest rate risk into [14.3]–[14.73], while 14.73 adds a 3% gross-position charge to protect against basis, interest rate and forward gap risk in commodities. Accordingly, capitalise a bank's interest rate positions under the simplified rules and check that interest rate elements embedded in forward/derivative positions (e.g. commodities carry) are captured through the additional charges, not omitted.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 161 / 14.73):** "to protect the bank against basis risk, interest rate risk and forward gap risk under the simplified approach... an additional capital requirement equivalent to 3% of the bank's gross positions."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 134 / 14.1(1)):** "[14.3] to [14.73] deal with interest rate, equity, foreign exchange (FX) and commodities risk."
