# Regulations for Foreign Banks Branches (FBB)

## Connections

### [[Banking Control Law]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When establishing the legal authority and hierarchy behind FBB obligations, read the Banking Control Law as the parent statute from which these regulations derive their binding force: the FBB regulations are issued under SAMA's Charter and BCL Article 16(3), which designates SAMA the legislative body for regulatory control over banks. The link tells you the FBB rules are subordinate implementing instruments, and that FBBs must comply with the BCL and its prudential requirements independently of the FBB-specific rules. Conclude that any FBB compliance analysis must layer BCL obligations, general bank obligations, and the FBB-specific additions together — the FBB regulation does not displace the BCL, it supplements it.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / para 3):** "SAMA requires all FBBs to comply with the Banking Control Law (BCL), its prudential requirements, and other relevant laws and regulations as applicable to all local and foreign banks."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / para 2):** "the Banking Control Law – issued via Royal Decree No. M/5... Article 16 (3), which sets SAMA as the legislative body responsible for exercising regulatory and supervisory control over banks"

### [[Corporate Governance and Risk Management (FBB)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping an FBB's governance and risk obligations, read the corporate-governance/risk-management provisions as an integral part of the FBB regulation, not an optional appendix: the same instrument imposes documented liquidity risk tolerance, an independent Head of Risk Management pre-approved under Fit and Proper rules, and (for significant-retail or systemic wholesale FBBs) a Risk Oversight Team. The link tells you these governance duties are conditioned on the branch's activity profile, so applicability depends on whether the FBB meets the significant-retail or systemic thresholds defined elsewhere in the regulation. Conclude that you must first classify the FBB's activity level, then apply the corresponding governance and risk-oversight requirements as mandatory for that class.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / para 6):** "SAMA considers retail banking activities to be significant where an FBB: Has more than SAR 5 billion of retail and... account balances or more than 1,000 retail and MSME account holders"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 14 / para 44):** "a FBB that is involved in significant retail business or is a systemically important wholesale FBB should establish a mechanism for providing risk oversight to the branch's business activities"

### [[FBB Return Form (Attachment A)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining what an FBB must actually file with SAMA, treat Attachment A (FBB Return A) as the operative instrument that the regulation's reporting duty points to: the body text mandates quarterly submission of information 'in accordance with' this Return, within 30 days of quarter-end, and additionally requires all outsourcing activities to be reported through the same form. The link tells you the substantive scope of the reporting obligation is defined by the attachment, not the narrative alone. Conclude that compliance turns on completing and timely-submitting the Return itself, and that outsourcing reporting is folded into this same channel rather than a separate one.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 16 / para 55):** "All FBBs must provide SAMA with information in accordance with the FBB Return A (Attachment A) accompanying these regulations... within 30 days of the date to which the information relates."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / para 48):** "All outsourcing activities must also be reported using the FBB Return Form (Attachment A)."

### [[Funding Ratio (FR) Requirements]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing prudential exposure for a foreign bank branch, treat the Funding Ratio as a discretionary, case-by-case quasi-capital requirement rather than a universal one. The FBB Regulations (Section 3) establish that FBBs are not required to maintain capital in KSA, but SAMA may impose an FR on branches conducting high-risk business or significant retail operations, calculated on total RWA (Pillar 1 plus Pillar 2). You should conclude that FR applicability depends on the branch's business profile and any specific SAMA determination, and verify whether your branch has been made subject to FR before treating it as binding.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Section 1):** "SAMA requires all FBBs to comply with the Banking Control Law (BCL), its prudential requirements, and other relevant laws and regulations"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 16-17 / Section 3.1):** "a quasi-capital in the form of a Funding Ratio requirement may be set on a case-by-case basis, for example, those intending to conduct high-risk businesses"

### [[Liquidity Requirements]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping ongoing obligations for an FBB, the Liquidity Requirements are a distinct, mandatory pillar within the parent FBB Regulations, separate from the discretionary Funding Ratio. Section 4 imposes firm 'must' obligations: maintaining a documented liquidity risk management framework, a GM/CEO-approved risk tolerance reviewed at least annually, and a cushion of unencumbered liquid assets. You should conclude these liquidity governance duties apply as core prudential requirements to the branch and not treat them as optional or conditional on business scale in the way FR is.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Section 1):** "SAMA requires all FBBs to comply with the Banking Control Law (BCL), its prudential requirements, and other relevant laws and regulations"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 19 / Section 4.3):** "An FBB must maintain a liquidity risk management framework commensurate with the level and extent of liquidity risk to which the FBB is exposed"
