# Unsecured Wholesale Funding Run-off

## Connections

### [[Cash Outflows]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Unsecured wholesale funding run-off is a defined outflow category contributing to total expected cash outflows, with run-off factors that turn on the counterparty type and operational relationship (e.g. 40%, reduced to 20% if fully insured, and 100% for other legal-entity customers). Note the definition captures liabilities from non-natural persons callable within 30 days, expressly excluding derivative obligations. You should classify each funding source by counterparty and operational status to apply the correct factor, and check that debt securities are only re-categorised as retail where the stated distribution limitations are met.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 29 / para 85):** "'unsecured wholesale funding' is defined as those liabilities and general obligations that are raised from non-natural persons... Obligations related to derivative contracts are explicitly excluded"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 26 / para 69):** "Total expected cash outflows are calculated by multiplying the outstanding balances of various categories or types of liabilities and off-balance sheet commitments by the rates at which they are expected to run off"

### [[Operational Deposits]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When classifying corporate or institutional deposits for LCR run-off purposes, do not treat 'operational deposits' and 'unsecured wholesale funding run-off' as separate silos: operational deposits are a preferential carve-out within the unsecured wholesale funding regime, attracting the 25% factor only for the portion proven to serve clearing, custody or cash-management needs. Any excess balance falls back into the ordinary non-operational wholesale categories (e.g. 40% or 100% run-off), and if the bank cannot isolate the operational portion the entire deposit is treated as non-operational. You would therefore verify the operational-need methodology before applying the favourable factor, defaulting to the higher wholesale run-off rate where it is not demonstrable.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 33, para 109):** "deposits and other funding from other institutions ... not specifically held for operational purposes ... The run-off factor for these funds is 100%."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 31, para 96):** "only that part of the deposit balance ... proven to serve a customer's operational needs can qualify as stable. Excess balances should be treated in the appropriate category for non-operational deposits"
