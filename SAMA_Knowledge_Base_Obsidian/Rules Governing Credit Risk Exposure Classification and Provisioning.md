# Rules Governing Credit Risk Exposure Classification and Provisioning

## Connections

### [[Default Definition]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining whether a finance company must move an exposure to a higher-risk stage or recognise a loss, treat the 'default' definition as a binding regulatory trigger, not an accounting nicety. These Rules require finance companies to adopt SAMA's regulatory definition of default (both qualitative 'unlikeliness to pay' events and the 90-day quantitative criterion) and apply it consistently for regulatory and financial-reporting purposes, or document good reasons not to. You should therefore check that classification, provisioning, restaging and write-off decisions all key off this single default definition, and not rely on a firm-specific or purely IFRS 9 accounting default without documented justification.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4):** "These Rules set out the minimum requirements on Credit Risk Exposure Classification and Provisioning."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 21):** "Finance companies are required to adopt SAMA's regulatory definition of default and apply it consistently for both, regulatory and financial reporting purposes, or document good reasons why not."

### [[Deposit Taking Finance Companies (DTFCs) Regulations 2020]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping which SAMA rules apply to a Deposit Taking Finance Company, do not treat the DTFC Regulations as self-contained: both instruments derive from the same parent (Finance Companies Control Law, Royal Decree M/51, and its Implementing Regulation), and the DTFC Regulations expressly require DTFCs to also comply with SAMA regulations applicable to all Finance Companies. You would conclude the credit-risk classification and provisioning Rules apply to a DTFC in addition to its DTFC-specific prudential requirements, so both must be checked together for a full obligation set.

### [[Eligible Collateral and Valuation]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When calculating provisions under these Rules, note that the eligible collateral list and valuation requirements in Annexure 1 feed directly into the Loss Given Default (LGD) measure and thus into provisioning outcomes — collateral is expressly excluded from Exposure at Default but included, after haircuts, in LGD. The Rules are issued under the Finance Companies Control Law and its Implementing Regulation (Art. 62 requiring IFRS-based provisioning), so only collateral meeting the Annexure 1 eligibility and valuation-frequency conditions (e.g. annual re-valuation for Stage 3 immovable collateral, appraisers meeting commercial pledge law) may reduce recognised losses. Conclude that collateral not satisfying these criteria cannot be relied on to lower provisions.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6):** "Eligible collateral as elaborated in Annexure 1 should be included in the Loss Given Default calculation."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 24-25):** "The following list includes examples of eligible collateral ... used for calculation of provisions ... immovable collateral relating to Stage 3 or Default Exposures should be re-valued once every year."

### [[Expected Credit Loss Provisioning]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a finance company's provisioning adequacy, treat expected credit loss (ECL) provisioning as the core operative obligation of these Rules, governed both by IFRS 9 methodology and by SAMA-specific governance duties. The Rules require sound, documented, board-approved ECL methodologies reviewed at least annually (more often as new information arises), with quarterly reporting to the board and independent annual internal-audit evaluation. You should conclude that ECL is not merely an accounting output but a supervised process with defined review, reporting and audit cadences, and check those governance elements when evaluating compliance.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4):** "the finance company must set provisions for contingent losses and risks in accordance with international accounting standards."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 23):** "Adopting, documenting and approving sound expected credit loss methodologies... reviewed annually, or more frequently when the need arises."

### [[Finance Companies Control Law]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When establishing the legal authority behind these provisioning Rules, trace them to the Finance Companies Control Law and its Implementing Regulation — this is the enabling instrument that makes the Rules enforceable against finance companies. SAMA issued the Rules under the Law (Royal Decree M/S1) and expressly relies on Article 13 (provision for contingent operation losses) and Article 62 of the Implementing Regulation (provisions per international accounting standards, plus SAMA's power to require additional provisions). You should conclude the Rules bind entities licensed as finance companies under that Law, and that SAMA retains statutory discretion under Article 62 to demand provisions beyond the IFRS 9 baseline.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4):** "SAMA issued these rules in exercise of the powers vested upon it under Finance Companies Control Law promulgated by the Royal Decree No. (M/S1)."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4):** "Article 62... 'The finance company must set provisions for contingent losses and risks in accordance with international accounting standards. SAMA may require... additional provisions.'"

### [[Forbearance and Restructuring]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a finance company modifies a troubled borrower's terms, use the Rules' forbearance concept to determine that the exposure cannot simply stay or return to performing status. The Rules define forbearance as a concession granted because of borrower financial difficulty, require such exposures to be reported only in Stage 2B, 3A or 3B, and set strict cure/probation periods and exit criteria before re-categorisation as performing. You should conclude that restructuring triggers mandatory downgrading and extended cure periods (including that partial write-offs or collateral repossession do not restore performing status), and check these criteria before treating any restructured exposure as recovered.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 12):** "minimum eligibility requirement for re-staging stage 2 exposures into stage 1 exposures, which should at least include the following conditions."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 17):** "For regulatory classification purposes, these [forborne] exposures should only be reported in Stage 2B, 3A or 3B."

### [[IFRS 9]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When applying these Rules, read the core measurement terms (PD, EAD, LGD, ECL, staging, past due) as anchored in IFRS 9, but note SAMA overlays its own regulatory categories on top. The Rules explicitly define each metric 'as prescribed by IFRS 9' and map IFRS 9 stages to regulatory categories (Regular, Special Monitoring, Substandard, Doubtful, Loss), with sub-stages (2A/2B, 3A/3B) used for regulatory reporting only, not accounting. You should conclude that IFRS 9 supplies the accounting foundation while SAMA's staging/category framework governs regulatory classification, and that the earlier Provisions Guidelines definitions (Circular 381000046342) are superseded and must no longer be used.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11):** "SAMA has provided mapping of IFRS 9 stages to regulatory categories... The definitions given in SAMA previous circular... should no longer be used."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6):** "As prescribed by IFRS 9, the estimated credit losses expected to be incurred from the occurrence of a credit event, e.g. default."

### [[Stage 123 Credit Risk Classification|Stage 1/2/3 Credit Risk Classification]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining how a finance company must classify and re-stage exposures, treat the Stage 1/2/3 classification framework as the operative core of these Rules, not a mere concept: the Rules mandate three-stage classification and set specific re-staging cure periods (90 days non-retail; 60 days retail moving from 2B). The Rules derive their authority from Article 13 of the Finance Companies Control Law and Article 62 of its Implementing Regulation on provisioning. You would conclude that any compliance assessment of provisioning adequacy must test whether stage transitions and documented re-staging conditions meet these minimum requirements.

### [[Write-off Rules]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When advising a finance company on when a defaulted asset may or must be removed from the books, read the write-off rules as mandatory time-bound requirements within these Rules: unsecured exposures within 360 days, secured within 720 days, mortgages/corporate before 1,080 days from Stage 3 classification, unless the company's own policy is more conservative. Note the rules also preserve a legal point—de-recognition for accounting does not extinguish the company's recovery rights against collateral or borrower. You would conclude write-offs cannot be deferred hoping for recovery, and that continued collection efforts remain legally permissible post-write-off.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / 1.1 Introduction):** "These Rules set out the minimum requirements on Credit Risk Exposure Classification and Provisioning."
