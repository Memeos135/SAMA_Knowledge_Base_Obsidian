# Loss Data Identification, Collection and Treatment

## Connections

### [[Detailed Loss Event Type Classification]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining what belongs in the loss data set, treat the general/specific collection criteria and the detailed gross-loss classification rules as one operative regime, because the criteria (section 9) explicitly cross into the itemized list of what must be included or excluded from the gross loss computation. Section 9.2 defines gross/net loss and recoveries, and then specifies inclusions (direct charges, consequential costs, provisions, pending and timing losses) and exclusions (general maintenance, business enhancements, insurance premiums). For a compliance review you would conclude that a bank cannot self-define loss scope: an event's classification (e.g. whether legal expenses or timing losses count) directly changes the loss data set and therefore the LC and capital outcome.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 541 / 9.2.1):** "Banks must be able to identify the gross loss amounts, non-insurance recoveries, and insurance recoveries for all operational loss events."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 541-542 / 9.2.2-9.2.3):** "The following items must be included in the gross loss computation... following items should be excluded from the gross loss computation of the loss data set"

### [[Loss Component (LC)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a bank's operational risk capital under the standardized approach, you must read the Loss Component definition together with the loss data identification/collection/treatment criteria, because the LC's value (15x average annual losses over 10 years) is only valid if the underlying data meets those minimum standards. The document ties them directly: the LC calculation depends on the 10-year observation period, thresholds (SAR 44,600 / 446,000) and net-of-recovery rules set out in sections 8-9. Practically, you would conclude that a bank failing the data criteria cannot rely on its LC as calculated — it must instead hold capital at minimum 100% of BIC, potentially with a SAMA-imposed ILM greater than 1, and disclose the exclusion in Pillar 3.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 539 / Section 8):** "The proper identification, collection and treatment of internal loss data are essential prerequisites to capital calculation under the standardized approach."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 538 / 7.3.1):** "the Loss Component (LC) is equal to 15 times average annual operational risk losses incurred over the previous 10 years"
