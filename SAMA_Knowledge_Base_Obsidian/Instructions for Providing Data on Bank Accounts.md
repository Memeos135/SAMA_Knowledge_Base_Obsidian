# Instructions for Providing Data on Bank Accounts

## Connections

### [[Anti-Money Laundering Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding whether an exchanger or bank may hand customer/account information to a requesting authority, read these two together but note the link is thematic, not a direct citation. Node A (from the Exchange Business Procedures Manual, tagged AML) governs the AML/CTF customer-data and reporting regime; node B is the SAMA circular restricting direct disclosure of customers' banking data to governmental and non-governmental bodies without SAMA's no-objection, routing criminal/civil requests through SAMA. The practical consequence: banking-secrecy disclosure rules (node B) sit alongside — and constrain — routine data handling, so an AML obligation to gather/retain data does not authorise onward disclosure; check node B's enumerated permitted-disclosure exceptions before releasing anything, and do not treat the AML framework as a disclosure gateway.

### [[Banking Secrecy]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When advising a bank or licensed money changer on whether it may hand customer account data directly to a requesting government or non-government body, treat banking secrecy as the operative default rule that this circular implements: the instrument invokes banking secrecy as an established principle and sets a general prohibition on disclosure without SAMA's no-objection, with only enumerated exceptions. Because the circular is built on the secrecy principle and channels criminal-case, civil-case and other statutory information requests through SAMA, you would conclude that a direct request must generally be refused in writing (citing this circular's reference and date) unless it falls within the specifically permitted cases. Verify the requested disclosure against the two lists (prohibited-direct vs permitted-direct) before releasing any customer information.
