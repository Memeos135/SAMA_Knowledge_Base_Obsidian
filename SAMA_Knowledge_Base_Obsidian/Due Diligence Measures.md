# Due Diligence Measures

## Connections

### [[Know Your Customer (KYC)]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping customer onboarding for a deposit-taking finance company (DTFC), read the AML Law's due-diligence obligation and the DTFC KYC chapter together, because the KYC rules operationalize the statutory CDD duty for that sector — customer identification, acceptance policy, ongoing monitoring and STR reporting to SAMA's Financial Investigation Unit. The AML Law requires firms to apply and risk-calibrate due diligence, while the DTFC Rules prohibit anonymous/numeric accounts, require identifying the real beneficiary, and bar opening accounts where KYC suspicions arise. Because the link is inferred and the KYC text is a sector-specific rulebook rather than an express cross-reference to the AML Law, confirm the AML Implementing Regulation controls the detailed CDD triggers before treating the KYC chapter as the complete standard.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Art 7):** "Apply due diligence measures to their customers and the Implementing Regulation shall set forth the instances in which such measures shall be taken."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 50 / Chapter 13):** "DTFCs must NOT deal, open, operate or maintain any anonymous account, or with vague, counterfeited or incorrect name."
- **Caveat:** Inferred link; the DTFC KYC chapter does not expressly cite the AML Law. Verify the AML Implementing Regulation for the governing CDD triggers.

### [[Preventive Measures for FIs and DNFBPs]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping CDD obligations, treat 'Due Diligence Measures' as a component within the broader 'Preventive Measures' regime, not a separate track — the Law requires risk-based CDD, PEP screening (Art 8), correspondent-banking safeguards (Art 9) and wire-transfer originator/beneficiary information (Art 10), all within Section III. The due-diligence node draws the specific measures from the same articles the preventive-measures node covers, and crucially the Law defers the triggering instances and measure types to the Implementing Regulation. You would conclude that the extent of CDD is risk-calibrated (EDD where higher risk is identified) and that you must consult the Implementing Regulation to know exactly when each measure applies.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Art 7-8):** "Determine the extent of due diligence measures based on the risks... they shall apply enhanced due diligence measures"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Art 7(1)):** "Apply due diligence measures to their customers and the Implementing Regulation shall set forth the instances"
