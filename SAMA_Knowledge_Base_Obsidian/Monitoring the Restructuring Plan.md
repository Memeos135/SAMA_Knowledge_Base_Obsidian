# Monitoring the Restructuring Plan

## Connections

### [[Early Warning Signals (EWS)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating whether a bank's NPL lifecycle is coherent, connect early identification (EWS) to the restructuring/monitoring stage: the Guidelines state that effective early identification and early restructuring can prevent long-term default and losses. This link tells you the two functions are ends of one continuum — EWS triggers should ultimately support timely restructuring where a borrower is viable and cooperative. Verify that early signals actually drive escalation to restructuring options rather than allowing accounts to drift, since delay is expressly said to lower recovery.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 83 / Appendix 3 (iii)):** "If banks are effective in identifying early warning signs ... engaging in early restructuring solutions, this could prevent long-term default and losses"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / 2.1):** "To allow the opportunity to develop a corrective action plan at a very early stage"
- **Caveat:** The two nodes are thematically joined within the same Guidelines rather than by a direct article-level cross-reference; confirm the intended escalation path in the primary text.

### [[Restructuring KPIs]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank's restructuring practice meets SAMA's expectations, treat the Restructuring Principles and the monitoring of the restructuring plan as one continuous obligation rather than separate boxes. Both sit within the same SAMA credit-workout guideline: the principles (early warning identification, good faith, sustainable solutions, avoiding repeated short-term fixes) set the standard the ongoing monitoring must operationalise once a plan is live. For a compliance review this means checking that monitoring activity demonstrably follows the stated principles — e.g. that early warning signs trigger action and that repeated short-term rollovers are flagged rather than tolerated.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 46 / 5.2.1):** "the bank and the viable... borrower will agree on the restructuring strategy aiming to return the defaulted borrower to a fully performing status in the shortest feasible time frame."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 83 / Appendix 3):** "If banks are effective in identifying early warning signs... this could prevent long-term default... Banks and borrowers should seek sustainable solutions and avoid repeated short-term fixes."
