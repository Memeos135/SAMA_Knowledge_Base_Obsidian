# Cyber Security Risk Management and Compliance Domain

## Connections

### [[Cyber Security Framework (CSF)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding how cyber risks may be treated or accepted, apply the Risk Management and Compliance domain, because the CSF mandates a defined risk management process aligned with enterprise risk management and subject to periodic review and audit. Critically, any accepted risk must be within risk appetite, reported to the cyber security committee, and 'does not contradict SAMA regulations' — so risk acceptance cannot be used to waive a SAMA requirement. For a compliance decision, conclude that risk-acceptance sign-offs are bounded: they cannot override mandatory regulatory controls.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / 3.2):** "A cyber security risk management process should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organization's enterprise risk management process"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 21 / 3.2 control considerations):** "the accepted cyber security risk does not contradict SAMA regulations"

### [[Cyber Security Risk Management Process]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping what the Risk Management and Compliance domain actually demands, recognize that the domain is operationalized through the defined four-step Cyber Security Risk Management Process (identification, analysis, response, monitoring/review). Section 3.2.1 requires that this process be defined, approved, implemented and aligned with enterprise risk management, so the domain's obligations cannot be met by generic compliance activity alone — the specific process must exist. Conclude that assessing this domain means verifying the risk management process is documented and board-aligned, not just that periodic reviews occur.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / 3.2):** "Risk management is the ongoing process of identifying, analyzing, responding and monitoring and reviewing risks."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 19 / 3.2.1):** "A cyber security risk management process should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organization's enterprise risk management process."

### [[PCI-DSS]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a Member Organization's compliance obligations under the SAMA Cyber Security Framework, treat PCI-DSS not as optional best practice but as a mandatory standard the Framework incorporates by reference. The Risk Management and Compliance domain (section 3.2.3) expressly requires compliance with mandatory (inter)national industry standards and names PCI-DSS first among them, so a card-handling organization's PCI-DSS posture becomes a SAMA-enforceable requirement rather than merely a card-scheme contractual matter. Conclude that a gap against PCI-DSS is simultaneously a gap against the SAMA Framework and should be evaluated on that basis.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / 3.2):** "3.2 Cyber Security Risk Management and Compliance ... The compliance with the cyber security controls should be subject to periodic review and audit."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 22 / 3.2.3):** "The Member Organization should comply with: a. Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI-DSS)"

### [[SWIFT Customer Security Controls Framework]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** For an organization using SWIFT messaging, treat the SWIFT Customer Security Controls Framework as a mandatory standard pulled into SAMA's enforceable regime, not a separate voluntary attestation. Section 3.2.3 lists the SWIFT CSCF (March 2017) alongside PCI-DSS and EMV among the (inter)national industry standards the Member Organization must comply with. Conclude that SWIFT CSCF conformance is directly examinable under the SAMA Framework's compliance domain and non-conformance is a SAMA compliance gap.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 22 / 3.2.3):** "The Member Organization should comply with mandatory (inter)national industry standards."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 22 / 3.2.3):** "c. SWIFT Customer Security Controls Framework – March 2017."
