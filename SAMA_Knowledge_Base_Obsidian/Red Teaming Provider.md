# Red Teaming Provider

## Connections

### [[Green Team]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining who controls approval and engagement of an external red-teaming vendor, note that the Green Team (SAMA's IT Risk of Financial Sector Supervision) holds the gatekeeping role: the Member Organization cannot proceed to procurement until the Green Team approves the selected Red Teaming Provider. The Framework states the Green Team 'approves the selection of Red Teaming Provider,' and only 'upon approval of the Red Teaming Provider by the Green Team' does procurement begin against the Appendix A requirements. For a decision, this confirms vendor selection is not purely an internal commercial choice — SAMA-side sign-off is a precondition, and you should check that Green Team approval preceded contracting.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11 / Section 3.5):** "Upon approval of the Red Teaming Provider by the Green Team, the Member Organization should initiate their procurement process"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 7 / Section 2.2):** "The Green Team approves the selection of Red Teaming Provider and provides – when applicable – additional or specific threat intelligence for the Financial Sector"

### [[Red Team Evaluation Report (RTER)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When verifying deliverables owed by a contracted Red Teaming Provider, note that the reporting output (the basis of the Red Team Evaluation Report) is a defined obligation of the Provider, not merely a courtesy. After completing or stopping the test, the Provider must prepare initial observations and findings which provide the basis for evaluating the Blue Team's detection and response — and these feed the formal evaluation report. A reviewer should confirm the engagement contract and Appendix A provider requirements tie the Provider's methodology to producing this reporting deliverable, since the RTER underpins all downstream remediation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 20 / Appendix A):** "The Red Teaming Provider should have a clearly defined process ... the preparation, scenario development, execution and lessons"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / 5.4 Reporting):** "the Red Teaming Provider should prepare their initial observations and findings ... These observations and findings provide the basis of evaluating the detection and response capabilities of the Blue Team."

### [[SAMA Cyber Security Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When placing the Red Teaming Provider's role in the regulatory hierarchy, understand that the F.E.E.R. Framework (which governs the Provider) is a SAMA-mandated instrument aimed at testing the resilience and effectiveness of implemented cyber security controls — i.e. those established under the broader SAMA cyber security regime. Red Teaming is expressly not an audit or penetration test but a simulation of the organization's control effectiveness. A reviewer should treat provider engagement as a compliance obligation flowing from SAMA's mandate and scope its findings against the organization's existing cyber security controls, not as a standalone exercise.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / 1.1 Introduction):** "It ... seeks to provide insight into the level of resilience and effectiveness of the implemented cyber security controls and relevant processes (i.e. detection and response)."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / 1.4 Responsibilities):** "The framework is mandated by SAMA. SAMA is the owner and is responsible for periodically updating the Framework."
- **Caveat:** Node B labels the 'SAMA Cyber Security Framework'; the source text references the F.E.E.R. Framework and its SAMA mandate rather than naming a separate CSF document, so treat the link as one mandated SAMA cyber regime and verify the specific framework reference in primary text.
