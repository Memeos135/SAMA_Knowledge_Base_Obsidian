# Expected Credit Loss Provisioning

## Connections

### [[Eligible Collateral and Valuation]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining provisioning levels for a finance company, do not treat eligible collateral as a standalone concept — it feeds directly into the ECL computation only through the Loss Given Default channel, not Exposure at Default. The Rules expressly state that eligible collateral in Annexure 1 'should be included in the Loss Given Default calculation,' while 'no consideration is given to collateral when determining the exposure at default.' The practical consequence: collateral recognition, its haircuts and valuation-frequency requirements (e.g. annual re-valuation, appraiser licensing under commercial pledge law) govern LGD inputs and therefore the size of the ECL provision — so a compliance reviewer should verify collateral eligibility and valuation currency when testing whether ECL provisions are adequate.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 (definitions)):** "No consideration is given to collateral when determining the exposure at default."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 24 / Annexure 1):** "Eligible collateral as elaborated in Annexure 1 should be included in the Loss Given Default calculation."

### [[IFRS 9]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When testing whether a finance company's ECL provisioning is compliant, recognize that the Rules build the ECL concept directly on IFRS 9 mechanics: expected credit loss, its 12-month and lifetime variants, and the PD/EAD/LGD components are all defined 'as prescribed by IFRS 9.' The Rules layer SAMA-specific minimums (annual methodology review, quarterly reassessment, Board reporting) on top of that accounting standard. You would conclude that ECL measurement must satisfy both the IFRS 9 accounting basis and SAMA's governance/frequency overlays—compliance with one does not evidence the other.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 23 / Section iii):** "Adopting, documenting and approving sound expected credit loss methodologies... expected credit loss methodologies must be reviewed annually"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Definitions):** "As prescribed by IFRS 9, the estimated credit losses expected to be incurred from the occurrence of a credit event, e.g. default."

### [[Rules Governing Credit Risk Exposure Classification and Provisioning]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a finance company's provisioning adequacy, treat expected credit loss (ECL) provisioning as the core operative obligation of these Rules, governed both by IFRS 9 methodology and by SAMA-specific governance duties. The Rules require sound, documented, board-approved ECL methodologies reviewed at least annually (more often as new information arises), with quarterly reporting to the board and independent annual internal-audit evaluation. You should conclude that ECL is not merely an accounting output but a supervised process with defined review, reporting and audit cadences, and check those governance elements when evaluating compliance.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 23):** "Adopting, documenting and approving sound expected credit loss methodologies... reviewed annually, or more frequently when the need arises."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4):** "the finance company must set provisions for contingent losses and risks in accordance with international accounting standards."
