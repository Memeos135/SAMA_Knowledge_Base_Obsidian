# Caps for Securitization Exposures

## Connections

### [[Look-Through Approach for Senior Exposures]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing the cap on senior securitization exposures, treat the look-through approach as the mechanism that delivers the cap: it permits a senior exposure to receive a maximum risk weight equal to the exposure-weighted-average risk weight of the underlying pool, conditional on the bank knowing the pool composition at all times. The caps section (18.50 onward) is where the look-through option is defined and bounded, including the 15% floor interaction in 18.51. Conclude that reliance on the look-through cap is only available for senior exposures and requires continuous knowledge of underlying composition — otherwise the cap cannot be claimed.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 248 / 18.51):** "Where the risk weight cap results in a lower risk weight than the floor risk weight of 15%, the risk weight resulting from the cap should be used."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 247 / 18.50):** "Banks may apply a "look-through" approach to senior securitization exposures ... maximum risk weight equal to the exposure weighted-average risk weight"

### [[SAMA Circular No. 341000015689 (Regulatory Capital Under Basel III)]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When applying the securitization capital caps, note that the surrounding framework is anchored to the Basel III regulatory-capital regime SAMA adopted via Circular No. 341000015689; paragraph 18.49 expressly cites that circular for the rule barring recognition of gain-on-sale in regulatory capital where implicit support is provided. Treat the circular as the governing instrument giving these capital-treatment provisions their binding force in KSA. Conclude that the caps and related capital rules must be read against that circular's terms, and any gain-on-sale recognition claim tied to a supported securitization is precluded under it.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 247 / 18.49):** "banks would not be permitted to recognize in regulatory capital any gain on sale, in accordance with SAMA Circular No. 341000015689"
- **Grounding — related node (markdown/SAMA_EN_3502_VER1.md (document node)):** "SAMA Circular No. 341000015689, Date: 19 December 2012"

### [[Securitization of Non-Performing Loans]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When applying capital caps to a securitization, first check whether the underlying pool qualifies as an NPL securitization (W ≥ 90% under 23.1), because Chapter 23 overrides the ordinary treatment for such pools. Paragraph 18.48 directs that exposures to securitizations of non-performing loans be handled with the adjustments in Chapter 23, and 23.3 even precludes SEC-IRBA where the foundation approach is used. Conclude that the general caps section does not stand alone for NPL pools — you must apply the Chapter 23 adjustments and eligibility restrictions before relying on the standard cap.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 247 / 18.48):** "For exposures to securitizations of non-performing loans as defined in paragraph 23.1, banks must apply the framework with the adjustments laid out in ... chapter 23"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 323 / 23.1):** "A non-performing loan securitization (NPL securitization) means a securitization where the underlying pool's variable W ... is equal to or higher than 90%"
