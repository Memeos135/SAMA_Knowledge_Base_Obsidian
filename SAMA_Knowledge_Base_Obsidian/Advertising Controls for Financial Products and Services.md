# Advertising Controls for Financial Products and Services

## Connections

### [[Finance Companies Control Law (M51)|Finance Companies Control Law (M/51)]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a finance company advertises its products, don't assume the Advertising Controls override its sectoral law: the Controls expressly require compliance with related laws and regulations 'in a manner not conflicting' with them, and list the Finance Companies Control Law and its Implementing Regulation among the related instruments. This means the Advertising Controls sit alongside, not above, the Finance Companies Control Law. You should reconcile any advertising requirement with the finance-company regime and, where conflict arises, the sectoral law and its implementing regulation continue to bind.

### [[Financial Institution Customer Protection]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing advertising obligations, treat customer protection as the governing purpose that drives every requirement in these Advertising Controls, not a background aspiration. The Controls are issued expressly under SAMA's Article 4 mandate to 'put in place instructions and procedures to protect the customers of financial institutions,' and their stated objective is protecting customers and reinforcing disclosure and transparency. You should therefore interpret ambiguous advertising provisions in favour of customer/consumer protection and disclosure, since that is the enabling authority and stated aim of the instrument.

### [[SAMA Central Bank Law (Royal Decree M36)|SAMA Central Bank Law (Royal Decree M/36)]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When testing SAMA's authority to impose these Advertising Controls, rely on the Central Bank Law as the enabling instrument: the Controls are issued 'based on' the SAMA Law (Royal Decree M/36) and quote its Article 4 empowering SAMA to issue regulations and instructions relating to financial institutions and to protect their customers. This is a subordinate-to-parent-law link. You should conclude that the Controls' binding force derives from and is bounded by the Central Bank Law, and that their validity would be measured against that statutory mandate rather than any lower instrument.
