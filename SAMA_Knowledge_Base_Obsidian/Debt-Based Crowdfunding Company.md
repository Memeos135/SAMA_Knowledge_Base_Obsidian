# Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company

## Connections

### [[Credit Limits]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a crowdfunding platform's financing capacity and exposure, apply Article 26's credit-limit ceilings directly to the licensed Company as binding prudential caps. The limits bind the same defined Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company: aggregate outstanding finance may not exceed 40x capital and reserves absent SAMA non-objection, financing is confined to KSA-registered commercial enterprises (no consumer-purpose finance), per-SME beneficiary exposure is capped at SAR 7.5m, and per-Participant contribution is limited to 25% of a request and SAR 250,000 overall (Eligible Participants excepted). The reader should treat these as hard limits requiring a written SAMA non-objection to exceed, and note the express carve-out that consumer-purpose lending is prohibited.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3, Art 1.2.3):** "Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company: A joint-stock company licensed to engage in Debt-Based Crowdfunding activity"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15, Art 26):** "The total amount of existing finance provided through the Platform shall not exceed 40 times the ... capital and reserves, unless a written non-objection letter from SAMA is obtained"

### [[DBC Risk Management Requirements]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a DBC Company's obligations, the risk-management article is a mandatory, board-owned framework rather than a general aspiration. Article 19 requires a written, board-approved and annually-updated policy covering an enumerated risk list — including money laundering/terrorist financing, fraud, outsourcing and Participant-protection (Institutional Beneficiary default) risk — which ties this AML/CTF and consumer-protection exposure directly into the Company's governance duty. Conclude that a DBC's AML/CTF and default-risk controls are legally inseparable from its risk-management policy, and any compliance assessment must confirm board approval and coverage of the full listed risk taxonomy.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 21):** "SAMA may take necessary actions to ensure the compliance of Debt-Based Crowdfunding Companies with the relevant rules, laws, regulations, and instructions"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / Art 19):** "Establish a written policy that includes a clear business strategy for risk management, approved and annually updated by its board of directors"

### [[Debt-Based Crowdfunding Platform]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing DBC obligations, treat the Platform not as mere technology but as the defined regulated channel through which the Company must discharge disclosure, consent and fund-handling duties. Article 1 defines the Platform as the digital means 'used and run by a Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company', and Article 28 attaches operative duties to it (clarify business nature, publish risk information, obtain electronic acknowledgment before use). The consequence: obligations expressed as 'on the Platform' or 'before using the Platform' are enforceable Company duties, so compliance review of the Company necessarily includes what is displayed and consented-to via the Platform.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 17 / Art 28):** "By using the Platform, the Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company shall clarify the nature of the business offered and publish awareness-raising information"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 3 / Art 1 (2.4)):** "Debt-Based Crowdfunding Platform: A web-based platform or any other digital means... used and run by a Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company"

### [[Rules for Engaging in Debt-Based Crowdfunding]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When identifying the duty-holder for every obligation in these Rules, note that the Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company is the defined licensed entity on whom substantially all requirements fall. Article 1 defines it as a joint-stock company licensed to engage in the activity, and operative articles (segregation of Collection Account funds, disclosure, cooperation with SAMA inspections) address that entity by name. The reader should therefore attribute compliance liability — including the express warning that refusal to provide information to SAMA is a violation of the Rules and the Law — to the licensed Company, not to Participants or Institutional Beneficiaries.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 21):** "The Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company and its staff shall not conceal ... any information, documents, violations"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 3, Art 1.2.3):** "Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company: A joint-stock company licensed to engage in Debt-Based Crowdfunding activity"
