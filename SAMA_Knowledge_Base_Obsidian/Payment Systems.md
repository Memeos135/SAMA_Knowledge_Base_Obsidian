# Payment Systems

## Connections

### [[Law of Payments and Payment Services]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping who is caught by the Law of Payments and Payment Services, anchor on its Article 1 definition of 'Payment Systems' because that defined term fixes the licensing perimeter. The Law applies to payment systems and their operators (Article 3) and prohibits operating a payment system without SAMA licensing (Article 4), and 'Payment Systems' is a defined set of instruments, procedures, and rules for processing and settling payment orders. Conclude that whether an arrangement is a 'payment system' — including possible SIPS classification — determines licensing, capital, and outsourcing obligations, so classify the activity against this definition first.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 2 / Art 1):** "Payment Systems: A set of instruments, procedures, and rules for processing and settling payment orders and clearing tr[ansactions]."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Art 3-4):** "This Law shall apply to payment systems and their Operators ... A person may not operate a payment system ... unless licensed by SAMA."

### [[Saudi Central Bank]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When establishing SAMA's legal authority to license and supervise payment systems, anchor it to the Saudi Central Bank Law rather than treating the Payment Systems Law as self-standing. The SCB Law expressly empowers the Bank to establish/operate national payment, settlement and clearing infrastructures and to issue rules, directives and licenses and supervise them within its jurisdiction — the enabling mandate that the Payment and Payment Services Law then operationalises. For a licensing or enforcement question, cite both: the SCB Law for institutional competence and the Payment Systems Law for the specific licensing prohibition (Article 4).
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / Art 4):** "A person may not operate a payment system or provide payment services in the Kingdom unless licensed by SAMA."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 3 / Art 4(7)):** "Establishing, developing, and operating infrastructures of national payment, settlement, and clearing systems; issuing rules, directives, and licenses; and overseeing and supervising"

### [[Systemically Important Payment System (SIPS)]] — `implements` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing what regime a given payment system falls under, treat SIPS as a subset of Payment Systems that carries heightened obligations, not a separate track. The Payment and Payment Services Law gives SAMA power to classify any payment system by importance, and the SIPS definition is triggered by SAMA's discretionary classification based on volume, value, nature and connectivity to settlement/clearing. Practically, you cannot know a system's SIPS-specific duties (Articles 9–10) until SAMA has issued a classification notice; absent that notice, only the general Payment Systems obligations apply.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8):** "Setting classifications and requirements for payment systems based on their importance, including classifying any payment system as a SIPS."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4):** "Systemically Important Payment System (SIPS): Any payment system classified by SAMA as important pursuant to certain criteria set thereby based on the volume, value, and nature of transactions"
