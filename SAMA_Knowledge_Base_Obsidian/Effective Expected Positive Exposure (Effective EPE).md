# Effective Expected Positive Exposure (Effective EPE)

## Connections

### [[Exposure at Default (EAD)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining how a bank must measure counterparty credit exposure for capital, treat Effective EPE and EAD as parts of one calculation chain rather than separate concepts: the internal models framework (SAMA_EN_4283) defines EAD as the exposure amount for a counterparty, and Effective EPE is the input from which EAD is derived under the IMM. If you are assessing capital adequacy for OTC derivatives or SFTs, you should trace the EAD figure back to the Effective EPE model that produced it, because the two documents describe the same measurement pipeline. Conclude that the disclosure and definitional treatments in both sources must be read together to validate a bank's counterparty exposure figures.
- **Grounding — this node (SAMA_EN_4283 Page 10):** "the effective exposure at the... Effective expected exposure"
- **Grounding — related node (SAMA_EN_4283 Page 16 / Para 5.10):** "the exposure amount or EAD for a given counterparty is equal to the sum of the exposure amounts"

### [[Internal Models Method (IMM)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating a bank's counterparty exposure measure, Effective EPE should be understood as an output of the Internal Models Method regime, since IMM is the approved route for modelling counterparty exposure/EAD under section 7. The provided IMM context establishes that model-based exposure estimation for regulatory capital requires SAMA approval and application across all relevant CCR exposures. Because the direct definitional link (Effective EPE within the IMM formula) is not quoted in the supplied context, treat this as a lead: verify the specific Effective EPE definition and its role in the IMM EAD calculation in the primary text before relying on it.
- **Grounding — this node:** "Effective Expected Positive Exposure (concept associated with counterparty credit risk exposure measurement)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 597 / 7.2):** "A bank may also choose to adopt an internal models method to measure counterparty credit risk (CCR) for regulatory capital purposes"
- **Caveat:** The supplied context for the EAD/Effective EPE node is credit-risk-mitigation disclosure templates, not the Effective EPE definition itself; confirm the Effective EPE formula and its placement within the IMM section of the primary text before relying on this link.

### [[Internal Models Method (IMM)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating whether a bank may use its internal model to compute counterparty exposure, read Effective EPE as the mandatory output the IMM must generate: paragraph 7.36 requires the distribution of exposures generated by the internal model used to calculate effective EPE to be integrated into day-to-day CCR management, and paragraph 7.6 measures EAD under the IMM at netting-set level. This means IMM approval hinges on the credibility and use of the Effective EPE calculation, not merely on possessing a model. You should check that any bank claiming IMM treatment demonstrates the track record and 'use test' tied to its Effective EPE distributions before relying on its reduced capital figures.
- **Grounding — this node (SAMA_EN_4283 Page 59 / Para 7.36):** "The distribution of exposures generated by the internal model used to calculate effective EPE must be closely integrated into the day-to-day CCR management process"
- **Grounding — related node (SAMA_EN_4283 Page 48 / Para 7.1):** "A bank that wishes to adopt an internal models method to measure exposure or exposure at default (EAD)... must seek SAMA approval"
