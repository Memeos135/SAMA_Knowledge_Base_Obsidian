# ILAAP Process

## Connections

### [[Guidelines on the Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment Plan (ILAAP)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping who is accountable for liquidity adequacy at a locally incorporated bank, treat the 'ILAAP Process' concept as the operational heart of the guidelines document rather than a separate standard: the document defines the ILAAP and Section 5 assigns its governance. The link tells you that the guidelines place ultimate responsibility on the Board of Directors, require annual Board-approved and signed submissions, and mandate segregation of functions and internal review/validation. For a compliance decision, conclude that ILAAP obligations cannot be delegated away from Board accountability, and that supervisory dialogue with SAMA presupposes a Board-owned, annually renewed process.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / Section 5.1):** "The Board of Directors has the ultimate responsibility for the implementation of the ILAAP... adhering to the segregation of functions."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Section B.1):** "The Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment Process (ILAAP) is defined as "the processes for the identification, measurement, management and monitoring of liquidity...""

### [[LCRNSFR Liquidity Ratios|LCR/NSFR Liquidity Ratios]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining what liquidity metrics the ILAAP must reflect, do not treat the LCR/NSFR (Pillar 1 regulatory ratios) as satisfied merely by standalone reporting — the ILAAP references them as inputs it must incorporate. The guidelines require the ILAAP to set monitoring limits against LCR, NSFR, LDR and the SAMA Liquidity Ratio, disclose their minimum requirements, and demonstrate how those limits map into SAMA returns. For a compliance decision, conclude that the internal (Pillar 2) assessment and the regulatory (Pillar 1) ratios are interdependent: LCR/NSFR positions and limit-breach analysis under normal and stressed conditions must appear within the ILAAP, not only in the separate prudential returns.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Section 4.2):** "The Pillar 2 liquidity framework should focus on liquidity risks not captured, or not fully captured, under Pillar 1 requirements."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 13 / Sections 6.1–6.2):** "Regulatory Liquidity requirements under LCR, NSFR, LDR, and SAMA Liquidity Ratio... a demonstration of how the liquidity limits are reflected in SAMA's returns."

### [[Liquidity Stress-testing]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When judging whether an ILAAP submission is defensible, treat liquidity stress-testing as an embedded requirement of the ILAAP rather than a separable exercise: the guidelines require the ILAAP to analyse the internal stress-testing framework and feed its results into the risk framework and strategic planning. Section 9 and Annexure (1) set the substance (scenario design, governance, sensitivity analysis, results by risk driver), and stress results must inform the liquidity adequacy conclusion the Board signs. For a compliance decision, conclude that an ILAAP lacking a governed, challenged stress-testing programme whose outputs drive adequacy assessment would not meet the 'comprehensive and rigorous' standard SAMA expects.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / Section 9):** "Analyse the internal liquidity risk stress-testing framework, including the process and governance of and challenge to scenario design..."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 22 / Annexure (1)):** "Stress-testing is a generic term for the assessment of vulnerability of individual financial institutions and the financial system to internal and external shocks."

### [[Pillar 2 Liquidity Risk Drivers]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank's ILAAP is 'comprehensive' for SAMA supervisory purposes, use the Pillar 2 liquidity risk drivers as the minimum content checklist the guidelines mandate. The ILAAP (a Pillar 2 process) must identify, measure and provide mitigation for at least the enumerated drivers — wholesale/retail funding, intra-day, intra-group, cross-currency, off-balance-sheet, franchise-viability, and marketable/non-marketable asset risks. For a compliance decision, conclude that omitting any of these named drivers (or failing to describe behaviour under normal and stress conditions) leaves the ILAAP incomplete against SAMA's stated minimum, regardless of how well Pillar 1 ratios are met.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Section 4.2):** "It is incumbent on banks to undertake their own assessment of liquidity risks, including Pillar 2 risks, and take appropriate measu[res]."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 14 / Section 6.4):** "At a minimum, the ILAAP should describe, assess and analyse the following pillar 2 liquidity risk drivers: i. Wholesale secured and unsecured funding risk..."
