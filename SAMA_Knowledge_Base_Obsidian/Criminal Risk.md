### [[Forgery]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Forgery is expressly one of the criminal-risk sub-elements (section 2.1.2) within this operational-risk guide, not a separate compliance heading. The index nests 'Forgery' under '2.1 Criminal Risks,' and the text records it as historically the second-largest area of operational loss (~12%), tied to employee failures to verify document authenticity. For assessment this means forgery-loss controls belong to the operational/criminal-risk program the Board-approved plan must cover, and the guide flags an expected shift from simple to technically sophisticated forgeries as advances in payment systems occur.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / Section 2.1):** "financial losses attributable to fraud end other criminal activities... 100% of all operational losses claimed under existing insurance coverage"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 7 / Section 2.1.2):** "forgery (including check fraud) was the second largest area of operational loss, accounting for approximately 12% of total reported losses"

### [[Fraud]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Fraud is the first and largest enumerated criminal-risk element (section 2.1.1) in this guide, so it should be scoped inside the operational/criminal-risk framework rather than treated as an isolated AML matter. The guide states criminal activity is the single largest operational-risk area and identifies fraud (predominantly employee-perpetrated, plus card, ATM and POS fraud) as the leading operational loss. For a compliance reader, this locates fraud-loss controls within the Board-approved Operational Risk Management Plan and signals particular attention to internal (employee) fraud and payment-card exposure.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / Section 2.1):** "Historically, the single largest area of operational risk within the Saudi banks has been that associated with criminal activities"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Section 2.1.1):** "Fraud currently represents the single largest area of operational loss within the Kingdom's banking system"

### [[Overview on Operational Risk]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping this SAMA operational-risk guidance, treat criminal risk as a sub-category of the broader operational-risk concept rather than a separate regime. The overview defines operational risk as 'pure risk' with only loss/no-loss outcomes and expressly names 'losses due to criminal activity (fraud, counterfeiting, forgery, etc.)' as examples, so criminal risk falls inside the operational-risk framework the guide addresses. For a compliance reader this means criminal-risk controls are part of the Operational Risk Management Plan approved by the Board, not an independent AML obligation, and should be assessed within that governance structure.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 51 / Glossary):** "Examples of operational risk are: losses due to criminal activity (fraud, counterfeiting, forgery, etc.)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Section 1):** "Operational risk - as distinct from financial risk - represents pure risk... only two possible outcomes - loss or no loss"
