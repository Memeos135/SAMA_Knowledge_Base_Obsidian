# Foreign Bank Branch Regulation

**Cohesion:** 0.12 - loosely connected
**Members:** 19 nodes

## Why this community

Prudential and governance regime for foreign bank branches and agent banking arrangements in KSA, all subordinate to the Banking Control Law. Covers how a licensed foreign branch must be governed, funded and liquid, and how a principal bank may deploy banking agents while retaining regulatory responsibility.

## How members connect

- Hierarchy: the Banking Control Law is the enabling statute cited by both the FBB Regulations and the Agent Banking Regulation, which act as implementing rules with mandatory attachments (Return Form A, LCR/NSFR B, RWA/Funding D) as prescribed reporting obligations.
- FBB Regulations chain governance and prudential duties onto the branch: corporate governance/risk management, the GM/CEO Senior Management Function, and liquidity requirements (LCR, NSFR, FR, CFP) that resolve into specific reporting attachments.
- Agent Banking Regulation defines the principal Bank and its Banking Agent, allocating obligations: Fit & Proper assessment sits on the principal, while AML/CFT-KYC and Consumer Protection duties attach at the agent-facing customer interface.
- Cross-regime flag: agent banking pulls in AML/CFT/KYC and Consumer Protection regimes — the principal bank remains accountable for agent conduct despite outsourcing the customer touchpoint.
