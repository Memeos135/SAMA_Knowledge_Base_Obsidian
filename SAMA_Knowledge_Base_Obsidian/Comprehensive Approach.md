- **What this link tells you:** When determining haircuts for collateralized repo-style and capital-market-driven transactions, read the collateralized-transactions provisions together with the comprehensive approach, because 9.55 is the operative framework that assigns different holding periods and haircuts based on revaluation and remargining frequency. This distinguishes repo-style transactions and OTC derivatives/margin lending (documentation with remargining clauses) from secured lending (generally without), which drives the minimum holding periods in Table 15. ### [[Master Netting Agreements for SFTs]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When recognizing bilateral netting across SFTs, read the master-netting provisions as an integral part of the comprehensive approach rather than a separate regime: 9.63 states that all other haircut rules under the comprehensive approach (9.40-9.60) apply equivalently to banks using bilateral netting agreements for SFTs. Recognition is gated on the 9.61-9.62 conditions (legal enforceability in each jurisdiction, daily mark-to-market, eligible collateral), and the resulting E* becomes the exposure value/EAD. Conclude that netting benefit for SFTs is only available where those enforceability and revaluation conditions are met, and the comprehensive-approach haircut mechanics still govern the calculation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 80 / 9.63):** "All other rules regarding the calculation of haircuts under the comprehensive approach stated in paragraphs 9.40 to 9.60 equivalently apply for banks using bilateral netting agreements for SFTs"

### [[Supervisory Haircuts]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining how much collateral relief a bank may claim under the credit risk mitigation rules, treat the comprehensive approach and supervisory haircuts as a single mechanism: the comprehensive approach mandates adjusting exposure and collateral values, and supervisory haircuts are the prescribed factors used to do so. Chapter 9 states banks 'must use the applicable supervisory haircuts to adjust both the amount of the exposure to the counterparty and the value of any collateral,' with haircut size driven by holding period, instrument type, maturity and remargining frequency. You should conclude that any RWA reduction from eligible collateral must be net of these haircuts (volatility-adjusted), and cannot be based on nominal collateral values.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 71 / para 9.40):** "In the comprehensive approach... banks must use the applicable supervisory haircuts to adjust both the amount of the exposure to the counterparty and the value of any collateral received"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 71 / para 9.41-9.42):** "The size of the haircuts that banks must use depends on the prescribed holding period... type of instrument, type of transaction, residual maturity and the frequency of marking to market and remargining"
