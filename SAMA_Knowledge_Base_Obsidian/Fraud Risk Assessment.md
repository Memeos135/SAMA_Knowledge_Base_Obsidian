# Fraud Risk Assessment

## Connections

### [[Due Diligence]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When reviewing a Member Organisation's fraud Due Diligence standards, do not evaluate them in isolation: the framework requires that Due Diligence standards be aligned to the risks identified in the Fraud Risk Assessment, so the FRA output is the benchmark against which DD scope and intensity are judged. Practically, an organisation cannot demonstrate adequate employee, customer and third-party DD without showing those standards trace back to the risks its FRA identified. Conclude that a stale or missing FRA undermines the defensibility of the DD regime, and any DD review should first confirm the underlying FRA linkage. Note this is fraud-regime Due Diligence, distinct from AML/CTF CDD.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 28 / 4.1.2 Fraud Risk Assessment):** "Member Organisations should conduct a Fraud Risk Assessment to identify fraud risks to which they or their customers are subject"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 31 / 4.2 Due Diligence):** "Due Diligence standards should be aligned to the risks identified in the Fraud Risk Assessment."

### [[Fraud Risk Appetite]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a Member Organisation's Fraud Risk Appetite is validly set, check that it derives from the Fraud Risk Assessment: the framework requires appetite to be 'based on the outcome of the Fraud Risk Assessment' and aligned to overall enterprise risk appetite. This creates a dependency — appetite thresholds and limits are only defensible if grounded in identified inherent and residual fraud risks. Conclude that a Fraud Risk Appetite statement disconnected from current FRA output is a compliance gap, and appetite reviews should be tested against the most recent assessment.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 28 / 4.1.2 Fraud Risk Assessment):** "Determination of the residual risk of fraud that the Member Organisation remains exposed to following testing of implemented controls."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 30 / 4.1.3 (Fraud Risk Appetite)):** "The Member Organisation Fraud Risk Appetite should be based on the outcome of the Fraud Risk Assessment and aligned to the overall risk appetite of the organisation."

### [[Fraud Risk Management Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the Risk Management (Prevent) obligations, treat the Fraud Risk Assessment as a required component sitting within the broader Fraud Risk Management Framework, which itself must be defined, approved, implemented and aligned to the enterprise risk management process. This means an FRA is not a standalone deliverable — its adequacy is judged as part of whether the overall FRMF is in place and effective. Conclude that reviewing the FRA requires also confirming the governing framework exists and is board/senior-management approved, since the framework is the parent obligation the FRA operates under.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 28 / 4.1.2 Fraud Risk Assessment):** "A Member Organisation should conduct an enterprise-wide Fraud Risk Assessment as part of its Counter-Fraud Programme."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 26 / 4.1 Risk Management):** "A Fraud Risk Management Framework should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organisation's enterprise risk management process."
