# Code of Conduct and Work Ethics for Financial Institutions

## Connections

### [[AML and CFT Principles]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping which conduct rules a financial institution's staff owe, note that AML/CFT is embedded as a substantive pillar of this Code of Conduct, not a separate regime: under the heading 'combating financial and administrative corruption crimes,' the Code lists 'combating money laundering, terrorist financing and suspicious transactions' as a required principle of employee conduct. Because the Code sets a minimum standard binding on all 'financial institution personnel' — including board, executives, contractors and third-party staff — the reader should conclude that AML/CFT expectations here reinforce (and do not replace) the detailed AML/CFT law and rules, and that individual staff conduct breaches on this point are actionable under the Code's non-compliance consequences as well as under AML/CFT instructions.

### [[Conflict of Interest and Confidentiality]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing governance obligations on individuals within a SAMA-supervised institution, treat confidentiality and information-handling as enforceable conduct duties: the Code lists 'safeguarding confidentiality and mechanisms for information disclosure,' misuse and leaking of inside information for market manipulation, and gifts/hospitality among its mandatory principles. These bind all personnel (directors, executives, employees, consultants and third-party staff) as a minimum standard that the board must adopt into a firm-specific policy. The reader should conclude that conflict-of-interest and confidentiality breaches by individuals are disciplinable under the Code's consequences section and should be checked against, not substituted for, related governance and insider-information rules.
