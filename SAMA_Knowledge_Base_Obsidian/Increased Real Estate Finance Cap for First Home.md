# Increased Real Estate Finance Cap for First Home

## Connections

### [[Real Estate Finance Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining permissible loan-to-value limits on residential real estate finance, read this circular as an exercise of a power expressly reserved to SAMA under the Real Estate Finance Law's implementing regulation. Article 12 caps financing and states "وللمؤسسة تغيير هذه النسبة وفقاً لأوضاع السوق السائدة"; on that basis SAMA raised the first-home LTV cap for citizens while retaining 70% (banks) / 85% (finance companies) for second homes. Conclude that the current binding LTV ceiling is set by this circular (as amended), not the base regulation figure, and that the cap varies by lender type and by first- vs. second-home status.

### [[Real Estate Finance Product Instructions for Individuals]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When advising on individual real-estate finance obligations, treat these two as complementary layers of the same regime rather than duplicates: 4897 fixes the minimum conduct/disclosure requirements financiers must meet when offering the product (affordability study, product explanation, binding offer, independent credit advisor), while 5410 changes a quantitative prudential parameter — raising the loan-to-value cap for citizens' first home. Both bind banks and real-estate finance companies supervised by SAMA, and 5410 traces its authority to Article 12 of the Real Estate Finance Law's Implementing Regulation, so they operate together on the same product. The link tells you a compliant offer must satisfy 4897's process rules AND respect the LTV limits as amended by 5410; check 5410's differentiated caps (first home vs. second-and-beyond, banks vs. finance companies) before relying on any single ceiling.
