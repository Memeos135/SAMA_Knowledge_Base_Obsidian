# Key Principles of Governance in Financial Institutions

## Connections

### [[Board of Directors]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping who is accountable for governance at a SAMA-supervised financial institution, read the Board of Directors as the apex body to which the Governance Principles assign specific, non-delegable duties. The Principles require separation of Chairman and CEO roles and a control framework (risk, internal audit, compliance), and then vest the Board with approval of strategy, structures and remuneration policy plus oversight of committees and independence assessments (paras 11–12, 50–52). Conclude that governance obligations attach to the Board as a defined organ — not merely to management — and check that Central Bank no-objection/notification steps (para 12) are observed for any Board or leadership change, since these are express prerequisites.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / General Provisions (b)):** "independence of and separation between the position of the Chairman of the Board of Directors and that of the Chief Executive Officer (CEO)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / para 12):** "A written no-objection letter shall be obtained from the Central Bank prior to nominating any member of the Board and its committees"

### [[Executive Management]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping who bears which governance duty in a SAMA-supervised financial institution, treat 'Executive Management' as a defined role, not a loose label: the Principles define it as persons managing daily activities and proposing/implementing strategic decisions, and 4th Principle plus items 56-60 assign specific proposal-and-implementation obligations (strategy, capital expenditure, organizational structure, conduct policy, remuneration policy), all subject to Board approval. This tells you the document imposes enforceable, delegated responsibilities on executive management that are distinct from and subordinate to Board responsibilities. When allocating accountability, check the executive management definition and item 30 (Board 'forms' and supervises executive management) to confirm a duty sits with management rather than the Board.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 17 / items 56-60):** "Propose and implement a comprehensive strategy for the financial institution... Propose the organizational and functional structures... including the CEO, and submit same to the Board for approval."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 3 / Definitions):** "Executive Management: Persons entrusted with managing the daily activities of the financial institution, and proposing and implementing strategic decisions."

### [[Saudi Central Bank Law]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing the legal force of these governance Principles, note they are SAMA-issued instructions applying to 'financial institutions under the control and supervision of the Saudi Central Bank' — the supervisory authority created and empowered under the Saudi Central Bank Law. This makes the Principles subordinate regulatory instruments deriving their binding character from SAMA's statutory supervisory mandate, and they expressly do not override other laws. When determining enforceability or scope, treat the Principles as SAMA supervisory requirements and check the parent Central Bank Law and the other named governance regulations for the full obligation set rather than reading the Principles in isolation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / General Provisions (c)):** "These principles shall not prejudice the requirements imposed on financial institutions under other relevant laws, regulations, and instructions."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 1 / title):** "Key Principles of Governance in Financial Institutions under the Control and Supervision of the Saudi Central Bank"
- **Caveat:** Link rests on the document's identification of SAMA as issuing/supervising authority; the Saudi Central Bank Law itself is referenced as parent statute rather than quoted in the provided context.
