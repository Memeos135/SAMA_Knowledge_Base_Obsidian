# Information Technology Governance Framework

## Connections

### [[Business Continuity Management Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping obligations under the IT Governance Framework, do not assume it absorbs business-continuity requirements — the Framework explicitly states it has an interrelationship with other corporate policies but does not address non-IT requirements of those areas. This means a Member Organization must satisfy business continuity obligations through the applicable BCM regime, treating the IT Governance Framework as covering only the IT-governance dimension. For a compliance decision you should conclude that gaps in BCM cannot be closed by pointing to IT Governance compliance, and vice versa.

### [[IT Governance Maturity Model]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When judging whether a Member Organization is 'compliant' with the Framework, note that compliance is not binary but graded against the six-level maturity model (0–5), and that levels 3–5 require first meeting all criteria of preceding levels. SAMA reviews and audits the periodic self-assessment 'to determine the level of compliance with the framework and the IT maturity level'. For a compliance decision, conclude that partial or ad-hoc implementation still maps to a defined (low) maturity score, and that a target maturity level set by SAMA — not mere existence of controls — is the benchmark to satisfy.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6):** "To achieve an appropriate maturity level of IT controls within the Member Organizations."

### [[IT Governance and Leadership]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping which IT controls a SAMA-regulated Member Organization must implement, treat 'IT Governance and Leadership' as one of the mandated domains of the parent Framework, not an optional best-practice topic. ### [[IT Risk Management]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a Member Organization's IT risk obligations, read section 3.2 as binding Control Requirements under the Framework rather than as guidance. The Framework mandates that the IT risk management process 'should be defined, approved, implemented and communicated' and 'aligned with the Member Organization's enterprise risk management process', including third-party relationships and associated risks. For a compliance decision, conclude that IT risk identification, treatment, reporting and profiling must be evidenced and are subject to SAMA self-assessment and audit, and that outsourced/third-party IT risks fall squarely within this obligation.

- **What this link tells you:** When assigning IT-risk obligations to a Member Organization, treat the IT Governance Framework and SAMA's Cyber Security Framework as complementary but distinct instruments — the IT Governance Framework addresses effective use and governance of IT and expressly excludes non-IT requirements of related areas, so cybersecurity control obligations are not satisfied merely by meeting IT-governance maturity levels. ### [[System Change Management]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
