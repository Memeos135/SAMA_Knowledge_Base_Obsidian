# Profit Sharing Investment Account (PSIA)

## Connections

### [[Mudarabah Contract]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing loss-allocation and liability under a PSIA, read the Mudarabah definition together with the PSIA definition, because Mudarabah is one of the contract forms the Rules expressly permit for raising PSIA funding. In a Mudarabah PSIA the bank acts as Mudarib and the IAH as Rabb-ul-Mal, and IAHs bear investment losses except where caused by the bank's fraud, negligence, misconduct or breach — the same fault carve-out embedded in the PSIA definition. You would conclude that the contract type selected drives who bears loss and confirm the profit-sharing ratio and fault standard in the underlying agreement.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / Section 5):** "In a Mudarabah arrangement, the bank acts as the Mudarib and the fund providers as the Rabb-ul-Mal, otherwise called Investment Account Holders (IAH)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5):** "Mudarabah: a partnership contract... losses are borne by the capital provider unless the losses are due to misconduct, negligence or breach of contracted terms"

### [[Profit Equalization Reserve  Investment Risk Reserve|Profit Equalization Reserve / Investment Risk Reserve]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating how returns and losses are smoothed within a PSIA, treat PER and IRR as reserves that exist only in service of PSIAs and are appropriated from PSIA/IAH profits. PER is drawn from mudarabah profits to maintain returns to the mudarib and unrestricted IAHs, while IRR is drawn from IAH profit (after the mudarib's share) to cushion future investment losses; the Board must oversee that reserve levels are appropriate and fair to existing and new IAHs. You would conclude reserve governance and the PER/IRR revert-on-write-off/recovery arrangements are integral PSIA obligations, and that these reserves are excluded from regulatory capital.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 7 / Section 6):** "The level of reserves (PER/IRR), to ensure that the level is appropriate and as fair as possible to existing and new IAHs"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6):** "Profit Equalization Reserve (or PER): The amount appropriated out of the mudarabah profits, in order to maintain a certain level of return on investment"

### [[Profit Sharing Investment Accounts Rules]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining whether an arrangement falls within these Rules at all, start from the defined term PSIA, because the document's obligations attach only to accounts meeting that definition. The Rules define a PSIA as a Shari'ah-compliant, bank-managed account under a management agreement sharing profits in a specified ratio with the IAH bearing loss not caused by the bank's fault; SAMA may also direct that a given mudarabah/musharakah/wakalah arrangement be treated, or not treated, as a PSIA. You would conclude scope is definition-driven and confirm each product against the definition rather than its label.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8 / Section 8):** "SAMA may, by way of prudential assessment, direct a bank to treat, or not to treat, an arrangement between the bank and a client... to be a PSIA"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5):** "A Profit Sharing Investment Account (or PSIA): an account that satisfies the following conditions... managed by a bank in accordance with Shari'ah principles"
