# Capital Requirements for CCP Exposures

## Connections

### [[Cross-Product Netting Rules]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank can recognise netting to reduce counterparty credit risk (CCR) exposure for capital purposes, treat the Cross-Product Netting Rules as a precondition that must be satisfied before any CCP/CCR exposure benefit is claimed. The CCR framework (§7.59) only permits inclusion of a transaction in a netting set where it is covered by a legally enforceable netting contract meeting the standardised approach, chapter 9, or the Cross-Product Netting Rules at 7.61–7.71. For a CCR/CCP capital calculation you would therefore verify that the specific netting arrangement clears the legal-enforceability criteria of the referenced rules rather than assuming netting recognition follows automatically.

- **Grounding — related node (Page 616 / §7.59, 7.61):** "covered by a legally enforceable netting contract that meets the applicable requirements ... or the Cross Product Netting Rules set forth 7.61 to 7.71"

### [[Default Fund Exposure Capital Requirement]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a bank's total capital charge for exposures to a central counterparty, do not treat trade exposures and default-fund contributions as one figure — the CCR framework separates them, and the default-fund exposure carries its own capital requirement layered onto the CCP-exposure regime. Both provisions sit within the same SAMA CCR capital framework (SCCR chapters) governing exposures to CCPs. For a capital assessment you would confirm the default-fund contribution is capitalised distinctly from the CCP trade exposure, since omitting it understates the CCP-related charge.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 751):** "Counterparty credit risk: RWA and capital charges according to the counterparty credit risk chapters of the Basel framework (SCCR3 to SCCR10)."

### [[Qualifying CCP (QCCP)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining the capital charge for a bank's exposures to a central counterparty, the QCCP definition is the trigger that decides which treatment applies: the preferential 2% risk weight for trade exposures is available only where the CCP qualifies as a QCCP, and if a CCP ceases to qualify the exposures must be recapitalized under paragraphs 8.41-8.42. Paragraph 8.3 also stresses that QCCP status does not relieve the bank from holding adequate Pillar 2 capital. You should confirm current QCCP status (and SAMA's determination where no qualifying regulator exists) before applying the low risk weight, and re-assess capital promptly if that status lapses.
- **Grounding — this node (SAMA_EN_3487 Page 620 / Para 8.7):** "a risk weight of 2% must be applied to the bank’s trade exposure to the CCP in respect of OTC derivatives"
- **Grounding — related node (SAMA_EN_3487 Page 619 / Para 8.3):** "Regardless of whether a central counterparty (CCP) is classified as a qualifying CCP (QCCP), a bank retains the responsibility to ensure that it maintains adequate capital"
