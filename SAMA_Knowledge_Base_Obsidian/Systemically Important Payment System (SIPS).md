# Systemically Important Payment System (SIPS)

## Connections

### [[Payment Systems]] — `implements` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing what regime a given payment system falls under, treat SIPS as a subset of Payment Systems that carries heightened obligations, not a separate track. The Payment and Payment Services Law gives SAMA power to classify any payment system by importance, and the SIPS definition is triggered by SAMA's discretionary classification based on volume, value, nature and connectivity to settlement/clearing. Practically, you cannot know a system's SIPS-specific duties (Articles 9–10) until SAMA has issued a classification notice; absent that notice, only the general Payment Systems obligations apply.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4):** "Systemically Important Payment System (SIPS): Any payment system classified by SAMA as important pursuant to certain criteria set thereby based on the volume, value, and nature of transactions"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8):** "Setting classifications and requirements for payment systems based on their importance, including classifying any payment system as a SIPS."

### [[Settlement Finality]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When advising on the irrevocability of a payment order, note that Settlement Finality is legally scoped to SIPS rules — it is not a free-standing protection for any payment system. The definition makes finality binding, enforceable and non-reversible 'in accordance with SIPS rules', and Article 10 requires the SIPS-related rules to fix the point of finality (including multi-system execution) and to protect clearing arrangements on member/operator default. Therefore, whether a payment order enjoys finality/enforceability protection depends on whether the system is SIPS-classified and on the specific rules SAMA has set for it; do not assume finality applies to non-SIPS systems.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11 / Art 10):** "The point at which a payment order made by a member becomes final, and the point at which such order becomes subject to settlement finality"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5):** "Settlement Finality: The point at which a payment order is deemed to be duly completed... and may not be reversed, revoked, or modified... in accordance with SIPS rules."
