# Internal Assessment Approach (SEC-IAA)

## Connections

### [[ABCP Conduit]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If a bank holds unrated exposures (liquidity facilities, credit enhancements) to an ABCP programme, the SEC-IAA is the approach that lets it use internal assessments — but only for these conduit-related exposures and only with SAMA approval. Paragraph 21.1 ties SEC-IAA specifically to 'securitization exposures extended to ABCP programmes', requiring at least one approved IRB model and mapping of internal assessments to ECAI equivalents that then feed the SEC-ERBA risk weights. You would conclude that SEC-IAA is not a free-standing approach but a defined route conditioned on the ABCP context; check that the ABCP itself is externally rated (21.2(1)) and that SAMA approval has been obtained before relying on internal assessments.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 306 / 21.1):** "a bank may use its internal assessments of the credit quality of its securitization exposures extended to ABCP programmes"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 269 / 18.96):** "ABCP conduit/conduit – ABCP conduit, being the special purpose vehicle which can issue commercial paper"

### [[Hierarchy of Approaches for Securitization Exposures]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When selecting a securitization approach, treat SEC-IAA as a conditional branch of the hierarchy, not a freely available option. Per 18.44, a bank in Saudi Arabia permitted to use SEC-ERBA may use SEC-IAA for unrated exposures (e.g. liquidity facilities and credit enhancements) to an SA pool within an ABCP programme, and 21.1 requires the bank to notify SAMA and obtain approval, hold at least one approved IRB model, and map internal assessments to ECAI ratings. Conclude that SEC-IAA is only reachable after the hierarchy conditions are satisfied and SAMA approval is granted, so it cannot be assumed as a default for unrated ABCP exposures.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 306 / Art 21.1):** "banks shall notify SAMA of the transactions and seek approval to apply the IAA treatment... provided that the bank has at least one approved IRB model."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 246 / Art 18.44):** "A bank operating in Saudi Arabia that permit to use the SEC-ERBA may use an Internal Assessment Approach (SEC-IAA)... for an unrated securitization exposure... to an SA pool within an ABCP programme."

### [[Securitization Internal Ratings-Based Approach (SEC-IRBA)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank may use the Internal Assessment Approach for unrated ABCP-programme exposures, note SEC-IAA is conditioned on the bank's IRB standing: para 21.1 permits internal assessments 'provided that the bank has at least one approved IRB model,' and internal assessments feed the 'IRB capital requirement' arising from those exposures. SEC-IAA thus sits within the IRB family alongside SEC-IRBA and is only reached where a bank cannot rate the exposure directly (18.44). Conclude that SEC-IAA eligibility hinges on approved IRB model status; without it, the internal-assessment route is closed and the exposure must be handled under the remaining hierarchy methods.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 306 / Art 21.1):** "provided that the bank has at least one approved IRB model... to determine the IRB capital requirement"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Chapter 22):** "Internal ratings-based approach (SEC-IRBA) 311; Definition of KIRB 311"
