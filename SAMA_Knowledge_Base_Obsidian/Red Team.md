# Red Team

## Connections

### [[Cyber Kill Chain Methodology]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a F.E.E.R. exercise met the Framework's methodology expectations, the Cyber Kill Chain is the conceptual attack model the Red Team is expected to follow, distinguishing red teaming (realistic TTP-based simulation) from ordinary penetration testing. The Framework requires the Red Team Evaluation Report to explain the Cyber Kill Chain methodology and the TTPs planned and executed, and to summarise exploited kill chains as attack-vector diagrams. For a decision, this tells you the exercise should be judged against realistic multi-stage attack simulation covering detection and response — a penetration-test-style vulnerability scan would not satisfy the Framework's intent.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 22 / Appendix B (RTER)):** "Explanation of the Cyber Kill Chain methodology and Tactics, Techniques and Procedures that were planned and eventually executed"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / Section 2.4):** "The Cyber Kill Chain provides a conceptual model to describe an attack. The term "chain" reflects the end-to-end process adopted by an attacker"

### [[Financial Entities Ethical Red Teaming Framework (F.E.E.R.)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When identifying who conducts the simulated attack under F.E.E.R., recognise the Red Team (the certified external Red Teaming Provider's staff) as the actor executing the controlled cyber-attack, subject to Green Team approval and contractual controls (NDA, liability, Letter of Authorization). The Framework has the Red Teaming Provider use the latest TTPs to compromise the organization, with its team introduced to the White and Green Teams during procurement. For compliance you should conclude that Red Team engagement requires the LOA/NDA and provider-vetting steps in Appendix A, and that liability for test consequences must be contractually allocated before execution.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11 / 3.5):** "Agreeing on contractual considerations, e.g. Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) clauses, the liability for any consequence flowing from the test, and a Letter of Authorization (LOA)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4):** "The Red Teaming Provider will use the latest attack tactics, techniques and procedures (i.e. TTPs) in an attempt to compromise the Member Organization"

### [[Threat Intelligence]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a Member Organization's obligations under the F.E.E.R. Framework, treat threat intelligence provision as a mandatory input to Red Team activity rather than an optional aid. The scenario phase requires the Green and White Teams to independently supply available Threat Intelligence to the Red Team, which combines it with its own sources to design attack scenarios subject to Green Team approval. A compliance reviewer should therefore check that the intelligence-sharing and combining step actually occurred and was approved, because scenario validity — and the resilience conclusions drawn from the exercise — depend on it.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 7 / 2.2 Required Teams):** "The Green Team ... provides – when applicable – additional or specific threat intelligence for the Financial Sector."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 12 / 4.1 Overview):** "the Green and White Team should independently provide their available Threat Intelligence (TI) to the Red Team. The Red Teaming provider will combine the received Threat Intelligence, with their own"
