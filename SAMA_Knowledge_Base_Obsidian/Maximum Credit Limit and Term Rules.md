# Maximum Credit Limit and Term Rules

## Connections

### [[Licensed Credit Bureau]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When confirming affordability under Article 14, note the credit-bureau step is not optional diligence but an enforceable precondition tied to the defined term 'Licensed Credit Bureau' (a SAMA-licensed credit information company): Article 14(2) requires the Creditor to obtain borrower consent, then pull and examine the borrower's record from one or more Licensed Credit Bureaus to confirm compliance with the 33.33%/25% cap, and Article 14(4) requires refusal of the request if consent is not obtained. Article 14(3) additionally obliges registration and ongoing updating of the borrower's credit information. Conclude that Article 14 compliance is unachievable without using a SAMA-Licensed Credit Bureau specifically — an unlicensed data source will not satisfy the check, and missing consent is a mandatory ground to decline.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 15 / Art 14(2)):** "obtain and examine the credit record of the Borrower from one or more of the Licensed Credit Bureaus, to confirm the Borrower's compliance with the requirement under Article 14(1)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 (Definitions)):** "Licensed Credit Bureau: a credit information company licensed by SAMA to collect and maintain credit information on consumers"

### [[Regulations for Consumer Financing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank's consumer-financing underwriting is compliant, treat Article 14 as the binding affordability core of these Regulations rather than a standalone rule: the parent document scopes application to all Consumer Financing (excluding lease, real estate and margin lending) and defines Amount of Financing by reference to Gross Salary/pension, which Article 14 then converts into the hard 33.33% (25% for pensioners) deduction ceiling, 5-year maximum term, and mandatory credit-bureau check. The document's Article 3(2) expressly ties the financing-amount calculation to 'complying with Article 14(1)', so the two are legally interlocked. Conclude that any exemption or scope limit in the parent (e.g. Article 15(1) securities other than salary/pension deduction) directly narrows when Article 14(1) even applies — so verify the security basis before relying on the deduction cap.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 15 / Art 14(1)):** "the total monthly Repayments or Deductions ... do not exceed 33.33% of the Borrower's Gross Salary ... For retired Borrowers, the deduction limit is 25% of their monthly pension"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / Art 3(2)):** "For the purpose of complying with Article 14(1), the Creditor must calculate the Amount of Financing based on the Borrower's Gross Salary or monthly pension entitlement"
