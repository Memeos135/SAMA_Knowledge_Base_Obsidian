# Operational Risk Capital (ORC)

## Connections

### [[Business Indicator Component (BIC)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When you assess a bank's minimum capital compliance, the BIC is not a standalone charge — it is one of two multiplicands producing the ORC (ORC = BIC × ILM). This matters because a bank that fails the loss-data standards must hold capital at least equal to 100% of the BIC, effectively flooring ORC at the BIC even where internal losses are low. Confirm both which figure (BIC vs BIC×ILM) applies and whether SAMA has imposed an ILM greater than 1 before concluding on the capital requirement.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / section 1 Introduction):** "the revised standardized approach as the sole approach for calculating operational risk capital requirements"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / Art 7.1):** "The Banks must calculate minimum ORC requirements ... by multiplying the BIC and the ILM: ORC = BIC x ILM"

### [[Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When checking a bank's ORC, treat the ILM as the mechanism by which internal loss experience adjusts the capital charge — ORC = BIC × ILM. Critically, SAMA retains discretion to require an ILM greater than 1 where a bank fails the loss-data standards, and such use of multipliers must be disclosed under Pillar 3. So a bank's ORC is not purely formulaic: confirm whether SAMA has imposed a supervisory ILM override before concluding the capital figure is correct.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / section 1):** "calculating operational risk capital requirements ... enhancing the resilience and soundness of Saudi Arabia's banking system"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / Art 7.4.1):** "SAMA may require the bank to apply an ILM which is greater than 1 ... must be publicly disclosed in Pillar 3"
