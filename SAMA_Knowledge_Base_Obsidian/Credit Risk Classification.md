# Credit Risk Classification

## Connections

### [[Finance Companies Control Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining the legal authority and enforceability of the credit-risk classification and provisioning rules for a finance company, read the Rules as subordinate implementing instruments issued under the Finance Companies Control Law and its Implementing Regulation. SAMA expressly grounds the Rules in the Law's Article 13 (provision for contingent operation losses) and Article 62 of the Implementing Regulation (provisions per international accounting standards, plus SAMA's power to demand additional provisions). Conclude that the classification/provisioning requirements carry statutory force and that SAMA can impose extra provisions beyond IFRS 9 — a finance company cannot treat these Rules as mere guidance.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / 1.1 Introduction):** "SAMA issued these rules in exercise of the powers vested upon it under Finance Companies Control Law ... and in pursuance of the Implementing Regulation"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Art 13 / Art 62):** "The finance company must set provisions for contingent losses and risks ... SAMA may require the finance company to make one or more additional provisions"

### [[IFRS 9]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining how a finance company must classify exposures, you cannot read SAMA's regulatory categories in isolation from IFRS 9 — the Rules explicitly map IFRS 9 Stages 1/2/3 to SAMA's regulatory categories (Regular, Special Monitoring, Substandard, Doubtful, Loss). SAMA states the mapping is 'for the naming convention only,' meaning the underlying measurement (SICR, ECL) is driven by IFRS 9, while the category labels serve SAMA's conservatism and reporting expectations. For compliance you should conclude that IFRS 9 governs the substance of staging/measurement and that the superseded Provisions Guidelines definitions (Circular 381000046342) must no longer be used.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11 / Section 3):** "SAMA has provided mapping of IFRS 9 stages to regulatory categories (for the naming convention only keeping in view conservatism of IFRS 9)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6):** "Measures the estimated likelihood of default over a time horizon as prescribed by IFRS 9."

### [[Stage 123 Exposure Classification|Stage 1/2/3 Exposure Classification]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When applying the classification Rules, treat the Stage 1/2/3 framework as the operative mechanism of the credit risk classification requirement, not a separate concept — the Rules require companies to classify each exposure into one of three stages based on original credit risk and change since origination. Note the Rules further subdivide Stages 2 and 3 (2A/2B, 3A/3B) 'for regulatory reporting purposes only and not for accounting purposes' and impose specific cure-period thresholds (e.g. 90/60 days for restaging to Stage 1). For compliance, verify staging and restaging decisions against these documented conditions, and recognise the regulatory sub-tiers may diverge from pure accounting stages.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11 / Section 3):** "Finance companies will be required to classify exposures ... in one of three stages or regulatory categories based on their original credit risk at origination and the change in credit risk"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 7 / Page 12):** "There are 2 categories in Stage 2 exposures as defined in these rules for regulatory reporting purposes only and not for accounting purposes, i.e. Stage 2A and Stage 2B."
