# Cyber Security Risk Management and Compliance

## Connections

### [[(Inter)national Industry Standards]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping compliance obligations, treat mandatory (inter)national industry standards (3.2.3) as a discrete enforceable requirement sitting inside the Risk Management and Compliance domain (3.2), not as optional best practice. The framework states the Member Organization 'should comply with' PCI-DSS, EMV, and the SWIFT Customer Security Controls Framework, and these obligations are audited under the domain's periodic review-and-audit regime. A reviewer should confirm applicability of each named standard to the organization's activities (e.g. card and payment operations) and conclude that non-compliance is a compliance-domain breach regardless of general risk posture.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / 3.2):** "The compliance with the cyber security controls should be subject to periodic review and audit."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 22 / 3.2.3):** "The Member Organization should comply with: a. Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI-DSS); b. EMV ...; c. SWIFT Customer Security Controls Framework"

### [[Cyber Security Risk Management Process]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating risk-management obligations, treat the Cyber Security Risk Management Process (3.2.1) as the mandatory core mechanism of the Risk Management and Compliance domain (3.2), comprising identification, analysis, response, and monitoring/review. The framework requires this process to be defined, approved, implemented, and aligned with the enterprise risk management process, and expressly binds accepted risks to SAMA regulations ('does not contradict SAMA regulations'). A reviewer should conclude that an unapproved, undocumented, or ERM-misaligned process is a domain-level failure, and should check that risk-acceptance sign-offs stay within risk appetite and remain reported to the cyber security committee.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 21):** "the accepted cyber security risk ... does not contradict SAMA regulations."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 19 / 3.2.1):** "A cyber security risk management process should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organization's enterprise risk management process."

### [[Regulatory Compliance]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When identifying compliance duties, read Regulatory Compliance (3.2.2) as a required sub-process within the Risk Management and Compliance domain (3.2), obligating the organization to identify, communicate and comply with the cyber security implications of relevant regulations. Because this process must be run periodically or when new regulatory requirements take effect and must feed updates back into policies and standards, it operationalises the domain's compliance mandate. A reviewer should conclude that failure to track changing SAMA or other regulatory requirements is itself a compliance-domain deficiency, and should check that policy updates are triggered by regulatory change.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / 3.2):** "The compliance with the cyber security controls should be subject to periodic review and audit."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 22 / 3.2.2):** "A process should be established ... to identify, communicate and comply with the cyber security implications of relevant regulations."

### [[SAMA Cyber Security Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a Member Organization's obligations under the SAMA Cyber Security Framework, treat the risk-management-and-compliance domain as a mandatory, enforceable component of the Framework itself, not an optional add-on. The Framework (Section 3.2) requires that a cyber security risk management process be defined, approved and implemented, aligned with enterprise risk management, and that compliance with cyber security controls be subject to periodic review and audit; accepted risks must not contradict SAMA regulations. Conclude that Framework maturity assessments and audits will test whether the risk identification/analysis/response/monitoring cycle exists and whether residual-risk acceptance stays inside approved limits and SAMA rules.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / 3.2):** "The compliance with the cyber security controls should be subject to periodic review and audit."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 21 / Control considerations):** "the accepted cyber security risk does not contradict SAMA regulations."
