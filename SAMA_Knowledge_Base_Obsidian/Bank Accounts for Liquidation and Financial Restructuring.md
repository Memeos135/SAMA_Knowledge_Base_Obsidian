# Bank Accounts for Liquidation and Financial Restructuring

## Connections

### [[Bankruptcy Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When onboarding or freezing accounts tied to insolvency, treat the Bankruptcy Law as the statutory trigger that defines both the freeze obligation and the permitted liquidation-account regime — the rules require the bank to stop dealings and freeze balances upon official notice of liquidation/administrative liquidation under the Bankruptcy Law, and separately permit a dedicated liquidation account operated by the court-appointed officeholder. This link tells you the account rules are downstream of a court order and statutory procedure, not the bank's discretion. You would conclude the bank must verify the court order, the officeholder's appointment/powers, and the statutory validity period before operating the account.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 89-90 / 300.1.7):** "Commencement of any liquidation procedures under the provisions of Bankruptcy Law ... The bank shall receive the court order ... Appointing one or more bankruptcy officeholder"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 24 / 10.2.1):** "Starting any liquidation or administrative liquidation procedures under the Bankruptcy Law for the account holder"

### [[Companies Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a company is being wound up outside insolvency, the Companies Law provides the alternative legal basis for a liquidation account — the rules expressly permit the account 'for liquidation under the Companies Law' and tie it to the termination reasons stated in Article 16 of that law, requiring either a court order or a partners'/general assembly decision plus appointment of a liquidator. This link tells you two distinct statutory tracks feed the same account category (Bankruptcy Law vs Companies Law), each with its own trigger documents. You would conclude that voluntary/corporate liquidation is validated by the corporate decision and liquidator appointment, not necessarily a court insolvency order.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 89 / 300.1.7):** "for liquidation under the Companies Law. The following conditions and documents shall be met and provided"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 90 / 300.1.7(II)):** "Liquidation for the termination reasons stated in Article 16 of Companies Law ... In case of a voluntary liquidation by partners, the decision issued by the partners or general assembly"

### [[Rules for Bank Accounts]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a customer enters bankruptcy or company liquidation, consult Rule 300.1.7 (Bank Accounts for Liquidation and Financial Restructuring) as the specific procedure that governs who may open and operate the account and what court/appointment documents are required. This link matters because control shifts from the account holder to a liquidation officeholder or bankruptcy committee under court order pursuant to the Bankruptcy Law or Companies Law, and the account must be specifically named as a 'Liquidation Account'. Conclude that ordinary authorised-signatory and operation rules are displaced during liquidation, and verify the court order, officeholder identity and mandated account naming before permitting transactions.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 89 / 300.1.7):** "The bank may open bank accounts for liquidation, for depositing the proceeds of the sale of the bankruptcy assets covering the debtor's debt."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Table of Contents):** "300.1.7 Bank accounts for liquidation and financial restructuring 64"
