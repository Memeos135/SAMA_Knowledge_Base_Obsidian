# Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)

## Connections

### [[Beneficial Owner]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether EDD applies to a legal-person customer, do not confine PEP screening to the named account holder — the two concepts appear deliberately linked because a PEP may sit behind the customer as beneficial owner. The Guide's PEP provision expressly directs the institution to determine 'if a customer or beneficial owner is a PEP,' while the beneficial-owner section requires identifying the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the customer (the 25% control test and fallback measures). The link is a strong lead but the corpus does not spell out a single cross-reference article joining them, so verify the primary EDD/BO text: you would conclude that PEP determination must run through to identified beneficial owners, not stop at the front-facing customer.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 39 / Section 4.9):** "The financial institution shall use appropriate tools and measures to determine if a customer or beneficial owner is a PEP, whether that person is foreign or local."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 32 / Section 3.B):** "the financial institution shall be certain of the identity of the beneficial owner or any person controlling the business relationship... a natural person who owns or controls the legal person directly or indirectly."
- **Caveat:** Relation is INFERRED — both concepts co-appear ('customer or beneficial owner is a PEP') but no explicit cross-referencing article is quoted; confirm in the primary EDD and beneficial-owner text before relying on it.

### [[Enhanced Due Diligence Measures]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping when EDD is mandatory rather than discretionary, treat PEP status as an automatic trigger that sits inside the broader EDD regime, not as a separate obligation. The Guide places PEPs under Section 4 (Enhanced Due Diligence Measures), and grounds it in Article (8) of the AML Law and its Implementing Regulations, which require the institution to identify PEPs and 'implement enhanced due diligence measures regarding them.' Practically, you conclude that identifying a customer or beneficial owner as a PEP in a high-risk relationship compels the full EDD package (including documentation per Paragraph 6.1), extended to family members and close associates — you cannot apply ordinary CDD to a PEP relationship.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 39 / Section 4.B):** "Article (8) of the Anti-Money Laundering Law and its Implementing Regulations require the financial institution to develop internal procedures... to identify PEPs and implement enhanced due diligence measures regarding them."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 39 / Section 4):** "In cases of high-risk business relationships with PEPs, the financial institution shall apply enhanced due diligence measures and apply the same to all types of PEPs, their family members, and persons close to those PEPs."
