# Implementation Rules for Banking Control Law (SAMA 1429)

## Connections

### [[Bank Periodic Data Reporting]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a bank's ongoing reporting duties, treat the periodic-data obligations as a direct, enforceable output of Article (17) of the Banking Control Law as operationalised by these Implementation Rules — not optional practice. The Rules specify the exact statements and frequencies (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual) banks must submit to SAMA, plus auditor-provided data on request. You should therefore scope reporting obligations by reference to the frequency schedule in these Rules and treat late, incomplete, or withheld submissions as a compliance breach exposed to Article (22) penalties.

### [[Banking Control Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Read this document as subordinate implementing legislation: the Implementation Rules derive their authority entirely from the Banking Control Law (Royal Decree M/5) and exist to operationalise specific articles (12, 16, 17, 18, 22). When interpreting any obligation in the Rules, you should anchor it to the parent article it implements, because the Rules cannot exceed or contradict the Law. For compliance decisions this means the Law defines the outer limits of duties and SAMA's powers, while the Rules supply the operative detail.

### [[SAMA Supervision and Inspection]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When preparing for a SAMA inspection, treat cooperation as a legally mandated, non-discretionary duty: these Implementation Rules operationalise Article (18) by expressly prohibiting refusal of access to records, hiding information, or ignoring inspection recommendations. Non-cooperation is itself an enumerated violation, and Article (22) (implemented in the same Rules) authorises SAMA to apply procedures and penalties. You should therefore conclude that obstruction or concealment during inspection is independently sanctionable, separate from any underlying substantive breach.
