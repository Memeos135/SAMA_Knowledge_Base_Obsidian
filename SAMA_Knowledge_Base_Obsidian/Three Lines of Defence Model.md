# Three Lines of Defence Model

## Connections

### [[Independence and Objectivity of Audit Unit]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank's internal audit function satisfies SAMA's Internal Audit Principles, treat independence/objectivity and the three-lines model as a single obligation set, not separate ideas: the Principles position the audit unit as the third line of defence — distinct from and reviewing the first and second lines — precisely so that its assurance is independent and objective. Because the same instrument defines the unit's independence as flowing from its third-line role (a supervisory tool for the board/management, 'not a substitute for it'), a compliance reviewer should verify that reporting lines, mandate and staffing keep audit structurally separated from operational and risk/control (second-line) functions. Conclude that a firm cannot claim compliance with the independence principle while blurring audit into second-line control activities.

### [[Internal Audit Principles for Local Banks]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping a bank's governance and control responsibilities, use these Principles to place internal audit as the third line of defence — a supervisory/assurance tool for the bank's management, not a substitute for the first and second lines. The introduction expressly describes the unit's role 'as a third line of defence' complementing the first and second lines, and a dedicated Principle (8) governs the unit's relationship with second-line functions and external auditors. Conclude that first-line (business/controls) and second-line (risk/compliance) responsibilities remain with those functions; internal audit's assurance does not discharge them, and you should not treat audit coverage as replacing the second line's own monitoring.

### [[Risk-Based Internal Audit Plan]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating the scope of a bank's internal audit plan, read the risk-based audit plan requirement together with the three-lines model, because in these Principles the audit unit's third-line role is what defines what its risk-based plan must cover — it independently assesses the governance, risk management and controls operated by the first and second lines. The same document sets out a 'risk-based internal audit plan' and a risk-assessment methodology as unit requirements, tied to that third-line assurance mandate. A reviewer should therefore check that the plan's coverage universe and cycle reflect the risks in the lines being audited, and should not accept a plan that omits material first/second-line activities.
