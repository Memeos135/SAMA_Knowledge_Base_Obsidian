# Internal Models Method (IMM)

## Connections

### [[Alpha Multiplier]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When reviewing a bank's IMM EAD figure, check the alpha parameter as a distinct supervisory lever, because IMM does not merely output an exposure — it multiplies Effective EPE by alpha to produce regulatory EAD. Alpha is fixed at 1.4 by default, but SAMA may impose a higher value for concentrated or wrong-way-risk exposures, and internal alpha estimates require SAMA approval and are floored at 1.2. You would therefore confirm which alpha applies and whether any own-estimate was formally approved before relying on the bank's IMM capital number.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 48 / 7.1):** "A bank that wishes to adopt an internal models method to measure exposure or exposure at default (EAD) for regulatory capital purposes must seek SAMA approval."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 51 / 7.14–7.16):** "Alpha (α) is set equal to 1.4. SAMA may require a higher alpha... Banks should seek approval from SAMA to compute internal estimates of alpha subject to a floor of 1.2"

### [[Backtesting  Model Validation|Backtesting / Model Validation]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When judging whether a bank's IMM approval is defensible, treat backtesting and ongoing model validation as an integral, mandatory condition of IMM eligibility rather than good practice. The IMM chapter conditions approval on meeting requirements 7.6–7.60, and those provisions require pre-approval backtesting over multiple horizons, ongoing validation, and permit SAMA to demand corrective action or additional capital where realized exposures diverge from forecasts. You would therefore verify the validation record exists and is current, since deficient backtesting can justify SAMA imposing higher capital or withdrawing IMM reliance.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 59 / 7.35):** "The bank must satisfy SAMA that, in addition to meeting the operational requirements identified in 7.36 to 7.60 below, it adheres to sound practices for CCR management"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 57–58 / 7.33, (6)-(7)):** "A bank must carry out backtesting using historical data... Significant differences between realized exposures and the forecast distribution... SAMA may require additional capital"

### [[Cross-Product Netting Rules]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank may net across SFTs and OTC derivatives for capital purposes, note that cross-product netting is available only within IMM, not as a general entitlement. The rules state expressly that the Cross-Product Netting Rules (7.61–7.71) apply to netting across SFTs, or across SFTs and OTC derivatives, for regulatory capital computation under IMM, and require a written bilateral arrangement plus reasoned legal opinions of enforceability across all relevant jurisdictions. You would therefore confirm the bank holds IMM approval and the requisite legal opinions before accepting any cross-product netting benefit in its capital figures.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 48 / 7.2):** "A bank may also choose to adopt an internal models method to measure counterparty credit risk (CCR)... to only OTC derivatives, to only SFTs, or to both, subject to the appropriate recognition of netting specified in 7.61 to 7.71."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 67 / 7.61):** "The Cross-Product Netting Rules apply specifically to netting across SFTs, or to netting across both SFTs and OTC derivatives, for purposes of regulatory capital computation under IMM."

### [[Effective Expected Positive Exposure (Effective EPE)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating whether a bank may use its internal model to compute counterparty exposure, read Effective EPE as the mandatory output the IMM must generate: paragraph 7.36 requires the distribution of exposures generated by the internal model used to calculate effective EPE to be integrated into day-to-day CCR management, and paragraph 7.6 measures EAD under the IMM at netting-set level. This means IMM approval hinges on the credibility and use of the Effective EPE calculation, not merely on possessing a model. You should check that any bank claiming IMM treatment demonstrates the track record and 'use test' tied to its Effective EPE distributions before relying on its reduced capital figures.
- **Grounding — this node (SAMA_EN_4283 Page 48 / Para 7.1):** "A bank that wishes to adopt an internal models method to measure exposure or exposure at default (EAD)... must seek SAMA approval"
- **Grounding — related node (SAMA_EN_4283 Page 59 / Para 7.36):** "The distribution of exposures generated by the internal model used to calculate effective EPE must be closely integrated into the day-to-day CCR management process"

### [[Minimum Capital Requirements for CCR and CVA]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When advising on whether a bank may use its own internal exposure models for CCR, note that IMM is a permission-gated method inside this framework, not a default option — a bank must obtain SAMA approval, meet the qualifying standards (7.6–7.60), and generally apply IMM to all exposures subject to CCR except long settlement transactions. Reversion to the standardized approach is only allowed under exceptional circumstances or for immaterial exposures and must not enable regulatory-capital arbitrage. Conclude that IMM use should be verified against an actual SAMA approval and the use-test/track-record conditions before relying on internally-modelled EAD.

### [[Netting Set]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When checking IMM exposure figures, confirm the unit of measurement is the netting set as defined in the framework, because IMM measures CCR exposure or EAD at netting-set level, not per trade. Paragraph 7.6 states EAD is measured at the netting set defined in Chapter 4 and 7.61–7.71, so an incorrectly constituted netting set (or improper aggregation of collateral and margin agreements) directly distorts the capital outcome. You would therefore verify that trades are validly grouped into legally enforceable netting sets before relying on the aggregated IMM EAD.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 49 / 7.6):** "CCR exposure or EAD is measured at the level of the netting set as defined in Chapter 4 of this framework and 7.61 to 7.71 of this framework."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 47 / 6.79):** "Where a single margin agreement applies to several netting sets... collateral will be exchanged based on mark-to-market values that are netted across all transactions"

### [[Regulatory CVA Calculation]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping capital obligations, keep CCR/IMM exposure measurement distinct from the CVA capital requirement: they are separate charges applying to the same covered transactions, and the CVA framework uses 'regulatory CVA' (excluding the bank's own default) computed per counterparty. Chapter 11 requires the CVA capital requirement to be calculated by all banks in covered transactions in both banking and trading books, independent of the exposure method used for CCR. Conclude that IMM approval for CCR does not discharge CVA obligations; you must separately calculate regulatory CVA (via BA-CVA by default, or SA-CVA with SAMA approval) for each counterparty with a covered position.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 48 / 7.1):** "A bank that wishes to adopt an internal models method to measure exposure or exposure at default (EAD) for regulatory capital purposes must seek SAMA approval."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 97 / 11.31):** "A bank must calculate regulatory CVA for each counterparty with which it has at least one covered position for the purpose of the CVA risk capital requirements."

### [[Saudi Central Bank (SAMA)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether your bank can use internal models to measure CCR exposure/EAD for capital purposes, treat this as a supervisory gating requirement, not a self-selected option: the IMM cannot be used without prior SAMA approval, and SAMA also sets the conditions for reversion and continued eligibility. This link is grounded in the requirement that a bank 'must seek SAMA approval' and 'must satisfy SAMA' on sound CCR practices before applying models for capital adequacy. Conclude that IMM eligibility, scope, phase-in plans and any reversion to the standardized approach are all conditional on SAMA's determination, so you cannot rely on internal model outputs for regulatory capital until that approval is documented.

### [[Stress Testing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** If your bank uses or seeks to use the IMM, do not treat stress testing as a separable exercise: it is an embedded qualifying condition of IMM approval, so failure to run the prescribed CCR stress program undermines the model's regulatory acceptability. The framework places the stress-testing mandate (7.46) inside the IMM operational requirements (7.36–7.60) that a bank 'must meet all' to adopt the method, requiring monthly single-factor and at-least-quarterly multifactor and joint exposure/creditworthiness stress testing with senior-management ownership. Conclude that an IMM firm must be able to evidence the full stress-testing elements set out here; gaps in that program are a direct IMM compliance deficiency, not merely a risk-management shortfall.

### [[Wrong-Way Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating IMM eligibility, treat wrong-way risk identification and monitoring as a mandatory qualifying element rather than optional analytics: it sits within the 7.36–7.60 operational requirements the bank 'must meet all' of to adopt the method. Provision 7.47 obliges banks to identify general wrong-way risk exposures, design stress/scenario testing around risk factors positively correlated with counterparty creditworthiness, monitor by product/region/industry, and report to senior management and the Board. Conclude that an IMM firm must demonstrate specific wrong-way risk (7.48) and general wrong-way risk governance to SAMA; absence of this analysis is a defect in the model's qualifying standards.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 59 / 7.36–7.60):** "in addition to meeting the operational requirements identified in 7.36 to 7.60 below, it adheres to sound practices for CCR management."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 63 / 7.47):** "Banks must identify exposures that give rise to a greater degree of general wrong-way risk. Stress testing and scenario analyses must be designed to identify risk factors that are positively correlated with counterparty credit worthiness."
