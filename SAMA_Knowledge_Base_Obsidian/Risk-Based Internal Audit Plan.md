# Risk-Based Internal Audit Plan

## Connections

### [[Internal Audit Principles for Local Banks]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating a local bank's audit coverage, note that SAMA requires the internal audit plan to be risk-based rather than a fixed cyclical checklist: the Principles set out, within the unit's organisation and responsibilities, both a 'risk assessment methodology' and a 'risk-based internal audit plan.' This means the plan's scope and prioritisation must flow from the unit's own risk assessment, and the list of auditable units and the audit cycle sit alongside it. Conclude that when assessing compliance you should check that the audit plan is demonstrably derived from a risk assessment and covers the entities subject to audit, since a purely calendar-driven plan would not meet the framework.

### [[Three Lines of Defence Model]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating the scope of a bank's internal audit plan, read the risk-based audit plan requirement together with the three-lines model, because in these Principles the audit unit's third-line role is what defines what its risk-based plan must cover — it independently assesses the governance, risk management and controls operated by the first and second lines. The same document sets out a 'risk-based internal audit plan' and a risk-assessment methodology as unit requirements, tied to that third-line assurance mandate. A reviewer should therefore check that the plan's coverage universe and cycle reflect the risks in the lines being audited, and should not accept a plan that omits material first/second-line activities.
