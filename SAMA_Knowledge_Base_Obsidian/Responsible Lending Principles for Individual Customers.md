The document defines Deductible Ratio as the ratio of the consumer's monthly credit obligations to total monthly income and then sets binding ceilings by income band (e.g. non-real-estate obligations capped at 45%, total obligations at 55%/65% for Housing/REDF beneficiaries). So to test compliance, apply the specific income-band caps in paragraphs 15–17 using the defined inputs (Monthly Credit Obligations, Gross Salary, Total Monthly Income) rather than a general affordability judgment.

### [[Real Estate Finance Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When advising a real estate finance entity on borrower onboarding and affordability, note that the Responsible Lending Principles overlap with the real estate finance regime: the Principles expressly apply to 'all credit products and programs designed for individuals, including...real estate finance,' so a real estate finance entity that is a bank or finance company falls within both regimes. The two use different but overlapping defined terms — the Real Estate Finance Law/Regulation speaks of 'Borrower' (a natural person obtaining finance) and 'Consumer' (any person targeted by services), while the Principles define 'Consumer' as an individual who obtains or applies for finance directed at them. Conclude that real estate lending must satisfy the Responsible Lending creditworthiness and repayment-capacity duties in addition to the Real Estate Finance Law requirements; do not treat the Law's contract rules as the full compliance set.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Ch II(3)):** "The principles apply to all creditors and finance activities directed at consumers... including...real estate finance."
