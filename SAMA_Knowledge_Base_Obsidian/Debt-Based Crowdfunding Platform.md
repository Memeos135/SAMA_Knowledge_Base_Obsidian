# Debt-Based Crowdfunding Platform

## Connections

### [[Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing DBC obligations, treat the Platform not as mere technology but as the defined regulated channel through which the Company must discharge disclosure, consent and fund-handling duties. Article 1 defines the Platform as the digital means 'used and run by a Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company', and Article 28 attaches operative duties to it (clarify business nature, publish risk information, obtain electronic acknowledgment before use). The consequence: obligations expressed as 'on the Platform' or 'before using the Platform' are enforceable Company duties, so compliance review of the Company necessarily includes what is displayed and consented-to via the Platform.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Art 1 (2.4)):** "Debt-Based Crowdfunding Platform: A web-based platform or any other digital means... used and run by a Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 17 / Art 28):** "By using the Platform, the Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company shall clarify the nature of the business offered and publish awareness-raising information"

### [[Institutional Beneficiary]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When considering how the Institutional Beneficiary interacts with the regime, note that the Platform is the mandatory disclosure and diligence venue: the Company must display defined Institutional Beneficiary information on it and must perform Institutional Beneficiary due diligence under AML/CTF law before that display. Article 1 defines the Institutional Beneficiary by reference to obtaining financing 'through a Debt-Based Crowdfunding Platform', and Articles 24/28 require project details, credit-rating standards and a 'not advice' statement on the Platform. The link is definitional and operational — verify that the Beneficiary's KYB/CDD and mandated disclosures are complete before the project is presented, since the Platform display is where the disclosure duty crystallises.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / Art 28):** "displaying the project on the Platform shall not be considered as advice to provide financing to the Institutional Beneficiary"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 13 / Art 24):** "set clear policies regarding the Institutional Beneficiary due diligence in accordance with the Anti-Money Laundering Law and the Law of Combating Crimes of Terrorism and its Financing"

### [[Participant]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When analysing Participant protections, read the Platform as the point at which investment limits, risk disclosure and consent obligations bind: the Participant is defined as one who finances 'through a Debt-Based Crowdfunding Company', and the Platform is where Article 26 credit limits (e.g. SAR 250,000 aggregate per Participant, 25% cap, Eligible Participant carve-out) and Article 28 risk acknowledgment operate. Conclude that Participant-facing limits and consents are Company duties executed via the Platform — check both the quantitative caps and the pre-use acknowledgment when assessing consumer/investor-protection compliance.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 17 / Art 28):** "obtain an electronic acknowledgment from the Participants through a (Pop-Up Window), which includes their acknowledgment of the risks"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / Art 26):** "The existing financing for each Participant shall not exceed SAR 250,000... Eligible Participants shall be excluded"
