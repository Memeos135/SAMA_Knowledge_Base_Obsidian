# Specialized Lending (SL)

## Connections

### [[Commodities Finance (CF)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When treating commodities finance for capital purposes, apply the specialized lending rules — commodities finance (CF) is defined as one of the three SL subcategories. Under the standardised approach, where no issue-specific external rating exists CF is risk-weighted at 100%; under the IRB slotting approach CF is one of the exposure types (PF, OF, CF, IPRE) mapped to five supervisory categories. Conclude that CF cannot be treated under general corporate rules by default: check whether an issue-specific rating is available (issuer ratings are not permitted for SL) and, absent qualifying PD estimation, apply supervisory slotting.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 145 / 13.2):** "For project finance (PF), object finance (OF), commodities finance (CF) and income producing real estate (IPRE) exposures... map their internal grades to five supervisory categories"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 33 / 7.42(3)):** "Commodities finance Refers to short-term lending to finance reserves, inventories, or receivables of exchange-traded commodities... repaid from the proceeds of the sale of the commodity"

### [[Corporate Exposures]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When classifying a corporate credit exposure, do not treat specialized lending as a separate asset class — it is expressly a subcategory of the corporate exposure class. Paragraph 7.37 lists 'Specialized lending exposures, as defined in paragraph 7.41' as one of the corporate subcategories, and specialized lending carries distinct rules (e.g. issue-specific external ratings only, issuer ratings prohibited). Conclude that an exposure meeting the SL definition must be routed to the SL treatment even though it sits within the corporate class, so check whether a corporate exposure qualifies as project/object/commodities finance before applying the general corporate risk weights.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 33 / 7.42):** "Exposures described in paragraph 7.41 will be classified in one of the following three subcategories of specialized lending"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 30 / 7.37):** "The corporate exposure class differentiates between the following subcategories: 1. General corporate exposures; 2. Specialized lending exposures, as defined in paragraph 7.41"

### [[High-Volatility Commercial Real Estate (HVCRE)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When capital-treating high-volatility commercial real estate (HVCRE), treat it as a specialized lending exposure with its own dedicated slotting treatment distinct from other SL types. The framework defines HVCRE as financing of commercial real estate exhibiting higher loss-rate volatility than other SL, and lists separate SL slotting risk weights for HVCRE alongside PF/OF/CF/IPRE. Conclude that HVCRE (including ADC financing of qualifying property types categorised by SAMA) must not be defaulted into the general IPRE or corporate slotting weights — verify whether an exposure meets the HVCRE definition, since that classification changes the applicable supervisory slotting weights.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 10 / Ch.13):** "Risk weights for specialized lending (PF, OF, CF and IPRE)... Risk weights for specialized lending (HVCRE)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 102 / 10.15):** "HVCRE lending is the financing of commercial real estate that exhibits higher loss rate volatility (i.e. higher asset correlation) compared to other types of SL."

### [[Income-Producing Real Estate (IPRE)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining the capital treatment of an income-producing real estate exposure, treat IPRE as a defined sub-class of the Specialized Lending regime rather than as a generic corporate or real estate loan. The SL framework (para 10.8) lists IPRE among the five SL sub-classes, and IPRE is distinguished by repayment depending 'primarily on the cash flows generated by the asset'; that classification pulls the exposure into the SL risk-weight and slotting rules (chapter 13, para 13.2). Conclude that an IPRE exposure inherits SL treatment — including the supervisory slotting mapping where PD estimation requirements are not met — so verify which SL sub-class applies before assigning risk weights.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 100 / 10.8):** "The five sub-classes of SL are project finance (PF), object finance (OF), commodities finance (CF), income-producing real estate (IPRE) lending, and high-volatility commercial real estate (HVCRE) lending."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 102 / 10.14):** "IPRE lending refers to a method of providing funding to real estate ... where the prospects for repayment and recovery on the exposure depend primarily on the cash flows generated by the asset."

### [[Object Finance (OF)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When classifying financing for equipment such as ships, aircraft or railcars, treat Object Finance as a defined sub-class of Specialized Lending, not as ordinary secured corporate lending. Para 10.8 lists OF as one of the five SL sub-classes, and where issue-specific external ratings are unavailable, OF exposures are risk-weighted at 100% (para 7.44) or mapped through supervisory slotting (para 13.2). Conclude that OF exposures follow the SL risk-weight rules — check whether an issue-specific rating exists and whether PD-estimation requirements are met before selecting the applicable weight.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 100 / 10.8):** "The five sub-classes of SL are project finance (PF), object finance (OF), commodities finance (CF), income-producing real estate (IPRE) lending, and high-volatility commercial real estate (HVCRE) lending."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 33 / 7.42(2)):** "Object finance ... funding the acquisition of equipment (e.g. ships, aircraft, satellites, railcars, and fleets) where the repayment of the loan is dependent on the cash flows generated by the specific assets."

### [[Project Finance (PF)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When setting the capital treatment for project finance, treat PF as a defined sub-class of Specialized Lending so that SL-specific risk weights and phase-based adjustments apply. Para 10.8 lists PF as one of the five SL sub-classes; para 7.44 then imposes distinct weights (130% pre-operational, 100% operational, 80% for high-quality operational-phase PF meeting para 7.45 conditions). Conclude that PF exposures cannot be weighted like generic corporate exposures — verify the project's operational phase and the para 7.45 high-quality conditions before applying a reduced weight.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 100 / 10.8):** "The five sub-classes of SL are project finance (PF), object finance (OF), commodities finance (CF), income-producing real estate (IPRE) lending, and high-volatility commercial real estate (HVCRE) lending."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 34 / 7.44(2)):** "Project finance exposures will be risk-weighted at 130% during the pre-operational phase and 100% during the operational phase ... deemed to be high quality ... will be risk weighted at 80%."

### [[Standardized Approach]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When classifying specialized lending exposures (project, object, commodities finance, IPRE/HVCRE), note that SL is treated under the IRB supervisory-slotting route, which is distinct from — and an alternative to — the Standardized Approach for credit risk. Both are chapters of the same SAMA credit-risk capital framework, and reporting separates 'standardised approach' (SCRE5–9) from the 'supervisory slotting approach' (SCRE13). You would conclude that SL exposures using slotting are not captured in standardized-approach RWA lines, so verify whether a given SL exposure meets IRB/PD estimation requirements or falls back to another treatment before selecting the risk weights.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 145 / 13.2):** "banks that do not meet the requirements for the estimation of probability of default (PD)...will be required to map their internal grades to five supervisory categories"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 751):** "Of which: supervisory slotting approach: RWA and capital requirements according to the supervisory slotting approach (as specified in SCRE13)."

### [[Supervisory Slotting Approach]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a bank cannot meet the PD-estimation requirements of the corporate IRB approach for its SL exposures, the Supervisory Slotting Approach is the mandatory fallback for calculating RWA and expected losses. Chapter 13 (para 13.1–13.2) requires banks not meeting PD-estimation requirements to map internal grades to five supervisory categories each carrying a specific risk weight, and the regulatory reporting template (OV1 row 4) recognizes 'supervisory slotting approach' as a distinct RWA basis under SCRE13. Conclude that SL exposures fall under slotting where own-PD estimates are not qualified — confirm which SL sub-classes (PF, OF, CF, IPRE, HVCRE) are being slotted and apply the corresponding supervisory category weights.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 145 / 13.2):** "banks that do not meet the requirements for the estimation of probability of default (PD) ... will be required to map their internal grades to five supervisory categories, each ... associated with a specific risk weight."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 751 / OV1 row 4):** "Of which: supervisory slotting approach: RWA and capital requirements according to the supervisory slotting approach (as specified in SCRE13)."
