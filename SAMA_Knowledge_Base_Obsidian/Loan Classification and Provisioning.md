# Loan Classification and Provisioning

## Connections

### [[Appendix C Asset Quality Risk Classification|Appendix C: Asset Quality Risk Classification]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining how a DTFC must classify a loan, do not rely on the Chapter 6 narrative alone — paragraph 36 mandates classification 'in the manner set out in Appendix C,' making the Appendix the operative criteria. Appendix C fixes the days-past-due bands (Normal/Watch/Substandard/Doubtful/Loss) that in turn drive the paragraph 45 provisioning percentages (1%/5%/25%/75%/100%). You should read the two together as one binding rule: the Appendix C arrears band determines the category, and the category determines the mandatory minimum provision.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11 / para 36):** "Every DTFC shall classify loans and advances in the manner set out in Appendix C to these Regulations"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 29 / Appendix C):** "All loans and advances should be classified by institutions according to the following criteria... Days at Risk (Number of days past due)"

### [[Loan Review Function]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating asset-quality compliance, read the loan review function and loan classification rules as two linked duties within a single Chapter 6 obligation chain, not as separate controls. Paragraph 34 makes the loan review function responsible for ensuring problem accounts are 'internally classified in accordance with the classification criteria in these regulations' and that adequate provisions are maintained — which is precisely what paragraphs 35-46 quantify (quarterly review, Appendix C classification, and fixed provisioning percentages). You should conclude that a failure in classification or provisioning is also a failure of the loan review function, and assess both as one accountability regime running up to the board.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11-12 / paras 35-45):** "Every DTFC shall review, classify and appropriately make provisions for its loan portfolio at least once every three months"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 11 / para 34):** "problem accounts are identified properly and on a timely basis and internally classified in accordance with the classification criteria in these regulations"

### [[SAMA (Saudi Central Bank)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When identifying who sets and enforces the loan classification and provisioning regime, attribute it to SAMA acting under the Finance Companies Control Law, not to internal DTFC discretion. The document is issued by SAMA under Royal Decree M/51, and the classification thresholds and minimum provisioning percentages are prescribed by 'these Regulations' — SAMA's rules — which also require SAMA-side statutory deposits and reporting. You should conclude that deviations from the classification and provisioning minimums are supervisory breaches enforceable by SAMA, and that any easier IFRS impairment outcome does not displace SAMA's floor.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 12 / para 45-46):** "Where the impairment charges computed under International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) are lower than provisions required under these Regulations"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 1-2 / issuing authority):** "Saudi Arabian Monetary Authority — Deposit Taking Finance Companies (DTFCs) Regulations 2020"
