# Specialized Lending

## Connections

### [[Exposures to Corporates]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When placing an exposure in the corporate class, you must further test whether it is specialized lending, because specialized lending is a defined subcategory of the corporate class with its own risk-weight rules. Para 7.37 lists 'specialized lending exposures, as defined in paragraph 7.41' as a corporate subcategory, and 7.42–7.43 split it into project, object and commodities finance with issue-specific-rating treatment (issuer ratings barred). Conclude that specialized-lending characteristics displace general corporate treatment: check 7.41–7.43 criteria and do not apply issuer ratings to these exposures.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / 7.43):** "Banks will assign to their specialized lending exposures the risk weights determined by the issue-specific external ratings... Issuer ratings must not be used."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 23 / 7.37):** "The corporate exposure class differentiates between the following subcategories: 1. General corporate exposures; 2. Specialized lending exposures, as defined in paragraph 7.41."

### [[Supervisory Slotting Approach for Specialized Lending]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When an IRB bank holds specialized-lending exposures but cannot estimate PD, this link tells you the fallback is not the general corporate IRB formula but the supervisory slotting approach in chapter 13. Para 13.2 requires banks that do not meet the PD-estimation requirements for PF, OF, CF and IPRE to map internal grades to five supervisory categories with fixed UL risk weights (70%/90%/115%/250%/0%). Conclude that eligibility for slotting turns on whether you meet the PD-estimation requirements — verify that before choosing between slotting and full IRB, and use the chapter-13 slotting criteria (13.13–13.15) for the mapping.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / 7.42):** "Exposures described in paragraph 7.41 will be classified in one of the following three subcategories of specialized lending: Project finance... Object finance... Commodities finance."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 138 / 13.2):** "banks that do not meet the requirements for the estimation of probability of default (PD)... will be required to map their internal grades to five supervisory categories."

### [[Supervisory Slotting Criteria Approach]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining capital treatment for a bank's project finance, object finance, commodities finance or IPRE exposures, treat 'specialized lending' and the 'supervisory slotting approach' as directly linked: the slotting approach is the mandatory fallback for those SL exposures where the bank cannot estimate PD under the corporate IRB. Chapter 13 defines the SL sub-classes and requires banks to map internal grades to five supervisory categories with fixed risk weights, and Chapter 16.27 confirms banks must assign to internal grades then map into those categories using the Chapter 13 slotting tables. You would conclude that eligibility to escape slotting depends on meeting the PD-estimation requirements, and that where those are not met the Chapter 13 slotting tables and their risk weights govern.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 138 / Art 13.2):** "banks that do not meet the requirements for the estimation of probability of default (PD) under the corporate internal ratings-based (IRB) approach will be required to map their internal grades to five supervisory categories"
