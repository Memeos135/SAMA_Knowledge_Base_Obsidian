# Material Outsourcing

## Connections

### [[Business Continuity Management]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When planning continuity for outsourced functions, note that the enhanced BCP duty is triggered specifically by the Material classification, not by outsourcing generally. Art 31-32 require a separate contingency plan for each Material outsourcing arrangement covering sudden termination or provider failure, plus documented alternatives and an in-house fallback procedure for each Material contract. You would conclude that materiality drives the depth of the continuity obligation — a non-Material arrangement does not attract the per-contract separate-contingency-plan requirement, but a Material one does.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 13 / Art 32):** "banks must set a procedure if they choose to bring the outsourced function in-house for each of their Material outsourcing contracts"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 12 / Art 31):** "For all Material outsourcing, banks should have a separate contingency plan for each outsourcing arrangement, which outlines the procedures to be followed in the event that the arrangement is suddenly terminated"

### [[Rules on Outsourcing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When classifying an outsourcing arrangement, the Material/non-Material distinction is the trigger that determines which heightened duties apply, so classify before you assess obligations. The Rules attach specific requirements to Material outsourcing — a business-case analysis before entry, separate contingency plans, immediate SAMA notification on termination, mandatory internal-audit review, and enhanced register entries. You would conclude that mislabelling a function as non-Material strips these obligations away improperly, so document the materiality reasoning and the date of the last assessment as the register requires.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 13 / Art 37, R.40):** "Where a Material outsourcing agreement is terminated, banks must notify SAMA immediately... Whether it is considered Material or not, the reasons why it is considered as such"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / Art 16):** "Banks must be able to demonstrate to SAMA that, in assessing the options for outsourcing a Material business function or activity to a third party, it has: a) Prepared and analyzed a business case"

### [[SAMA No Objection Requirement]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding whether prior SAMA clearance is needed, understand that the no-objection requirement is triggered by cross-border placement of an outsourced function rather than by materiality alone, though the two interact. Art 42 requires a written SAMA no-objection for any outsourcing to an overseas TPSP, and the submission must state whether the function is Material or non-Material. The overarching no-objection principle also forbids using outsourcing to avoid regulatory compliance. You would conclude that an overseas Material outsourcing needs both the materiality-driven duties and advance written SAMA no-objection before commencement, so verify the categorization and secure clearance first.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 13 / Section v Art 42):** "For any proposed outsourcing arrangements to a third-party service provider located overseas, banks are required to seek a written SAMA no objection... b) Categorization of the function (Material and non-Material outsourcing)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 7 / Section E Art 8):** "banks should ensure that outsourcing does not reduce the protection available to depositors nor be used as a way of avoiding compliance with regulatory requirements"
