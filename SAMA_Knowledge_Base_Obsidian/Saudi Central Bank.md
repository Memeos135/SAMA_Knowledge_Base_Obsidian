# Saudi Central Bank

## Connections

### [[Law of Payments and Payment Services]] — `references` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When identifying the legal source of SAMA's authority to license and supervise payment systems, read the Law of Payments together with the Saudi Central Bank Law, which is the parent grant of power. The SAMA Law empowers the Bank to establish and operate national payment, settlement, and clearing infrastructures and to issue rules, directives, and licenses and oversee such systems, and the Law of Payments operationalises this by requiring SAMA licensing to operate a payment system or provide payment services. Conclude that payment-sector licensing decisions rest on SAMA's statutory mandate under the SAMA Law, and that a payment-services applicant is a 'Financial Institution' subject to the Bank's supervision; verify the exact cross-reference as this link is inferred.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Art 4(7)):** "Establishing, developing, and operating infrastructures of national payment, settlement, and clearing systems; issuing rules, directives, and licenses; and overseeing and supervising ... payment ... systems."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Art 4):** "A person may not operate a payment system or provide payment services in the Kingdom unless licensed by SAMA."

### [[Payment Systems]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When establishing SAMA's legal authority to license and supervise payment systems, anchor it to the Saudi Central Bank Law rather than treating the Payment Systems Law as self-standing. The SCB Law expressly empowers the Bank to establish/operate national payment, settlement and clearing infrastructures and to issue rules, directives and licenses and supervise them within its jurisdiction — the enabling mandate that the Payment and Payment Services Law then operationalises. For a licensing or enforcement question, cite both: the SCB Law for institutional competence and the Payment Systems Law for the specific licensing prohibition (Article 4).
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Art 4(7)):** "Establishing, developing, and operating infrastructures of national payment, settlement, and clearing systems; issuing rules, directives, and licenses; and overseeing and supervising"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Art 4):** "A person may not operate a payment system or provide payment services in the Kingdom unless licensed by SAMA."

### [[Saudi Central Bank Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When identifying the source of SAMA's supervisory authority over any financial institution, anchor it here: 'Bank' is the defined term for the Saudi Central Bank in Article 1 of this Law, and Articles 3–4 vest the Bank with its objectives and its full catalogue of powers — supervision, issuing regulations and directives, consumer protection, and preventing crimes associated with financial institutions. This tells you that every downstream SAMA regulation, circular or licence traces its legal validity to powers conferred by this statute. Treat this Law as the apex instrument in the hierarchy; when testing whether a SAMA directive is intra vires, check it against the enumerated Article 4 competences.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Art 1):** "Bank: The Saudi Central Bank. ... Financial Institution: Any natural or legal person subject to the supervision, oversight, and regulation of the Bank."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 3 / Art 4):** "To achieve its objectives, the Bank shall have all the necessary powers and carry out the following duties, powers, and competences"
