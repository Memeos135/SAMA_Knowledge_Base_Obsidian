# Customer Due Diligence

## Connections

### [[AML Implementing Regulation (SAMA 1428)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping CDD obligations, read this concept as the core enforceable duty set of the Regulation: identify and verify the customer and beneficial owner using reliable, independent sources, with the level of measures set by risk. The Regulation imposes hard consequences for failure — the institution may not open the account or must terminate the relationship, and must consider filing an STR (Art 7). Conclude that CDD is not discretionary and that reliance on a third party (Art 7/10-7/11) does not transfer responsibility; the relying institution must immediately obtain all required information and satisfy itself the relied-upon party is regulated and supervised.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 7 / Art 7/8-7/9):** "may not open the account, establish the business relationship or carry out the transaction ... shall terminate the business relationship"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / Art 7/2):** "Identify the customer and verify the customer's identity, using reliable, independent source documents, data or information"

### [[Politically Exposed Persons]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping CDD obligations, treat PEP handling as a mandatory escalation within the risk-based due diligence framework rather than a separate regime. Section 7/14 requires enhanced due diligence where money-laundering risk is higher, and Article 8 designates PEPs (plus their family members to the second degree and close associates) as a category triggering those enhanced measures. For a compliance decision this means that identifying a customer or beneficial owner as a PEP is not optional profiling — it forces the higher tier of CDD and additional obligations, so a PEP determination should feed directly into whether simplified measures are ever permissible (they are not for higher-risk relationships).
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8 / 7/14):** "Where the risk of money laundering is higher... shall apply enhanced due diligence measures consistent with the risks identified."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / 8/2, 8/3):** "The obligations under Article 8 of the Law shall apply in relation to politically exposed persons, their close associates and family members."

### [[Reliance on Another Institution]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding whether you can accept another institution's identity verification instead of performing your own, read 7/10-7/11 as a conditional carve-out from the core CDD duties in 7/2, not an exemption from them. The reliance provision permits a financial institution to rely on another FI or DNFBP for customer/beneficial-owner identification, but only if it immediately obtains all information required under Article 7, secures prompt availability of underlying documentation, and confirms the relied-upon party is regulated and supervised. The practical consequence: ultimate CDD responsibility stays with the relying institution, so third-party reliance cannot be used to lower the identification standard set in 7/2 — verify the counterparty meets the qualifying conditions before relying.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / 7/2):** "Identify the customer and verify the customer's identity, using reliable, independent source documents, data or information"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 7 / 7/10-7/11):** "may rely on another financial institution... immediately obtains all necessary information as required under Article 7 of the Law and this Implementing Regulation"
