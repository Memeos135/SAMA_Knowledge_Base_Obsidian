# Inoperative Accounts

## Connections

### [[Abandoned Accounts]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When classifying an account's status, read 'abandoned' as a later stage within the single lifecycle of inoperative accounts, not a separate regime: an account becomes abandoned only 'after completing the periods specified' following unclaimed status, which is itself the terminal end of the dormant/unclaimed progression governed by this Rule. This matters because the escalating obligations (double supervision, separate filing, balance treatment as liabilities, eventual transfer to a suspense account) attach in sequence, and government-entity accounts are expressly excluded from the abandoned phase only. Conclude that you must track the timing chain that moves an account into the abandoned category and confirm whether the specific customer type is carved out of that final stage.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 15 / sec. 5.1):** "This Rule applies to all assets (accounts, banking relationships, transactions, etc.) ... deposited in banks operating in Saudi Arabia."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 17 / sec. 5.2.4):** "Accounts shall be considered abandoned after completing the periods specified in this paragraph from the date of classifying the accounts as unclaimed"

### [[Chapter II Supervisory Rules and Controls|Chapter II: Supervisory Rules and Controls]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining a bank's obligations for dormant/inoperative accounts, treat the specific dormancy provisions as living inside the Supervisory Rules chapter rather than as standalone guidance, because the dormant/unclaimed/abandoned-account lifecycle (24-month dormancy, dual supervision on reactivation, disclosure searches covering inoperative accounts) is set out within Chapter II's supervisory controls. This means the enforceability and confidentiality obligations attaching to inoperative accounts derive from the same supervisory-rule regime that also governs SAMA disclosure and enforcement requests. Conclude that dormant-account handling and SAMA disclosure/enforcement duties must be read together as one supervisory obligation set, not applied in isolation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 16 / 5.2.2):** "Accounts shall be considered dormant after (24) calendar months from the date of the last recorded debit transaction"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 9 / Chapter II):** "Chapter II. Supervisory Rules and Controls ... as a basis for opening, operating, and following up bank accounts."

### [[Communication Policy for Inoperative Accounts]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** In determining what a bank must do before reclassifying or closing an inoperative account, the customer-communication obligations are integral to the inoperative-account regime rather than an optional add-on: the Rule requires banks to contact customers, inform them of the intended action, and prompt reactivation before status change or closure. This is grounded in the express duties to notify customers 'before completing five years,' to give a month's notice before closing small balances, and to inform embassies/diplomats 'a year before changing the account status from dormant to unclaimed.' Conclude that failure to complete the prescribed communications is a compliance gap that can invalidate a status change or closure, so verify the notice steps for the specific stage in question.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 15 / sec. 5.1):** "changing account status to abandoned if banks are unable to reach the account holder after using all methods of contact."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 16-17 / sec. 5.2.2):** "Banks are required to contact customers and inform them of the action to be taken on his/her account before completing five years ... provided that the customer is notified a month prior to the date of closing"

### [[Dormant Accounts]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** For status classification, treat 'dormant' as the defined entry point of the inoperative-account lifecycle: an account becomes dormant after 24 calendar months from the last customer-initiated debit or documented correspondence, and this triggers the heightened controls (double supervision, restricted withdrawals, separate filing) that carry through the inoperative regime. The link is grounded in the Rule's own sequencing, where dormant status precedes unclaimed and then abandoned status under the same governing Rule. Conclude that the 24-month trigger and its accompanying activation controls are the baseline test you apply first, before assessing whether an account has escalated to later inoperative stages.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 15 / sec. 5.1):** "This Rule applies to all assets (accounts, banking relationships, transactions, etc.) ... deposited in banks operating in Saudi Arabia."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 16 / sec. 5.2.2):** "Accounts shall be considered dormant after (24) calendar months from the date of the last recorded debit transaction carried out by a customer or his/her authorized agent"

### [[Unclaimed Accounts]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When escalating an inoperative account, read 'unclaimed' as the stage that follows dormancy and triggers the most consequential balance actions: for government entities and embassies, an unclaimed classification requires issuing a bank check for the balance to the Ministry of Finance's account with SAMA and specific customer notification obligations. The link is grounded in the Rule's staged progression, where 'abandoned' status is defined by reference to 'the date of classifying the accounts as unclaimed.' Conclude that on reaching unclaimed status you must apply the prescribed remittance/notification steps for the relevant customer type, and confirm the one-year advance-notice requirement was met before the dormant-to-unclaimed change.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 22 / sec. 10.1):** "banks shall search for all relationships ... including all active; closed and suspended accounts, inoperative accounts, deposits, valuable boxes, credit cards"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 20 / b. Unclaimed accounts):** "A bank check shall be issued with the unclaimed account balance of the government entity to the Ministry of Finance's account with SAMA."
