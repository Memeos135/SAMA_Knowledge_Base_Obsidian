# Pillar 2 Risks

## Connections

### [[Basel II Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank holds sufficient capital, Pillar 1 alone is insufficient because the Basel II framework requires banks to identify, under Pillar 2, a wider spectrum of risks (strategic, reputational, liquidity, concentration, macroeconomic, interest-rate) and set aside capital via the ICAAP. The framework document makes ICAAP a mandatory 'cornerstone' obligation within Pillar 2 scope. For compliance you should conclude that a bank's own ICAAP-driven capital assessment is enforceable in addition to Pillar 1 minimums, and that SAMA may demand more capital even where Pillar 1 risks are well managed.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / 3.3 ICAAP as a part of Pillar 2):** "banks shall, within the framework of Pillar 2, identify all of the risks to which they are exposed"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Introduction and Overview):** "within the scope of Pillar 2, they develop their own Internal Credit Adequacy Assessment Plan – ICAAP"

### [[Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Plan (ICAAP)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When identifying which risks your ICAAP must address, use this link to confirm the ICAAP's mandatory scope extends well beyond Pillar 1: it is a Pillar 2 tool requiring banks to identify all risks they are exposed to, expressly including strategic, reputational, liquidity, concentration, macro/business-cycle and banking-book interest-rate risk. The document ties the ICAAP requirement directly to the Pillar 2 framework ('ICAAP as a part of Pillar 2'). You should therefore ensure the ICAAP evidences assessment of these additional risk categories, since omitting them would leave the Pillar 2 obligation unmet even if Pillar 1 is satisfied.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6):** "banks shall, within the framework of Pillar 2, identify all of the risks to which they are exposed ... Strategic risk ... Reputational risk ... Liquidity risk ... Concentration risk"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4):** "within the scope of Pillar 2, they develop their own Internal Credit Adequacy Assessment Plan – ICAAP"

### [[Liquidity Coverage Ratio]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** These two instruments appear to intersect because Pillar 2/ICAAP treats liquidity risk as one of the risks a bank must self-assess for capital adequacy, while the LCR is the dedicated Pillar-1-style minimum liquidity metric — but they are distinct obligations under separate SAMA circulars, so do not treat LCR compliance as discharging the Pillar 2 liquidity-risk assessment or vice versa. The Pillar 2 document lists 'Liquidity risk – the risks of difficulties in raising liquidity or capital,' signalling overlap in subject matter only. Treat this as a lead: verify each circular's own scope and requirements independently, since a satisfactory LCR does not by itself evidence adequate ICAAP treatment of liquidity risk.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6 / section 3.3):** "Liquidity risk – the risks of difficulties in raising liquidity or capital in certain situations"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 12 / para 11):** "during periods of stress, it would be entirely appropriate for banks to use their stock of HQLA, thereby falling below the minimum"
- **Caveat:** INFERRED conceptual link across two separate SAMA circulars; the overlap is thematic (liquidity risk) only. Confirm each document's obligations independently before treating them as related requirements.
