# Non-resident Commercial Bank Correspondent Accounts

## Connections

### [[AMLCFT Form|AML/CFT Form]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When opening correspondent accounts for non-resident commercial banks under Rule 300.2.5, the AML/CFT form requirement is a mandatory precondition, not a formality: the bank must obtain a completed AML/CFT form from each correspondent stating commitment to AML/CFT policies and confirming it does not allow shell banks to use its accounts. Both provisions live in the same Account Opening Rules, so the correspondent-account permission in 300.2.5 is conditioned on the due-diligence/attestation duties described on page 97. Conclude that a correspondent relationship cannot be relied upon as compliant unless the AML/CFT form and shell-bank assurance are documented at onboarding and for existing relations.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 96 / Rule 300.2.5):** "Correspondent accounts may be opened for non-resident commercial banks (including GCC banks), including correspondent accounts for central banks"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 97):** "Banks shall obtain a completed AML/CFT form from each correspondent bank, stating that such bank is committed to the AML/CFT policies... does not allow shell banks to use its accounts"

### [[Correspondence Relationship]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing correspondent-banking obligations, read the Account Opening Rules (300.2.5) alongside Section 13 of the AML/CTF Rules, because the latter supplies the enforceable AML/CTF regime that the account-opening permission presupposes — including senior-management approval, understanding each party's responsibilities, ongoing monitoring, and the absolute prohibition on dealing with shell banks. Section 13 grounds these duties in Article (9) of the AML Law and Article (68) of the Law on Combating Terrorism Crime and Financing, so they are statutory, not merely procedural. Conclude that permission to open a correspondent account under 300.2.5 does not discharge the separate Section 13 obligations, and both must be satisfied before and throughout the relationship.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 96 / Rule 300.2.5):** "Correspondent accounts may be opened for non-resident commercial banks (including GCC banks)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 62 / Section 13):** "Article (9) of the Anti-Money Laundering Law and Article (68)... set forth the obligations that must be fulfilled by the financial institution before entering into a correspondence relationship"

### [[Rules for Bank Accounts]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a non-resident bank relationship may be opened, read the parent Rules together with Rule 300.2.5 on correspondent accounts, which is the specific carve-out governing non-resident commercial banks (including GCC banks and central banks). This matters cross-regime: correspondent banking is a recognised higher-risk AML/CTF category, and note that non-resident money changers and non-GCC payment card companies are cross-referenced back to 300.2.5's documentation requirements or subjected to outright prohibition absent senior/compliance approval and SAMA notification. Conclude that eligibility and CDD depth for these entities are set by these specific sub-rules, and verify which sub-rule (300.2.5–300.2.8) applies to the counterparty type before opening.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 98 / 300.2.8):** "Banks are not permitted to open accounts for such companies. However, after obtaining approval of the CEO/general director and the manager of compliance department."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 22 / 10.1):** "banks shall search for all relationships between the bank and the customer, including all active; closed and suspended accounts."
