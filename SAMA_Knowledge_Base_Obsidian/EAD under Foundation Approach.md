# EAD under Foundation Approach

## Connections

### [[Credit Conversion Factors]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When estimating exposure at default for off-balance sheet commitments, the CCF concept and the foundation EAD approach are directly coupled: under the foundation approach EAD equals the committed-but-undrawn amount multiplied by a CCF, so the two documents describe the same computation across the two circulars. This link tells you that CCF definitions in SAMA_EN_3487 and the EAD-estimation standards in SAMA_EN_3502 form one continuous methodology. When choosing between foundation and advanced treatment, verify which CCF/EAD basis applies and note the advanced-approach constraints (e.g. EAD reference data must not be capped, and instability near fully-drawn limits must be quarantined).
- **Grounding — this node (Page 209 / 16.94-16.95 (SAMA_EN_3502)):** "undrawn limit factor (ULF) approach... to estimating CCFs... EAD reference data must not be capped to the principal amount outstanding or facility limits."

### [[Exposure at Default (EAD)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When computing EAD for off-balance-sheet items under F-IRB, apply the general EAD definition through the foundation-specific rule that EAD equals the committed-but-undrawn amount times a CCF. Paragraph 12.32 anchors the foundation calculation in the standardized-approach CCFs and commitment definition (per 7.86), while the general EAD concept (16.88) supplies the underlying meaning — expected gross exposure at default. Conclude that under F-IRB banks use supervisory CCFs rather than own estimates for undrawn commitments, and verify against the standardized CCF schedule (7.86–7.93) rather than deriving direct facility EAD, which is reserved for the advanced approach.
