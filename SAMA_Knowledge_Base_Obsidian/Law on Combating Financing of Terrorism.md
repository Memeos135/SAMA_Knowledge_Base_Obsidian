# Law on Combating Financing of Terrorism

## Connections

### [[Anti-Money Laundering Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping AML/CTF obligations for a Saudi FI or DNFBP, do not treat money-laundering and terrorist-financing rules as fully separate codes, because the CTF Law's own text cross-refers to the Anti-Money Laundering Law for the monitoring standard. Article 69 requires ongoing scrutiny of transactions 'as prescribed by the relevant provisions stipulated in the Anti-Money Laundering Law,' so the CTF preventive-measures regime borrows the AML transaction-monitoring baseline. For a compliance decision, conclude that TF-specific duties (risk assessment, EDD, STR reporting to the Directorate) sit on top of, and incorporate, the AML monitoring provisions — check both instruments together, not the CTF Law alone.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 1 / Chapter 1):** "Law: Combating Terrorism Crimes and their Financing Law"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / Art 69):** "FIs and DNFBPs must monitor and scrutinize transactions... as prescribed by the relevant provisions stipulated in the Anti-Money Laundering Law..."
- **Caveat:** The two node labels are drawn from the same source file; the link reflects an internal cross-reference from the CTF Law to the AML Law rather than two independent documents.

### [[Know Your Customer (KYC)]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining the legal source of a DTFC's AML/CTF duties, note that the DTFC KYC chapter and the Law on Combating the Financing of Terrorism *appear* to operate in the same regime, but the KYC chapter here is SAMA sectoral rule-making while the CTF Law is primary legislation carrying its own reporting, supervisory-sanction and Financial Intelligence Directorate architecture. The KYC chapter itself directs suspicion reporting to SAMA's FIU and requires conformity with statutory requirements on terrorism-finance prevention, which links conceptually to the CTF Law's regime for suspicious transaction reporting and supervisory penalties (up to SAR 5,000,000 per violation). Treat this as a lead: verify the CTF Law and its Implementing Regulation (and the AML Law) directly to confirm which reporting channel, defined terms, and penalty exposure actually bind the DTFC, since the DTFC chapter does not itself cite the CTF Law.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 18 / Article on supervisory measures):** "Impose a monetary fine of up to 5.000.000 riyals per violation"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 51 / para 166):** "policies and procedures at least conform with local statutory and regulatory requirements with respect to money laundering and terrorism finance prevention"
- **Caveat:** Relation is INFERRED; the DTFC KYC chapter does not expressly cite the CTF Law. Confirm the actual statutory reporting channel and penalty basis in the CTF Law and its Implementing Regulation before relying on this link.

### [[Permanent Committee for Combating Terrorism and its Financing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping who implements UN Security Council TFS obligations in KSA, identify the Permanent Committee for Combating Terrorism and its Financing as the body designated by the CTF Law to receive and operationalize such resolutions. Article 75 vests the Committee with receiving requests for implementation of UNSCRs and putting in place mechanisms, with those mechanisms issued by decision of the President of State Security. For a sanctions/TFS compliance decision, conclude that the primary listing/implementation mechanics flow from this Committee's issuances — verify the current mechanism it has set rather than relying on the Law's text alone, since the Law delegates the detail.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 16 / Art 75):** "The Permanente Committee for Combating Terrorism and its financing receive requests from countries and organizations for the implementation of UN Security Council Resolutions..."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 16 / Art 75):** "The Committee shall put in place, and update, mechanisms... The mechanisms shall be issued pursuant to a decision by the President of State Security."
