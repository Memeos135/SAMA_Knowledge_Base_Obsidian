# Electronic Crime

## Connections

### [[Criminal Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** Treat electronic crime as a listed sub-element of the guide's criminal-risk category (section 2.1.5), alongside fraud, forgery and counterfeiting. The index places 'Electronic Crime' under '2.1 Criminal Risks,' and the guide notes it is 'no different except for mode of execution,' with insurance (ECC) coverage discussed as a risk-transfer response. For a compliance reader, this confirms electronic-crime loss exposure is part of the operational/criminal-risk framework and should be scoped within the Operational Risk Management Plan, including any risk-transfer decisions.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 1 (Index) / 2.1.5):** "2.1 Criminal Risks... 2.1.5 Electronic Crime; 2.1.6 Retail Electronic Crime"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Section 2.1):** "the single largest area of operational risk within the Saudi banks has been that associated with criminal activities"

### [[Electronic and Computer Crimes Coverage]] — `conceptually_related_to` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping insurance coverage to the electronic-crime risk category, note that ECC coverage is the guide's designated risk-transfer response to third-party fraud affecting computer and telecommunications systems. Importantly, the guide states ECC 'may not be written without a BBB/FIB being present' and is treated as a companion piece purchased in the same limit, so a reader should conclude the two coverages are dependent, not free-standing. Treat this as guidance describing market coverage structure rather than a SAMA mandate to buy either.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8 / section 2.1.5):** "2.1.5 Electronic Crime ... no different except for mode of execution"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 24 / section 3.2.2):** "the ECC coverage may not be written without a BBB/FIB being present ... it is truly a companion piece to the BBB/FIB."
- **Caveat:** Relation is 'conceptually_related_to' and describes insurance-market structure; the electronic-crime node text is truncated, so confirm the substantive risk description before relying on the mapping.
