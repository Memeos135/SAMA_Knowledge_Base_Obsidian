# Cyber Security Risk Management Process

## Connections

### [[Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining who owns cyber risk methodology, note the Framework makes the CISO responsible for developing and maintaining the cyber security risk management process, which must in turn be aligned with the organisation's enterprise risk management. This ties the CISO's role directly to the risk-management provisions in section 3.2. For a compliance assessment you would verify the risk process is CISO-owned, defined, approved, implemented, and demonstrably aligned to enterprise risk management, as a disconnected or unowned process would fail both the roles and the risk-management control considerations.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19):** "A cyber security risk management process should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organization's enterprise risk management process"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 16):** "The CISO should be responsible for: developing and maintaining: ... 4. cyber security risk management process"

### [[Cyber Security Maturity Model]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** These two concepts appear to connect as measurement-versus-substance: the Maturity Model grades whether controls are defined, approved and implemented, while the Risk Management Process is one of the substantive processes being graded against those criteria. The model's levels (e.g. 'defined, approved and implemented in a structured and formalized way') mirror the language used to describe the required risk management process, suggesting a maturity assessment of an organization measures how formalized its risk process is. A reviewer using this as a lead should verify against the primary text how each control domain — including risk management — maps to specific maturity criteria before drawing conclusions about compliance scoring.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / 3.2.1):** "A cyber security risk management process should be defined, approved and implemented"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / 2.4):** "Cyber security controls are defined, approved and implemented in a structured and formalized way"
- **Caveat:** Relation is INFERRED from parallel 'defined, approved and implemented' phrasing; the model applies to all control domains, so confirm the specific maturity-to-risk-process mapping in the primary text.

### [[Cyber Security Risk Management and Compliance]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating risk-management obligations, treat the Cyber Security Risk Management Process (3.2.1) as the mandatory core mechanism of the Risk Management and Compliance domain (3.2), comprising identification, analysis, response, and monitoring/review. The framework requires this process to be defined, approved, implemented, and aligned with the enterprise risk management process, and expressly binds accepted risks to SAMA regulations ('does not contradict SAMA regulations'). A reviewer should conclude that an unapproved, undocumented, or ERM-misaligned process is a domain-level failure, and should check that risk-acceptance sign-offs stay within risk appetite and remain reported to the cyber security committee.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / 3.2.1):** "A cyber security risk management process should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organization's enterprise risk management process."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 21):** "the accepted cyber security risk ... does not contradict SAMA regulations."
