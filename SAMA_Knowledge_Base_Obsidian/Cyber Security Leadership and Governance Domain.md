# Cyber Security Leadership and Governance Domain

## Connections

### [[Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping accountability under the CSF's Leadership and Governance domain, the CISO is the defined head of the cyber security function — a function required to be independent from IT and to report directly to the CEO/managing director or a control-function general manager. This structural independence is itself a governance control, reinforced by the requirement to obtain SAMA no-objection before assigning the CISO. For compliance decisions, verify the CISO's reporting line and independence, and confirm SAMA no-objection was obtained, rather than treating the role as an ordinary IT appointment.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 14):** "obtain no objection from SAMA to assign the CISO"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 48 (Glossary)):** "headed by a CISO and that reports directly to the CEO/managing director of the Member Organization or general manager of a control function"

### [[Cyber Security Committee]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether risk-acceptance decisions are validly governed, note that the CSF's Leadership and Governance domain places the Cyber Security Committee in the escalation chain: accepted cyber security risks must be reported to the committee and confirmed to sit within risk appetite and not to contradict SAMA regulations. The committee is thus a governance control point, not advisory. For compliance decisions, check that risk acceptances carry both business-owner sign-off and reporting to the committee before relying on them as properly governed.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 15 / 3.1.4):** "Responsibilities to implement, maintain, support and promote cyber security should be defined throughout the Member Organization."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 21):** "the accepted cyber security risk is within the risk appetite and is reported to the cyber security committee"

### [[Cyber Security Framework (CSF)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping who is accountable for cyber security within a Member Organization, start from the Leadership and Governance domain, because the CSF places ultimate responsibility on the Board and assigns defined roles to senior management and the CISO. The policy must include 'a statement of the board's intent' and 'a definition of general and specific responsibilities for cyber security.' For a compliance decision, conclude that governance gaps (unassigned roles, no board endorsement) are direct CSF non-compliance, not merely best-practice shortfalls.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 15 / 3.1.4 Roles and Responsibilities):** "Responsibilities to implement, maintain, support and promote cyber security should be defined throughout the Member Organization"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / 3.1.4):** "The Board of Directors has the ultimate responsibility for cyber security"
