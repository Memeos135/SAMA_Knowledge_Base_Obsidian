# KYC Principle

## Connections

### [[Due Diligence Measures]] — `conceptually_related_to` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping customer due-diligence duties, the Account Opening Rules' KYC principle and the AML/CTF Rules' Due Diligence Measures appear to be two expressions of the same risk-based CDD obligation, and should be read together rather than as separate regimes. The KYC node itself directs that its rules be read in conjunction with the AML Law and Implementing Regulations, and the Due Diligence node builds those same law-based obligations (identifying customers, ownership/control structure, third-party reliance, simplified/enhanced measures). Treat the linkage as a strong lead but verify the specific article cross-references in each primary text before concluding that a step satisfied under one instrument automatically satisfies the other.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 21):** "These Rules shall be read in conjunction with the requirements of the Anti-Money Laundering Law and its Implementing Regulations"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 29 / Section 3):** "The financial institution shall understand the ownership and control structure of the (legal person) customer"
- **Caveat:** Relation is INFERRED/conceptual; the two documents are distinct instruments. Confirm the applicable article references before relying on cross-instrument equivalence of CDD steps.

### [[Non-resident Juristic Persons Account Rules]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping account-opening obligations for non-resident juristic persons (e.g. GCC establishments), do not treat the documentary checklist in the 300.2 rules as separate from the KYC principle — the same instrument requires banks to ascertain the nature of relationships, verify IDs and beneficial ownership, and read all of it in conjunction with the AML Law and its Implementing Regulations. Because both nodes sit in the same SAMA Account Opening Rules, the specific evidence requirements (memorandum of association, ownership >50% GCC test, verifying board and authorized managers) are the operationalization of the general KYC/CDD duty. Conclude that the non-resident juristic checklist is enforceable KYC, not optional documentation, and that omitting beneficial-ownership verification would breach the KYC principle it references.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 21):** "These Rules shall be read in conjunction with the requirements of the Anti-Money Laundering Law and its Implementing Regulations"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 92 / Rule 300.2):** "the ownership of GCC citizens (natural or juristic) exceeds 50% of the company's capital... Verifying the IDs of the members of the board of directors"
