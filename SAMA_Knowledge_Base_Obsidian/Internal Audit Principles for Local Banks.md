# Internal Audit Principles for Local Banks

## Connections

### [[Independence and Objectivity of Audit Unit]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a local bank's internal audit function against SAMA's Internal Audit Principles, treat independence and objectivity as a mandatory core attribute of the audit unit, not an aspirational value. The Principles (issued under the Central Bank Law and the Banking Control Law) list 'Independence and Objectivity' under Principle 4 on the unit's key characteristics, and the covering letter frames the whole regime as ensuring the unit performs an 'independent and objective assessment' of governance, risk management and controls. Conclude that when reviewing audit-unit arrangements you should verify this attribute is embedded in structure and reporting lines, since it is a substantive requirement of the framework rather than a general principle.

### [[Risk-Based Internal Audit Plan]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating a local bank's audit coverage, note that SAMA requires the internal audit plan to be risk-based rather than a fixed cyclical checklist: the Principles set out, within the unit's organisation and responsibilities, both a 'risk assessment methodology' and a 'risk-based internal audit plan.' This means the plan's scope and prioritisation must flow from the unit's own risk assessment, and the list of auditable units and the audit cycle sit alongside it. Conclude that when assessing compliance you should check that the audit plan is demonstrably derived from a risk assessment and covers the entities subject to audit, since a purely calendar-driven plan would not meet the framework.

### [[Three Lines of Defence Model]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping a bank's governance and control responsibilities, use these Principles to place internal audit as the third line of defence — a supervisory/assurance tool for the bank's management, not a substitute for the first and second lines. The introduction expressly describes the unit's role 'as a third line of defence' complementing the first and second lines, and a dedicated Principle (8) governs the unit's relationship with second-line functions and external auditors. Conclude that first-line (business/controls) and second-line (risk/compliance) responsibilities remain with those functions; internal audit's assurance does not discharge them, and you should not treat audit coverage as replacing the second line's own monitoring.
