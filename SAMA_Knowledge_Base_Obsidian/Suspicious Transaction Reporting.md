# Suspicious Transaction Reporting

## Connections

### [[Exchange Houses Operating Procedures Guide]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping an exchange house's AML duties, treat suspicious-transaction reporting as a mandatory, standalone obligation the Guide imposes: it requires reporting any operation where there are reasonable grounds to suspect a link to criminal or irregular activity to the competent authority (the FIU). The Guide grounds this in its transaction-monitoring duty (monitor all dealings for unusual patterns lacking a clear economic or legal purpose) and in its enumerated AML/CTF measures supplementing the AML Law/Bylaws. Conclude that suspicion — not just threshold breach — triggers a reporting duty, and that the exchange house cannot rely solely on CDD/KYC completion; check the AML Law/Bylaws for the reporting standard, protected-disclosure rules and any tipping-off prohibition, which the Guide references but does not exhaustively set out.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 15 / reporting item):** "report any operation where there are reasonable grounds to suspect it is linked to criminal or irregular activity to the competent authority (paraphrase; source Arabic OCR-garbled)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / item on monitoring and reporting):** "monitor all dealings to detect unusual patterns of activity that have no clear economic or legal purpose (paraphrase; source Arabic OCR-garbled)"
- **Caveat:** Source Arabic is OCR-garbled; both excerpts are paraphrases. Confirm the suspicion standard and reporting duty against the AML Law and Implementing Regulation.

### [[Financial Investigations Unit]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining where an exchanger's suspicious-activity report must go, treat these two nodes as a single obligation chain within the same Exchange Business Procedures Manual: the Manual's definition of the Financial Investigations Unit (FIU) fixes it as the authority empowered to receive and analyse reports of activity suspected of money laundering and terrorism financing, and the Manual's 'Reporting' (البلاغ) section imposes the duty to report. The consequence is that a suspicious-transaction obligation is discharged by reporting to the FIU as defined here, not to SAMA generally or to an authority the firm selects; confirm the FIU as the statutory recipient before concluding a report is properly filed. Because both nodes derive from the same document, the definitional linkage is internally consistent.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 2 (table of contents — الإبلاغ / البلاغ)):** "الإبلاغ (Reporting) — listed section of the Manual (paraphrase; source Arabic is OCR-garbled)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 (Definitions / تعريفات)):** "Financial Investigations Unit: the authority empowered to receive and analyse reports of activity suspected of money laundering and terrorism financing from all financial and non-financial institutions (paraphrase; source Arabic is OCR-garbled)"
- **Caveat:** Both nodes are extracted from the same OCR-garbled manual; the FIU-as-recipient reading is grounded in the definition but the specific reporting-channel wording should be verified against the clean primary text.
