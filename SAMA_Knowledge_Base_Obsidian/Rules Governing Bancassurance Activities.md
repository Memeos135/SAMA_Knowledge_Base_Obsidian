# Rules Governing Bancassurance Activities

## Connections

### [[Bancassurance Activity]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining what conduct the Rules actually regulate, anchor on the defined term 'Bancassurance Activities,' because scope and every downstream obligation turn on this definition. The Rules define it narrowly as 'Marketing and distribution of insurance products by the Bank to its Clients according to the signed Agreement,' and state the Rules 'shall apply to all Bancassurance Activities.' Conclude that activities outside marketing/distribution, or dealings with persons who are not the Bank's Clients, fall outside this regime — note in particular that the Bank must not conduct such activities with other than its Clients.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Scope):** "These Rules shall apply to all Bancassurance Activities."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 2 / Art 1.5):** "Bancassurance Activities: Marketing and distribution of insurance products by the Bank to its Clients according to the signed Agreement"

### [[Bank Obligations in Bancassurance]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping who bears the operative duties under the Rules, treat the Bank as the primary licensed obligor for the customer-facing distribution activity. The Rules impose direct, enforceable requirements on the Bank: activities practiced 'directly through the Bank,' a Bancassurance unit under an experienced director 'after obtaining SAMA's non-objection,' Authorised Employees holding the IFCE certificate, obtaining the Company's approval and notifying SAMA to expand channels, and a prohibition on serving non-Clients. Conclude that a bank cannot treat bancassurance as an insurer-only responsibility; these are the Bank's own licensing/conduct obligations, several gated by SAMA non-objection or notification.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 6):** "The Bank shall ensure that all Authorised Employees have the Insurance Foundations Professional Exam certificate (IFCE)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6):** "The Bank must not conduct Bancassurance Activities with other than his Clients."

### [[Banking Control Law (M5)|Banking Control Law (M/5)]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining which entities may lawfully conduct bancassurance, note that the Bancassurance Rules do not define 'Bank' independently but anchor it to the Banking Control Law, so only entities licensed to carry out banking business under that Law fall within scope. The Rules' definitions expressly tie 'Bank' to the Banking Control Law, and the scope clause makes that Law, alongside the Cooperative Insurance Companies Control Law and SAMA's relevant regulations, govern matters not addressed in the Rules. You should conclude that eligibility and residual governance for bancassurance flow back to the Banking Control Law, and check that any purported bancassurance provider holds a banking licence under it.
- **Grounding — this node (SAMA_EN_7908_VER1 Page 3 / Scope of Application):** "the Banking Control Law and SAMA's relevant regulations, rules, and instructions ... shall govern whatever is not provided for therein"
- **Grounding — related node (SAMA_EN_7908_VER1 Page 2 / Art 1.4):** "Bank: Any bank licensed to carry out banking business in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in accordance with the provisions of the Banking Control Law."

### [[Cooperative Insurance Companies Control Law (M32)|Cooperative Insurance Companies Control Law (M/32)]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing SAMA's authority to impose the Bancassurance Rules on banks and insurers, treat the Cooperative Insurance Companies Control Law (M/32) as the enabling parent instrument, not merely background. The Rules were issued by Governor's Decision expressly 'based on the powers vested to SAMA by the Cooperative Insurance Companies Control Law' and its Implementing Regulation, alongside the Banking Control Law. Conclude that the Rules are subordinate delegated legislation: they must be read consistently with M/32, and where the Rules are silent, the Control Law, its Implementing Regulation, and other relevant laws govern by their own terms.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Scope of Application):** "The Cooperative Insurance Companies Control Law, its Implementing Regulation, the Banking Control Law ... shall govern whatever is not provided for therein"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 1):** "based on the powers vested to SAMA by the Cooperative Insurance Companies Control Law promulgated by Royal Decree No. (M/32) dated 02/06/1424H"

### [[Insurance Company Obligations in Bancassurance]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When allocating responsibility between the two counterparties, recognise that the Rules also bind the insurance Company, not only the distributing Bank. The Company is the SAMA-licensed insurer under M/32 whose approval the Bank must obtain to expand channels, and the Rules govern 'the relationship between the Company and the Bank.' Conclude that compliance is a shared, two-sided obligation set: the insurer retains duties (including consenting to channel expansion and controlling honest, transparent, fair marketing/distribution) and cannot fully offload conduct risk to the Bank.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Objective):** "regulate Bancassurance Activities and practices in Saudi Arabia and the relationship between the Company and the Bank"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6):** "The Bank must obtain the Company's approval to expand the marketing and distribution channels ... along with notifying SAMA"
