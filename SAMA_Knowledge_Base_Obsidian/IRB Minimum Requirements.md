# IRB Minimum Requirements

## Connections

### [[Definition of Default]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When applying the IRB approach, the definition of default and the IRB minimum requirements operate together: the CR2/CR9 default-related templates require banks to disclose and backtest a definition of default, while Chapter 16 sets the entry and ongoing conditions (rating system design, risk quantification, validation) that make IRB estimates usable for capital. A bank's default definition must be consistent with the risk-quantification and validation minimums to keep its IRB permission. In practice you would check that the default definition used in PD backtesting is the same one governed by the Chapter 16 minimum requirements, since inconsistency undermines the IRB approval.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 184 / Para 16.1):** "This chapter presents the minimum requirements for entry and on-going use of the internal ratings-based (IRB) approach"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 816 / Template CR9):** "the template compares the PD used in IRB capital calculations with the effective default rates of bank obligors"

### [[IRB Approach]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank may lawfully use the IRB approach, do not treat the approach and its minimum requirements as separable — use of IRB is conditional on continuing satisfaction of the Chapter 16 minimum requirements for entry and on-going use. The IRB rows in the reporting/RWA framework only recognize IRB-computed RWA where the bank has SAMA approval, and that approval rests on the minimum-requirement set (rating system design, governance, validation, risk quantification, disclosure). A reviewer should conclude that a bank failing any minimum requirement cannot rely on its IRB numbers, and that these requirements cut across asset classes rather than applying class-by-class.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 184 / 16.1–16.2):** "This chapter presents the minimum requirements for entry and on-going use of the internal ratings-based (IRB) approach... cut across asset classes."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 755):** "RWA for modelled approaches that banks have SAMA approval to use... subject to the credit risk IRB approaches (F-IRB, A-IRB and supervisory slotting)."

### [[Rating System Design]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank qualifies to use the IRB approach for credit-risk capital, treat 'rating system design' not as a standalone concept but as one of the mandatory minimum-requirement sections a bank must satisfy at entry and on an ongoing basis. Section 16.1 enumerates rating system design as component (3) of the minimum requirements, and 16.9–16.34 specify its enforceable content (two-dimensional ratings, documentation, no cherry-picking). For a compliance decision you would conclude that failure to meet the rating-system-design standards is failure of the IRB minimum requirements overall — the bank cannot use, or must lose, IRB treatment for the affected exposures.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 184 / 16.1):** "the minimum requirements for entry and on-going use of the internal ratings-based (IRB) approach ... (3) Rating system design"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 187 / 16.9-16.10):** "Banks must demonstrate that each system used for IRB purposes is in compliance with the minimum requirements at the outset and on an ongoing basis."
