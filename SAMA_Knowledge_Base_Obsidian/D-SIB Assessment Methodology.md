# D-SIB Assessment Methodology

## Connections

### [[D-SIBs Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining whether a bank falls within the D-SIB regime, treat the Assessment Methodology not as guidance but as the operative mechanism the Framework enacts: the covering circular states SAMA 'has developed the enclosed assessment methodology' and mandates its implementation from 1 January 2016. The Framework is the instrument that gives the methodology binding force, so the indicators (size, interconnectedness, substitutability, complexity) are the exclusive criteria for designation. For a compliance decision, you cannot separate the two — designation risk turns entirely on the methodology the Framework incorporates, and its effective date is the trigger for HLA obligations.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4, para 3):** "SAMA has developed an assessment methodology based on an indicator-based measurement approach for assessing and designating D-SIBs in Saudi Arabia"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 1 (covering circular)):** "SAMA has developed the enclosed assessment methodology providing for an indicator-based measurement approach ... implemented effective from 1 January 2016"

### [[G-SIBs Framework (BCBS)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When interpreting SAMA's D-SIB methodology, treat the BCBS G-SIB framework as its source and benchmark: SAMA states its approach 'is consistent with the BCBS D-SIB assessment methodology,' which the BCBS itself derived by extending the G-SIB rules text to domestic banks. This means the BCBS standard is a legitimate interpretive aid where SAMA's text is silent or ambiguous. For a compliance decision, however, note that SAMA calibrated indicators to the Saudi banking system — so the KSA methodology and thresholds govern, and BCBS text informs but does not override the domestic rule.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4, para 3):** "consistent with the BCBS D-SIB assessment methodology ... calibrated to reflect the different aspects and operational dynamics of the Saudi Arabian Banking System"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4, para 1):** "The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) ... issued the rules text on the assessment methodology for global systemically important banks (G-SIBs)"

### [[Scoring and Bucketing]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing where a bank lands in the D-SIB regime, treat Scoring and Bucketing as the operative output stage of the Assessment Methodology: the methodology produces a score, and banks 'with a score above a certain level (the cut-off score) will automatically be classified as D-SIBs.' The bucket a bank is allocated to (four buckets plus a 4+1) directly determines the size of its HLA charge. For a compliance decision, note two things: designation is automatic above the 10% cut-off, and SAMA retains discretion to add banks scoring below it — so a below-cut-off score does not guarantee exemption.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4, para 3):** "an indicator-based measurement approach for assessing and designating D-SIBs in Saudi Arabia"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6, paras 8-9):** "banks with a score above a certain level (the cut-off score) will automatically be classified as D-SIBs ... allocated to a bucket based on their scores"
