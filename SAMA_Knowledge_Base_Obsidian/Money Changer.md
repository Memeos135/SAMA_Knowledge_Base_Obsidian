# Money Changer

## Connections

### [[Licensing Requirements and Procedures]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether an entity may lawfully offer money changing services in KSA, read the licensing requirements as the gateway that defines who qualifies as a 'Money Changer'—only an establishment licensed by SAMA under these Rules holds that status. Article 6's conditions (legal form, eligible founders, feasibility study, bank guarantee) attach directly to the defined term, and continued licensing depends on maintaining those requirements (Article 30 grounds for termination). Conclude that Money Changer obligations throughout the Rules apply only to licensed entities, and that any unlicensed party performing these activities is instead caught by the Article 4 prohibition rather than the operational regime.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Art 1):** "Money Changer: An establishment licensed by SAMA to conduct Money Changing Business in accordance with the provisions of the Rules."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Art 6):** "The application for a license to practice Money Changing Business shall be submitted to SAMA. The following are the requirements for issuing the license"

### [[Outsourcing to Third Party]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When a Money Changer delegates any function to a service provider, do not treat outsourcing as a transfer of regulatory responsibility—Article 15 keeps the Money Changer accountable for compliance with the Rules for outsourced work, subject to SAMA's outsourcing instructions. The 'Third Party' is a defined term (service provider including its owners, employees, and affiliated entities), and the Rules require a written fixed-term contract and prohibit dealing with a Third Party proven to have supplied false information. Conclude that outsourcing arrangements must be assessed as extensions of the Money Changer's own obligations (including AML/CTF and customer protection), and that the licensee remains the liable party before SAMA regardless of who performs the function.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11 / Art 15):** "the Money Changer shall be responsible for complying with the provisions of these Rules when outsourcing any functions to a Third Party"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Art 1):** "Third Party: A service provider to whom some work and functions are outsourced. This includes the owners of the service provider, his/her employees and contract employees"

### [[Rules Regulating Money Changing Business]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining who bears the obligations under this instrument, note that 'Money Changer' is the defined regulated subject — 'An establishment licensed by SAMA to conduct Money Changing Business' — so every duty, penalty, and license-termination trigger in the Rules attaches to that defined entity. Article 14 concentrates responsibility on the Money Changer's management to maintain oversight, controls, and transaction monitoring, and the termination grounds (Page 19) make continued licensing conditional on meeting these standards and cooperating with SAMA inspection. A reviewer should therefore anchor scope analysis to the Money Changer definition and confirm that management-level accountability and record/reporting duties are being met, since SAMA may revoke the license for failure to maintain requirements or for conduct detrimental to customers.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 19 / termination grounds):** "If the Money Changer fails to maintain one of the license requirements... If SAMA discovers that the Money Changer's conduct of business has been detrimental to the interest of its Customers."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / Art 1):** "Money Changer: An establishment licensed by SAMA to conduct Money Changing Business in accordance with the provisions of the Rules."
