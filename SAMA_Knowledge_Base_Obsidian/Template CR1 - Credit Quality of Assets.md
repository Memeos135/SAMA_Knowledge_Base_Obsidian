# Template CR1 - Credit Quality of Assets

## Connections

### [[SCRE - Minimum Capital Requirements for Credit Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When completing or reviewing Template CR1, apply SCRE as the substantive standard that defines what counts as credit-risk exposure and how it is measured: CR1's scope is drawn from the Basel/SCRE credit-risk framework (excluding securitisation and counterparty credit risk, which are reported elsewhere), so eligibility and boundary questions are resolved by reference to SCRE, not the template's cells. CR1 reports carrying values of on- and off-balance-sheet assets giving rise to credit risk under that framework. Conclude that CR1's default definitions and exclusions must be reconciled against SCRE, and that positions carved out by SCRE (e.g. securitisation) belong in section 21, not CR1.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 797):** "Gross carrying values: on- and off-balance sheet items that give rise to a credit risk exposure according to the Basel framework."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 751):** "Credit risk (excluding counterparty credit risk): RWA and capital requirements according to the credit risk standard of the Basel framework (SCRE)"

### [[Template CR2 - Changes in Stock of Defaulted Loans]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining Pillar 3 credit-quality disclosures, treat CR1 and CR2 as a linked pair rather than standalone templates, because both fall under section 19.2.1 'General information about credit risk' and share a common defined term. CR1 gives the point-in-time stock (gross carrying values, defaulted vs non-defaulted, allowances), while CR2 reconciles the movement in that defaulted stock over the period; the instructions expressly tie the two, stating that for a standardised-approach bank the defaulted exposures 'in Templates CR1 and CR2 should correspond' to exposures past due more than 90 days. A reader should check that the defaulted-exposure figure disclosed in CR1 is consistent with the closing balance derived in CR2, and use the same default definition and off-balance-sheet treatment across both.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 797):** "Template CR1: Credit quality of assets ... Provide a comprehensive picture of the credit quality of a bank's (on- and off-balance sheet) assets."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 798):** "the default exposures in Templates CR1 and CR2 should correspond to exposures that are "past due for more than 90 days", as stated in SCRE7.96"

### [[Template CR3 Credit risk mitigation techniques overview|Template CR3: Credit risk mitigation techniques overview]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the credit-risk disclosure suite, note that CR1 (credit quality of assets) and CR3 (credit risk mitigation techniques overview) both sit within section 19 and are both mandatory for all banks, but address different aspects — CR1 gives gross/net carrying values and defaulted-vs-non-defaulted quality, while CR3 shows how exposures are secured (collateral, financial guarantees, credit derivatives). The link is co-membership in the same credit-risk disclosure section rather than a computational tie. Conclude that both must be produced regardless of IRB approval (unlike the section 19.2.4 IRB templates), and read CR1's exposure/quality picture alongside CR3's mitigation coverage rather than in isolation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 797 / Template CR1):** "Provide a comprehensive picture of the credit quality of a bank's (on- and off-balance sheet) assets. Scope of application: The template is mandatory for all banks."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 794 / section 19.2.2(g)):** "Credit risk mitigation: ... Template CR3 - Credit risk mitigation techniques – overview"
- **Caveat:** Link reflects shared section membership; the two templates are not stated to reconcile numerically, so do not assume a direct figure tie.
