# Implementing Regulation for Payments Law (SAMA 1430)

## Connections

### [[Complaints and Disputes]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing dispute-handling obligations of a licensed PSP, read them as an integral chapter of this Implementing Regulation rather than a stand-alone regime, because the Regulation's own table of contents lists 'Complaints and Disputes' (الباب الحادي عشر: الشكاوى والمنازعات) as a titled section of the same instrument. The Regulation also defines 'Complaint' (الشكوى) as an expression of dissatisfaction submitted to the licensee by the customer or consumer, fixing who may complain and to whom. A compliance reader should therefore treat complaint-handling duties as enforceable obligations flowing from the Payments Law regime and check this chapter's articles for the specific procedures binding licensees.

### [[Customer Protection and Financial Inclusion]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping consumer-facing duties for a PSP, treat 'Customer Protection and Financial Inclusion' as a substantive chapter of this Regulation (الباب الخامس: حماية العملاء والشمول المالي) rather than aspirational policy, since it is enumerated as a titled Book with its own first chapter on customer protection. Note the Regulation distinguishes 'customer' (العميل) from 'consumer' (المستهلك) in its complaint definition, so protection obligations may attach to both categories. A reader should map which protection articles bind the licensee and confirm which counterparty class each obligation covers before treating any single term as exhaustive.

### [[Licensing of Payment Service Providers]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining whether an entity may lawfully provide payment services in KSA, treat licensing as a threshold requirement embedded in this Regulation, since the Regulation contains a dedicated chapter on licensing requirements for payment service providers (الفصل الثالث - متطلبات الترخيص لمقدمي خدمات المدفوعات) and defines 'Licence' (الترخيص) as the authorisation granted by the Central Bank. The covering circular confirms SAMA mandates that PSPs and payment-system operators subject to the Law and Regulation comply with all its provisions. A reader should conclude that unlicensed provision is not permitted and should check the licensing chapter and the 'controller' definition for who must be assessed on ownership/control.

### [[Payment Services]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When deciding whether a given activity falls within the Payments Law regime, anchor the analysis in this Regulation's own scope provisions, because it defines its terms by reference to the Payments Law (النظام: نظام المدفوعات وخدماتها, Royal Decree M/26) and dedicates Book Six to related payment services (الباب السادس: خدمات المدفوعات ذات الصلة). Whether an activity is a 'payment service' determines whether licensing, customer-protection and complaint obligations bite at all. A reader should first classify the activity against the Regulation's payment-services definitions before applying downstream duties, and rely on the Law's defined meanings where the Regulation does not restate them.

### [[Risk Management and Compliance]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a payment service provider's governance obligations, treat 'Risk Management and Compliance' as an enforceable chapter of the Implementing Regulation, not a standalone policy topic.
