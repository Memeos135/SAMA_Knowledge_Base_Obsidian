# Margin Requirements for Non-centrally Cleared Derivatives

## Connections

### [[BCBS-IOSCO Margin Requirements for Non-centrally Cleared Derivatives]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When interpreting SAMA's margin requirements for non-centrally cleared derivatives, read them as SAMA's domestic adoption of the BCBS-IOSCO framework, so the international paper is the authoritative reference for phase-in timing and scope. The SAMA document expressly states its requirements apply in a phased manner 'as described in' the BCBS-IOSCO paper and mirrors its Elements structure and thresholds. Conclude that where SAMA's text is silent or references the phase-in schedule, the BCBS-IOSCO paper governs the reading, though the SAR 30 billion covered-entity threshold and other localized figures are SAMA-specific and take precedence.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 16 / Element 8, para 49):** "applicable in a phased manner from 1 September 2016 as described in Margin requirements for Non-centrally Cleared Derivatives paper issued by Basel Committee... and IOSCO"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 16 / Element 8):** "Margin requirements for Non-centrally Cleared Derivatives paper issued by Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) and Board of the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO)"

### [[Eligible Collateral]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing what a covered entity may lawfully collect to satisfy SAMA margin obligations, treat 'Eligible Collateral' as a scoping constraint on the framework, not a free choice. Element 4 of this document limits acceptable collateral to assets permitted under the standardised approach for credit risk in SAMA's Risk-based Capital Framework, subject to prescribed haircuts and 'wrong way risk' exclusions (e.g. securities issued by the counterparty or its related parties). Conclude that collateral eligibility is governed by an external SAMA framework and cannot be self-defined; verify each posted asset against the standardised credit-risk list and Appendix B haircuts before relying on it to meet a margin call.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Element 1):** "these margin requirements apply to all non-centrally cleared derivatives"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 11 / para 32):** "SAMA only considers eligible collaterals, which are allowed under the standardised approach for credit risk under the Risk-based Capital Framework adopted by SAMA"

### [[Initial Margin]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** To determine a covered entity's obligations under this framework, read 'Initial Margin' as one of the two mandatory exchanges the document imposes on covered entities engaging in non-centrally cleared derivatives. Element 2 requires exchange of initial and variation margin, and Element 3 sets baseline amounts and permits either a SAMA-approved quantitative model or the standardised schedule (with no 'cherry picking' between methods). Conclude that initial-margin methodology choice is not discretionary in the ordinary sense — model use requires explicit SAMA approval and must be applied consistently per asset class; check approval status before treating a model-based figure as compliant.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Element 2 para 11):** "All covered entities that engage in non-centrally cleared derivatives must calculate, balance and exchange, on a bilateral basis"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 9 / para 25):** "it will not be allowed to switch between model- and schedule-based margin calculations in an effort to “cherry pick” the most favourable initial margin terms"

### [[Variation Margin]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping the exchange duties this framework creates, note that 'Variation Margin' is the second mandatory margin type and applies more broadly than initial margin — including to all components of cross-currency swaps. Element 2 requires covered entities to calculate, balance and exchange the full amount of variation margin bilaterally. Conclude that variation-margin obligations are not subject to the SAR 30 billion threshold or asset-class carve-outs in the same way; where a transaction is in scope at all, full two-way variation margin exchange is expected, so do not assume the physically-settled-FX carve-out for cross-currency swap principal extends to variation margin.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Element 1 para 6):** "The variation margin requirements that are described below apply to all components of cross-currency swaps"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / Element 2 para 11):** "must calculate, balance and exchange, on a bilateral basis, the full amount of variation margin"
