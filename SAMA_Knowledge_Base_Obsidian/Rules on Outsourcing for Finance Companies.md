# Rules on Outsourcing for Finance Companies

## Connections

### [[Finance Companies Control Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When judging the enforceability of the Outsourcing Rules, treat them as subordinate implementing instructions under the Finance Companies Control Law — breach is not a mere policy lapse but a statutory violation. The Rules expressly state they are set under the FCC Law (Royal Decree M/51), must be read in conjunction with the Law and its Implementing Regulations, and that non-compliance 'shall be deemed a violation of the Finance Companies Control Law and its Implementing Regulation.' So assess outsourcing breaches through the FCC Law's supervisory and penalty regime, and read the Rules together with the Law rather than as a self-contained code.

- **Grounding — related node (SAMA_EN_1949_VER1 / Ch 2 (Page 4)):** "These Rules shall be read in conjunction with the Finance Companies Control Law and its Implementing Regulations..."

### [[Financial Consumer Protection Principles and Rules]] — `references` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a finance company's outsourcing obligations, don't treat outsourcing rules as self-contained: the Outsourcing Rules require confidentiality safeguards for 'consumers' data' and verification of the External Service Provider's compliance with 'relevant laws, regulations and instructions', which appears to fold in SAMA's Financial Consumer Protection Principles and Rules whenever outsourced functions (e.g. debt collection, customer care/complaint handling) touch consumers. Because both instruments use the SAMA-defined 'consumer' concept and the finance company remains responsible for any non-compliance by the provider, consumer-protection duties do not shift to the outsourced party. The link is inferred rather than textually cited, so treat it as a lead: verify the primary text of both documents to confirm that consumer-protection conduct rules survive outsourcing before relying on this reading.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Rule 11):** "establish proper safeguards to protect the confidentiality of consumers' data ... upon the expiration or termination of the outsourcing contract"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / Rule 11(b)):** "employees of the third party ... sign the consumer information confidentiality form, ensure not to disclose personal information"
- **Caveat:** Relation is INFERRED; neither document cites the other by name. Confirm in primary text that consumer-protection duties bind outsourced functions before relying.

### [[Material Functions]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining whether SAMA's prior non-objection is required before an outsourcing, the classification of the function as 'Material' is the trigger — so apply the defined test first. Material Functions are defined as any function whose default or disruption may affect the company's activities, reputation or financial situation, or that involves sharing/transferring/processing/storing consumers' data; the Rules then require the company to classify each function and to seek SAMA's written non-objection at least 30 working days before outsourcing or renewing material functions. Conclude that the definition (plus the non-exhaustive Appendix 1 examples such as internal audit, debt collection, data storage) governs the approval obligation, so a misclassification as non-material to avoid the 30-day non-objection step is itself a compliance risk.
- **Grounding — this node (SAMA_EN_1949_VER1 / Ch 6 para 15 (Page 8)):** "Prior to outsourcing or renewing outsourcing of material functions... the finance company should request SAMA's non-objection in writing at least 30 working days prior..."
- **Grounding — related node (SAMA_EN_1949_VER1 / Ch 1 Definitions (Page 3)):** "Material Functions: Any function that the default or disruption of which may have an impact on the finance company's activities, reputation or the financial situation..."
