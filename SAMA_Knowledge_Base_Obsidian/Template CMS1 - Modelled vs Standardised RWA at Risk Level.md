# Template CMS1 - Modelled vs Standardised RWA at Risk Level

## Connections

### [[Output Floor Requirements]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When identifying how a bank must evidence its output-floor position, treat Template CMS1 as the disclosure vehicle that operationalises the floor's core comparison, because CMS1 sets out RWA under modelled approaches (cell a) against RWA under standardised approaches (cell b) at risk level — the same modelled-vs-standardised split that defines the output floor. The floor's prohibition on modelled approaches (IRB, SEC-IRBA, IMA, VaR, IMM) in the floor base is exactly what CMS1 makes transparent. You should conclude that the floored RWA logic and the CMS1 template must be read together to determine both the calculation and the reporting of the floor.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 755):** "RWA for modelled approaches that banks have SAMA approval to use (cell 1/a) ... RWA for portfolios where standardised approaches are used (cell 1/b)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 734 / 5.8):** "the following approaches are not permitted to be used ... in the calculation of the base of the output floor: (1) IRB approach to credit risk; (2) SEC-IRBA; (3) IMA for market risk"

### [[Template CMS2 - Modelled vs Standardised RWA at Asset Class Level]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the modelled-vs-standardised comparison disclosures, treat CMS1 and CMS2 as a paired requirement of the same section: CMS1 gives the comparison at the risk level and CMS2 elaborates the same comparison for credit risk at asset-class level. Both are triggered only where SAMA has approved the bank to use IRB and/or IMA, so a bank without such approval need complete neither. Conclude that CMS2 is a more granular decomposition of the credit-risk portion of CMS1 and the two should reconcile, and confirm SAMA approval status before assessing whether either applies.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 753 / section 13.2):** "Template CMS1 – Comparison of modelled and standardised RWA at risk level; Template CMS2 – Comparison of modelled and standardised RWA for credit risk at asset class level"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 753 / section 13.3):** "Template CMS1 provides the disclosure of RWA calculated according to the full standardised approach... Template CMS2 further elaborates on the comparison between RWA computed under..."

### [[Template OV1 Overview of RWA|Template OV1: Overview of RWA]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping Pillar 3 disclosure obligations for a SAMA-supervised bank, treat CMS1 as dependent on and cross-referenced to OV1: OV1 gives the authoritative overview of total RWA by risk type, and CMS1 reuses OV1's row structure (equity investments in funds rows 12-14, settlement risk row 15, banking/trading book switch row 23, sub-threshold deductions row 25) to define its 'residual RWA' scope. The instructions in CMS1 explicitly define residual RWA by pointing to those OV1 rows, so the two templates must be read together and reconcile. Conclude that CMS1 figures should tie back to OV1, and that CMS1 is only completed where SAMA has approved IRB/IMA use, unlike OV1 which is mandatory for all banks.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 756 / Residual RWA row 7):** "RWA not captured within rows 1 to 6 (ie the RWA arising from equity investments in funds (rows 12 to 14 in Template OV1), settlement risk (row 15 in Template OV1)...)"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 745 / section 12.6):** "Template OV1 provides an overview of total RWA forming the denominator of the risk-based capital requirements."
