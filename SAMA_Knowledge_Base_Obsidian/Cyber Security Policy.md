# Cyber Security Policy

## Connections

### [[Cyber Security Leadership and Governance]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating governance compliance, treat the Cyber Security Policy as the required output of the leadership-and-governance domain: the board must set the intent and the CISO must develop and maintain the policy. The Framework specifies mandatory policy contents (definition of cyber security, objectives and scope, statement of board intent, defined responsibilities, references to supporting standards, and requirements covering classification, risk assessment, breach reporting and BCM). Conclude that a governance review should verify the policy exists, is board-endorsed and CISO-maintained, and contains every enumerated element, since gaps here undermine all subordinate standards and procedures.

### [[Cyber Security Strategy]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating the documentation hierarchy, treat the cyber security policy and strategy as distinct but linked instruments both owned by the CISO, with the policy carrying Board intent and the strategy setting direction. The Framework lists both as CISO develop-and-maintain deliverables and requires the policy to include a statement of the board's intent and reference supporting standards. For a compliance check you would confirm the policy and strategy are consistent and that the policy contains the mandated elements; misalignment between them signals an incomplete governance framework.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 15):** "The cyber security policy should include: ... a statement of the board's intent, supporting the cyber security objectives"
