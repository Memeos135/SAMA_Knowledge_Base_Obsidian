# Interest Rate Risk

## Connections

### [[General Market Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When distinguishing risk types for market risk capital, understand that general market risk is a cross-cutting component (broad market-wide price movement) that applies to interest-rate instruments alongside specific risk, and interest rate risk is one of the defined risk classes to which it attaches. The glossary anchors 'general interest rate risk' as a listed risk class, and the simplified-approach text (14.75) confirms option carve-outs generate capital incorporating 'both general market risk and specific risk' added back to the interest-rate category. For a capital calculation you would therefore treat general market risk and interest rate risk as related layers — general market risk is measured within the interest-rate class, not as a substitute for it — and ensure both the general and specific components are captured for interest-rate positions.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 (glossary)):** "Risk class: A defined list of risks... general interest rate risk, credit spread risk (non-securitisation)... FX risk, equity risk and commodity risk."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 162 / 14.75):** "subject to separately calculated capital requirements that incorporate both general market risk and specific risk."

### [[Simplified Standardised Approach]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When applying the simplified standardised approach for an eligible bank, interest rate risk is the first of the four defined risk categories covered, and the framework also treats interest rate risk as a component within commodities and options measurement rather than only a standalone book. Paragraph 14.1(1) scopes interest rate risk into [14.3]–[14.73], while 14.73 adds a 3% gross-position charge to protect against basis, interest rate and forward gap risk in commodities. Accordingly, capitalise a bank's interest rate positions under the simplified rules and check that interest rate elements embedded in forward/derivative positions (e.g. commodities carry) are captured through the additional charges, not omitted.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 134 / 14.1(1)):** "[14.3] to [14.73] deal with interest rate, equity, foreign exchange (FX) and commodities risk."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 161 / 14.73):** "to protect the bank against basis risk, interest rate risk and forward gap risk under the simplified approach... an additional capital requirement equivalent to 3% of the bank's gross positions."

### [[Specific Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping market-risk capital charges under this SAMA framework, treat general interest rate risk and specific risk as two distinct components that must be capitalised separately, not merged. The framework's defined 'Risk class' list separates general interest rate risk from credit spread (specific) risk, and worked provisions (e.g. first-to-default credit derivative treatment) compute specific-risk charges independently of general rate movements. Conclude that an interest-rate instrument's capital requirement will draw on both risk categories, and verify which chapter/section governs each rather than assuming one charge covers both.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5):** "a bond ... has risk positions in general interest rate risk, credit spread risk (non-securitisation) and FX risk"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5):** "Risk class: ... general interest rate risk, credit spread risk (non-securitisation) ... FX risk, equity risk and commodity risk"
