# Freezing of Bank Accounts

## Connections

### [[Chapter II Supervisory Rules and Controls|Chapter II: Supervisory Rules and Controls]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining when and how a bank must freeze an account, locate those triggers within Chapter II's supervisory controls, which set out dormancy classification, document-expiry freezing and balance-transfer sequencing as enforceable bank duties. The freezing provisions (dormant after 24 months, freezing on ID/document expiry, transfer to unified then suspense accounts) sit alongside the electronic record and disclosure controls in the same chapter and apply across the customer categories the Rules enumerate. You should conclude that freezing is a mandatory status change tied to defined time triggers rather than a discretionary risk action, and check the specific document-expiry and dormancy periods applicable to the customer type before altering account status.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 16 / 5.2.2):** "Accounts shall be considered dormant after (24) calendar months from the date of the last recorded debit transaction"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 2 / TOC):** "Chapter II Supervisory Rules and Controls 9"

### [[Electronic Record System]] — `shares_data_with` [INFERRED]
- **What this link tells you:** If you are assessing how a bank must respond to a SAMA disclosure or enforcement (freezing) request, treat the electronic record as the source that makes that response possible: the identification and account data captured under the electronic-record rules is the same dataset the bank must search and act upon when SAMA orders blocking, compulsory deduction or transfer. The link appears to reflect that the unified electronic database of accounts underpins the Compliance Department's ability to 'timely access the customer identification data' and to locate 'all relationships between the bank and the customer' for enforcement, but the two provisions sit in different chapters and the relationship is inferred rather than stated as a cross-reference. Conclude that adequacy of the electronic record is a precondition for lawful, complete freezing/disclosure responses — but verify the primary text before treating this as a formal linkage.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 22 / sec. 10.1):** "banks shall search for all relationships between the bank and the customer, including all active; closed and suspended accounts, inoperative accounts, deposits, valuable"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 9 / Ch. II sec. 1):** "all banks shall establish an electronic registration system ... This system serves as an electronic record ... as a basis for opening, operating, and following up bank accounts."
- **Caveat:** Relationship is INFERRED; the provisions are in separate chapters and no explicit cross-reference ties the electronic record to freezing/enforcement.
