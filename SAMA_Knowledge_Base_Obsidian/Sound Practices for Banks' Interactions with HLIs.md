# Sound Practices for Banks' Interactions with HLIs

## Connections

### [[Internal Control Guidelines for Commercial Banks]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing how a Saudi bank must manage exposures to highly leveraged institutions or hedge funds, do not read the Basle HLI 'sound practices' paper in isolation — SAMA's transmittal expressly requires those exposures to be managed 'in accordance with the Internal Control Guidelines for Commercial Banks issued by SAMA.' That makes the HLI paper an additional, HLI-specific overlay on top of the pre-existing SAMA internal control regime and the bank's own credit policies, not a standalone or substitute framework. Conclude that both instruments apply cumulatively, and check the SAMA Internal Control Guidelines as the baseline against which HLI-specific practices are layered.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Preface):** "The management of credit risk in respect of HLIs involves the same principles as management of credit risk in general, but must also take account of the particular types of counterparty risk"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 1):** "We expect these exposures to be managed in accordance with the Internal Control Guidelines for Commercial Banks issued by SAMA, and prudent internal credit policies"

### [[Potential Future Exposure (PFE)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating whether a bank's counterparty credit-risk controls for HLIs meet SAMA's expectation, treat Potential Future Exposure (PFE) as a required measurement concept the sound-practices paper mandates, not an optional analytic. The paper states banks 'must have an effective measure of PFE' to convert derivatives to loan-equivalent amounts, to set binding counterparty limits, and to run stress tests under volatile conditions. Conclude that adequate HLI exposure measurement and limit-setting must be anchored in a defensible PFE methodology, and that simply applying higher confidence intervals may be treated as insufficient under stressed conditions.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 8 / II):** "methodologies for measuring current and future exposure, effective limit setting procedures, and ongoing monitoring of both the firm's exposure"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 12):** "Banks must have an effective measure of PFE which gives an accurate picture of the extent of their involvement with the counterparty"
