# Re-ageing and Overdrafts Treatment

## Connections

### [[Definition of Default]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether an exposure must be flagged as defaulted for capital and disclosure purposes, treat the disclosure-template definition of default and the re-ageing/overdraft rules as one coherent trigger set, not two independent tests. Both sit within the same SAMA credit-risk framework (SAMA_EN_3487): CR1/CR2 require banks to disclose defaulted exposures and to state their definition of default, while paragraphs 16.73–16.74 fix the operational triggers — 90-day past-due, re-ageing limits, and overdraft limit breaches counted from 90 to 180 days. The consequence is that the default figures you disclose in CR1/CR2 must be consistent with the re-ageing and overdraft mechanics of 16.73–16.74; you cannot report a re-aged or over-limit exposure as non-defaulted if those provisions would have triggered default.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 207 / 16.74):** "if the account were not brought under the limit after 90 to 180 days... it would be considered as defaulted."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 797 / Template CR1):** "Banks must include their definition of default in an accompanying narrative."
