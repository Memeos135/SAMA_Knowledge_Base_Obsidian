# Fraud Risk Appetite

## Connections

### [[Fraud Risk Assessment]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a Member Organisation's Fraud Risk Appetite is validly set, check that it derives from the Fraud Risk Assessment: the framework requires appetite to be 'based on the outcome of the Fraud Risk Assessment' and aligned to overall enterprise risk appetite. This creates a dependency — appetite thresholds and limits are only defensible if grounded in identified inherent and residual fraud risks. Conclude that a Fraud Risk Appetite statement disconnected from current FRA output is a compliance gap, and appetite reviews should be tested against the most recent assessment.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 30 / 4.1.3 (Fraud Risk Appetite)):** "The Member Organisation Fraud Risk Appetite should be based on the outcome of the Fraud Risk Assessment and aligned to the overall risk appetite of the organisation."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 28 / 4.1.2 Fraud Risk Assessment):** "Determination of the residual risk of fraud that the Member Organisation remains exposed to following testing of implemented controls."

### [[Fraud Risk Management Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When testing governance of fraud risk tolerance, note that Fraud Risk Appetite is a required element operating within the Fraud Risk Management Framework: the FRMF is the overarching structure that must be defined, approved, implemented and aligned to enterprise risk management, and appetite (with its board endorsement, thresholds and crisis-escalation triggers) is one of its constituent controls. This means appetite obligations inherit the framework's approval and alignment requirements. Conclude that a compliance review of Fraud Risk Appetite should verify it sits within, and is consistent with, an approved FRMF rather than existing as a detached statement.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 30 / 4.1.3 (Fraud Risk Appetite)):** "Fraud Risk Appetite should be reviewed on at least an annual basis and be formally endorsed by the Board."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 26 / 4.1 Risk Management):** "A Fraud Risk Management Framework should be defined, approved and implemented, and should be aligned with the Member Organisation's enterprise risk management process."

### [[Key Risk Indicators]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a Member Organisation satisfies SAMA's Counter-Fraud requirements, do not treat Fraud Risk Appetite and KRIs as independent controls: the standard requires KRIs to be defined specifically to measure position against the agreed Fraud Risk Appetite and to flag rising exposure early. Because control 4.1.4 ties KRIs directly to appetite thresholds (which in turn drive escalation to Senior Management and crisis management when breached), a firm claiming compliance must show its KRIs are calibrated to those appetite limits, not to generic metrics. Conclude that absence of KRIs mapped to defined appetite thresholds is a gap that undermines both provisions.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 30 / 4.1.3):** "Fraud Risk Appetite ... should put in place measures with associated thresholds and limits"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 30 / 4.1.4):** "monitor KRIs to measure and evaluate position against agreed Fraud Risk Appetite and provide an early indication of increasing fraud risk exposure"
