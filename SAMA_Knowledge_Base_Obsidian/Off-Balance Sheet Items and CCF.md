# Off-Balance Sheet Items and CCF

## Connections

### [[Counterparty Credit Risk (CCR) Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When measuring exposure for capital, you must decide whether an off-balance sheet item is treated under CCF rules or diverted to the CCR Framework, because exposures giving rise to counterparty credit risk (OTC derivatives, exchange-traded derivatives, long settlement transactions, SFTs) are carved out of the ordinary off-balance sheet CCF treatment and instead computed under the CCR Framework's rules. Paragraph 7.94 expressly routes such exposures to chapters 3–8 of the CCR Framework, and the advanced-approach EAD provisions likewise exclude counterparty-credit-risk transactions. Conclude that you should not apply generic CCFs to derivative/SFT exposures — classify them first, then apply SA-CCR or the internal models method as directed.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 206 / 16.88):** "the estimation of EAD for off-balance sheet items (excluding transactions that expose banks to counterparty credit risk as set out in chapter 5 of the Counterparty Credit Risk (CCR) framework)."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 48 / 7.94):** "For exposures that give rise to counterparty credit risk... the exposure amount... is to be calculated under the rules set out in chapters 3 to 8 in The Counterparty Credit Risk (CCR) Framework."

### [[Credit Derivatives]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When capitalising credit derivatives, note they sit adjacent to the off-balance sheet/CCF rules but follow a distinct treatment: the credit derivatives provisions (7.95) apply protection-provider capital rules and reference 7.94's CCR routing, while the off-balance sheet section (7.86–7.93) governs commitments and CCFs. Both are in the same standardized-approach exposure chapter and share the 7.86 commitment definition carried into the IRB EAD rules. Conclude that a first-/nth-to-default derivative on which the bank provides protection is not treated as an ordinary off-balance sheet commitment under CCF; apply the aggregation-of-risk-weights method in 7.95 rather than a CCF.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 126 / 12.33):** "The types of instruments and the CCFs applied to them under the F-IRB approach are the same as those in the standardized approach, as set out in paragraphs 7.86 to 7.93."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 48 / 7.95):** "A bank providing credit protection through a first-to-default or second-to-default credit derivative is subject to capital requirements on such instruments."
