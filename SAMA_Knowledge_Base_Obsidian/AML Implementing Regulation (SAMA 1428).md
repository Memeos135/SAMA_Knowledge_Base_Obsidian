### [[Customer Due Diligence]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping CDD obligations, read this concept as the core enforceable duty set of the Regulation: identify and verify the customer and beneficial owner using reliable, independent sources, with the level of measures set by risk. The Regulation imposes hard consequences for failure — the institution may not open the account or must terminate the relationship, and must consider filing an STR (Art 7). Conclude that CDD is not discretionary and that reliance on a third party (Art 7/10-7/11) does not transfer responsibility; the relying institution must immediately obtain all required information and satisfy itself the relied-upon party is regulated and supervised.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Art 7/2):** "Identify the customer and verify the customer's identity, using reliable, independent source documents, data or information"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 7 / Art 7/8-7/9):** "may not open the account, establish the business relationship or carry out the transaction ... shall terminate the business relationship"

### [[Directorate of Financial Intelligence]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining where suspicious activity must be reported, the Directorate of Financial Intelligence is the mandatory recipient of STRs under this Regulation — including where CDD is aborted to avoid tip-off, in which case the institution must file and state why due diligence was not applied (Art 7/9). The Regulation frames the Directorate as operationally independent and the analytical/receiving node for financial intelligence (Art 17). Conclude that reporting obligations run to this named body specifically, and that opting not to complete CDD does not remove the reporting duty — it triggers it.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 7 / Art 7/9):** "shall submit a suspicious transaction report to the Directorate of financial intelligence, and stating the reasons as to why due diligence was not applied"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 14 / Art 17/4):** "The Directorate of financial intelligence shall be operationally independent and autonomous"

### [[Mutual Legal Assistance]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** This link is relevant mainly for understanding the cross-border enforcement backdrop rather than a direct compliance duty on a financial institution. The Regulation's later articles establish the mutual legal assistance and extradition machinery (Arts 39-42), including that assistance may not be refused on grounds of fiscal matters or secrecy (Art 39/4) and that the Permanent Committee at the Ministry of Interior executes requests (Art 42/1). Conclude that customer/transaction information a firm holds may become subject to international information exchange and confiscation processes, so bank secrecy is not a shield against these state-level obligations; the operative duties here fall on competent authorities, not the firm directly.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 20 / Art 39/4):** "Mutual legal assistance may not be refused solely on the grounds that the offense is considered to involve fiscal matters, or based on secrecy or confidentiality provisions"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 22 / Art 42/1):** "The Permanent Committee for Mutual Legal Assistance at the Ministry of Interior shall arrange for the execution of mutual legal assistance requests"
