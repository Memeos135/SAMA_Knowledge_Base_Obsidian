# Treatment of Guarantees and Credit Derivatives (CRM)

## Connections

### [[CRM Recognition under Advanced Approach]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining capital relief from guarantees or credit derivatives under the IRB advanced approach, read the advanced-approach recognition rules together with the general CRM treatment of guarantees and credit derivatives, because the former builds on and constrains the latter. Paragraph 12.22 fixes a floor — recognized CRM cannot produce a risk weight below a comparable direct exposure to the protection provider and no CRM may raise the capital requirement — mirroring the standardized-approach rule that protected portions take the protection provider's risk weight. Conclude that eligibility limits on credit derivatives (e.g. nth-to-default instruments give no relief) carry into the advanced approach, and check the guarantee/credit-derivative eligibility conditions before assuming any A-IRB capital benefit.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 88 / Para 9.78):** "The protected portion is assigned the risk weight of the protection provider. The uncovered portion of the exposure is assigned the risk weight of the underlying counterparty."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 123 / Para 12.22):** "the adjusted risk weight will not be less than that of a comparable direct exposure to the protection provider... banks may choose not to recognize credit protection if doing so would result in a higher capital requirement."

### [[CRM Recognition under Foundation Approach]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When applying the F-IRB approach to guaranteed or credit-derivative-protected exposures, do not devise a separate eligibility test — the foundation approach expressly follows the standardized-approach treatment of guarantees and credit derivatives. Paragraph 12.23 states the F-IRB approach 'closely follows' the standardized treatment in paragraphs 9.69–9.83, uses the same range of eligible guarantors (plus internally rated companies), and requires meeting 9.69–9.74 for recognition. Conclude that the standardized-approach eligibility and risk-weight-substitution rules govern F-IRB CRM recognition; verify those cross-referenced paragraphs before granting capital relief, rather than relying on the IRB chapter alone.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 61 / Para 9.3):** "No transaction in which credit risk mitigation (CRM) techniques are used shall receive a higher capital requirement than an otherwise identical transaction where such techniques are not used."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 123 / Para 12.23):** "the approach to guarantees and credit derivatives closely follows the treatment under the standardized approach as specified in paragraphs 9.69 to 9.83."

### [[Eligible Purchased Receivables]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When crediting a guarantee or credit derivative against a purchased-receivables pool, apply the general CRM guarantee/credit-derivative rules rather than a bespoke receivables carve-out, because the receivables treatment explicitly channels seller or third-party guarantees back to the existing IRB/CRM framework regardless of whether they cover default risk, dilution risk, or both. The CRM chapter sets which guarantees and credit derivatives are eligible (e.g. only CDS and total return swaps providing guarantee-equivalent protection; nth-to-default derivatives excluded), and those eligibility limits govern any protection asserted on the receivables pool. Conclude that a mitigant which fails the CRM eligibility tests gives no capital relief on the receivables, so check the guarantee against the CRM rules before substituting the guarantor's risk weight.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 88 / 9.76-9.77):** "Only credit default swaps and total return swaps that provide credit protection equivalent to guarantees are eligible for recognition"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 100 / 10.27):** "the purchasing bank's programme for corporate receivables complies with both the criteria for eligible receivables and the minimum operational requirements"
