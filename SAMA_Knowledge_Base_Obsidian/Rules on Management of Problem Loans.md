# Rules on Management of Problem Loans

## Connections

### [[BCBS 403 Prudential Treatment of Problem Assets]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When classifying exposures as non-performing under the Problem Loans Rules, use the BCBS 403 definitions rather than an in-house standard, because the Rules expressly anchor 'non-performing exposures and forbearance' to BCBS 403 as endorsed by SAMA through circular no. 381000099757. This makes the BCBS 403 definitions a binding reference incorporated into the Rules for all banks licensed under the Banking Control Law. Conclude that a bank cannot treat NPE/forbearance definitions as discretionary — the endorsed BCBS 403 text and the SAMA circular govern.

### [[Collateral Management]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When reviewing collateral practices for stressed or non-performing exposures, apply section 7 of the Problem Loans Rules 'throughout the credit process irrespective of the performance on the loan' — the collateral requirements are not confined to enforcement but bind from origination onward. The Rules require board-approved collateral policies, legal-enforceability review (including foreign branches/subsidiaries), and internal-audit review of valuer independence and valuation quality, consistent with SAMA's real-estate valuation requirements. Conclude that a bank must be able to demonstrate documented, enforceable collateral arrangements as a live obligation, and cross-check against SAMA's separate valuation rules referenced within.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Section 1.3):** "These rules are applicable for all banks licensed under Banking Control Law"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 14 / Section 7):** "Banks should ensure proper collateral management and apply the following requirements throughout the credit process irrespective of the performance on the loan"

### [[Early Warning Signals]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a bank's problem-loan prevention duties, treat Early Warning Signals as a defined, mandatory element within the Rules on Management of Problem Loans rather than a discretionary practice: the Rules define EWS in section 1.4 and impose in section 2.1 the obligation to develop clear, robust policies, tools and governance around them, fully integrated into the risk management system. The EWS definition ('Quantitative or qualitative indicators, based on liquidity, profitability, market, collateral and macroeconomic metrics') and the required economic/financial/behavioral indicators are supplied by the same instrument. For compliance assessment, an absent or non-integrated EWS framework is a direct breach of these Rules, so check that EWS coverage is portfolio-specific and demonstrable.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / 1.4):** "Early Warning Signals: Quantitative or qualitative indicators, based on liquidity, profitability, market, collateral and macroeconomic metrics."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / 2.1):** "Banks should develop a clear, robust and demonstrable set of policies, procedures, tools, and governance around the establishment of Early Warning Signals (EWS) which are fully integrated into the bank's risk management system."

### [[NPL Strategy]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When evaluating a bank's NPL governance, note that the NPL Strategy is a mandatory, board-approved instrument required by section 3.1 of the Rules on Management of Problem Loans, not merely good practice: it must set annual quantitative targets over short, medium and long-term horizons and consider all resolution options including restructuring, sales, write-offs, taking collateral, and legal/out-of-court routes. Because the requirement flows from Rules issued under the Banking Control Law and binding on all licensed banks, absence of board approval or of a documented strategy is a direct non-compliance. Check that the strategy is board-approved, target-driven, proportionate to materiality, and integrated into the risk management framework.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Contents 3.1):** "Non-performing loans (NPL) Strategy ... 3.1 Developing the NPL Strategy"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 8 / 3.1):** "Banks should develop and implement an NPL strategy that is approved by the Board of Directors or its delegated authority."

### [[Problem Loans Code of Conduct]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a bank's obligations toward stressed borrowers, treat the Code of Conduct (section 5.2) as a mandated component of the Problem Loans Rules, not an optional policy: the Rules require banks to 'develop a written Code of Conduct' defining a robust resolution process that gives viable borrowers a chance at a workout before enforcement. This operationalises the Rules' stated objective of fair treatment and rehabilitation of viable borrowers (especially MSMEs). Conclude that absence of a compliant, board-level Code of Conduct is itself a gap against the Rules, and that borrower-communication and workout procedures must align with relevant Laws and Regulations.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Section 1.2):** "To ensure banks look into aspects of customer conduct and fair treatment whilst dealing with problem loans, especially in instances involving the MSMEs"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 11 / Section 5.2):** "Banks should develop a written Code of Conduct for managing problem loans ... to ensure that viable borrowers are provided a chance for reaching a workout solution, rather than invoking outright enforcement actions"

### [[Restructuring Viability Assessment]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When approving a restructuring of a problem loan, the Rules make viability a binding gate: long-term and short-term restructuring measures may 'only be considered viable' where specified documented affordability, arrears-treatment and repeat-restructuring conditions are met, including Senior Management Committee approval for repeat cases. The Rules also import 'Viability Assessment' as a defined term (borrower's ability to generate adequate cash flow to service outstanding loans). For a compliance decision, verify that each restructuring is supported by reasonable documented financial information demonstrating affordability and does not simply defer default; a restructuring failing these tests is not defensible as viable under section 5.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / 1.4):** "Viability Assessment: An assessment of borrower's ability to generate adequate cash flow in order to service outstanding loans."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / 5.1):** "Long-term restructuring measures should only be considered viable where the following conditions are met: ... the borrower can realistically afford the restructuring solution."

### [[SAMA Rules on Credit Risk Classification and Provisioning]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a bank's obligations for stressed and non-performing exposures, do not treat the Problem Loans Rules as self-contained: they are expressly issued to be read alongside the Credit Risk Classification and Provisioning Rules, so definitions and requirements on restructuring, forbearance, financial-difficulty identification, concession identification and stage allocation are sourced from the latter. Both instruments rest on SAMA's authority under the Banking Control Law and apply to all licensed banks. Practically, when determining whether a loan is problem/NPL or whether a restructuring counts as forbearance, verify the operative definitions in the Credit Risk Classification Rules rather than relying on the Problem Loans Rules alone.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / 1.1):** "These rules should be read in conjunction with SAMA rules on Credit Risk Classification and Provisioning."

### [[Workout Plan]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When reviewing how a bank resolves a non-performing exposure, treat the Workout Plan in section 6 as a mandatory, borrower-agreed and Management-Committee-approved instrument, and note it must align with the viability criteria and the borrower's sustainable repayment capacity. The Rules also require a documented, time-bound policy for mandatory transfer of loans from Loan Originating Units to the independent Workout Unit, and impose transparency and impact-analysis conditions on any workout fee so it does not further deteriorate the borrower's finances. For a compliance decision, check that each workout plan carries the required committee approval, matches repayment capacity, and that fee charging is documented and communicated.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 12 / 6):** "Banks should develop a workout plan agreed between the viable borrower and the bank ... matching the borrower's sustainable repayment capacity with the correct restructuring option(s)."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 4 / 1.2(iii)):** "To ensure banks have adequate controls over non-performing and problem loan management and restructuring processes, including documented policies and procedures."

### [[Workout Unit]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping a bank's problem-loan obligations under these Rules, treat the Workout Unit not as an optional operating choice but as a mandated organizational control the Rules impose. The Rules on Management of Problem Loans (issued under the Banking Control Law and applicable to all licensed banks) require banks to have adequate controls over NPL management and restructuring, and the Workout Unit provisions operationalize that by requiring a dedicated unit independent of loan-originating units to avoid conflicts of interest. A reviewer should therefore verify the unit exists, is independent, and is adequately staffed with the legal/analytical skill sets specified — its absence is a compliance gap, not a design preference.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / 1.2):** "To ensure banks have adequate controls over non-performing and problem loan management and restructuring processes, including documented policies and procedures."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 9 / Section 4):** "Banks should establish a dedicated Workout Department/Section/or Unit... The Workout Department/Section/or Unit should be independent of the Business/Loan Originating Units to avoid any potential conflicts of interest."
