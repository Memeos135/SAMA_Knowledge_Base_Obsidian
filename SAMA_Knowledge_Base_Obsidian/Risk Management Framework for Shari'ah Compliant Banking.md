# Risk Management Framework for Shari'ah Compliant Banking

## Connections

### [[Credit Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a bank's obligations for Shari'ah-compliant financing, treat Credit Risk not as a stand-alone topic but as one enumerated risk category the RMF requires banks to manage under Principle 2.0. The framework explicitly lists Credit Risk as an exposure banks 'should be aware' arises from Shari'ah-compliant activities, and Section 7 imposes concrete obligations (well-defined approval criteria, monitoring of counterparty ability/willingness to repay, alignment with SAMA responsible-lending requirements). You would conclude that Credit Risk obligations are enforceable requirements derived from and read within the parent RMF, and must be integrated with the bank's overall risk management under Section 6 rather than managed in isolation.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Introduction):** "the conduct of Shari'ah compliant banking activities may expose the bank to various types of risks including... Credit Risk"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / Section 7.1 (Principle 2.0)):** "Banks shall have in place appropriate methodologies and adequate systems... for identifying, measuring, managing and reporting the credit risk exposures arising from Shari'ah compliant financing products."

### [[Equity Investment Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping RMF obligations for a bank holding Mudarabah or Musharakah investments, note that Equity Investment Risk is a distinct enumerated category with its own Principle (7.0), not a generic market exposure. The framework's introduction lists it among the risks unique to Shari'ah-compliant banking, and Section 11 imposes specific duties: defining investment objectives/risk tolerance, maintaining review policies for acquiring/holding/exiting profit-sharing investments, and monitoring risk transformation across investment lifecycles. You would conclude that profit-sharing partnership exposures trigger dedicated obligations under the RMF that cannot be discharged solely through generic credit or market risk controls.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Introduction):** "various types of risks including, but not limited to... Equity Investment Risk"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / Section 11.1 (Principle 7.0)):** "Banks must have in place appropriate strategies, risk management and reporting processes in respect of the risk characteristics of equity investments, including Mudarabah and Musharakah investments."

### [[Liquidity Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping a Shari'ah-compliant bank's risk obligations, treat Liquidity Risk as one of the enumerated categories the RMF requires banks to identify, measure, mitigate and report. The framework's introduction lists Liquidity Risk among the exposures arising from Shari'ah-compliant activities, and Section 10 requires banks to periodically review their liquidity risk profile and management policies and implement measures as market conditions change. You would conclude that liquidity obligations here supplement, not replace, SAMA's existing prudential liquidity requirements and must be integrated into the overall risk framework under Section 6.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Introduction & Objective):** "various types of risks including... Liquidity Risk... to supplement existing risk management requirements issued by SAMA"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / Section 10.4):** "Banks should review their liquidity risk profile and liquidity management policies periodically as market condition changes and implement appropriate measures..."

### [[Market Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing market risk obligations for Shari'ah products, note that Market Risk is an enumerated RMF category governed by Principle 3.0 with detailed requirements in Section 8 — including an independent middle-office function, pre-determined risk limits, and specific attention to Salam, Parallel Salam, Sukuk and Murabahah asset volatility. The introduction lists Market Risk among the exposures the framework addresses. You would conclude that market risk controls must be calibrated to the specific Shari'ah contract types named in Section 8, and that reporting to senior management and board when limits are approached is an enforceable requirement, not discretionary.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Introduction):** "various types of risks including, but not limited to... Market Risk"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 11 / Section 8.1 (Principle 3.0)):** "Banks must have in place an appropriate framework for market risk management (including reporting) in respect of all assets held, including those that do not have a liquid market..."

### [[Operational Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the RMF's coverage, note that Operational Risk is a defined term and enumerated category the framework requires banks to manage, distinct from credit, market and liquidity risk. The introduction lists Operational Risk among Shari'ah-banking exposures and Section 4 defines it as 'the risk of losses resulting from inadequacy or failure of internal processes, people and systems, or from external events.' You would conclude that operational-risk obligations fall within the RMF's overarching Section 6 duty to identify, measure, mitigate, monitor and report all risks, integrated with the bank's overall risk management, rather than being an optional add-on.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Introduction):** "various types of risks including, but not limited to... Operational Risk"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 5 / Section 4 (Definitions)):** "Operational Risk: The risk of losses resulting from inadequacy or failure of internal processes, people and systems, or from external events."
- **Caveat:** The provided context for the Operational Risk node does not include a dedicated operational-risk section; the link rests on the definition and the introduction's enumeration. Verify whether a standalone operational-risk principle exists in the full text.

### [[PSIAs Rules]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping the risk obligations of a bank offering profit-sharing investment accounts, treat the RMF as importing the PSIAs Rules by reference rather than as a self-contained standard: clause 6.5 of the RMF expressly directs PSIA-offering banks to comply with 'the risk management requirements set out in the PSIAs Rules (September 2022) and any subsequent updates.' Because the RMF and PSIAs Rules are both issued under the same Banking Control Law authority, the PSIA-specific requirements are enforceable in their own right and are not fully reproduced in the RMF. You should therefore consult the current PSIAs Rules directly for PSIA risk/allocation obligations, and confirm you are using the latest version rather than relying only on the RMF summary.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 14 / 9.10):** "The policy relating to safeguarding the interests of their PSIA holders may include the following with reference to the PSIAs Rules issued by SAMA"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 9 / 6.5):** "Banks must also comply with the risk management requirements set out in the PSIAs Rules (September 2022) and any subsequent updates."

### [[Shari'ah Governance Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing a bank's Shari'ah-compliance obligations, do not read the RMF in isolation: its Introduction states the framework 'shall be read together with the Shari'ah Governance Framework for Shari'ah Compliant Banking issued by SAMA (February 2020) and any subsequent updates.' The two instruments are complementary — the RMF addresses identification and management of risk (including SNCR under operational risk), while the Governance Framework supplies the governance structures (e.g. Shari'ah Committee) that the RMF assumes are in place. For a compliance decision you must map obligations across both documents and use the current version of the Governance Framework, since RMF requirements presuppose its governance mechanisms.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 / Introduction):** "This framework shall be read together with the Shari'ah Governance Framework for Shari'ah Compliant Banking issued by SAMA (February 2020) and any subsequent updates."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 13 / 9.6):** "Banks shall ensure that they comply at all times with the Shari'ah rules and principles as approved/instructed by the banks' Shari'ah Committee"
