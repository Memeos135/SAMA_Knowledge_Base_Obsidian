# Exposures to Banks

## Connections

### [[Due Diligence Requirements]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When risk-weighting exposures to banks, do not treat rating-based bucketing as sufficient on its own: the due-diligence obligation in chapter 6 conditions and can override the base risk weight. Both provisions sit in the same SAMA credit-risk framework, and paragraph 7.16 (within Exposures to Banks) expressly ties back to the general duty in 6.1 to understand counterparties 'at origination and thereafter on a regular basis (at least annually).' Conclude that a bank must be able to demonstrate to SAMA that its due diligence supports the applied risk weight, and that due diligence can force a higher — never a lower — weight than the external rating implies.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 17 / para 7.16):** "Due diligence analysis must never result in the application of a lower risk weight than that determined by the external rating."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 10 / para 6.1):** "Banks must perform due diligence to ensure that they have an adequate understanding, at origination and thereafter on a regular basis (at least annually), of the risk profile ... of their counterparties."

### [[External Credit Risk Assessment Approach (ECRA)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When choosing how to risk-weight a bank exposure, the ECRA (external-ratings-based) route is one of two mutually exclusive methods in the Exposures to Banks chapter, applicable only where the counterparty carries an eligible external rating; unrated exposures fall to SCRA. Both concepts derive from chapter 7 of the same framework, and ECRA's usability depends on ratings from an ECAI 'recognized by SAMA.' Conclude that eligibility of the external assessment (and SAMA recognition of the rating agency) is a precondition to using ECRA — check the counterparty's rating status and ECAI eligibility before applying rating-based weights.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 17 / para 7.16):** "Banks must perform due diligence to ensure that the external ratings appropriately and conservatively reflect the creditworthiness of the bank counterparties."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 301 / para 20.8(2)):** "The external credit assessments must be from an eligible external credit assessment institution (ECAI) as recognized by SAMA"
- **Caveat:** Node B's context is drawn largely from the securitization SEC-ERBA pages; the ECRA/ECAI-recognition principle it captures applies to bank exposures via chapter 7, but verify the exact ECRA paragraphs for bank exposures in the primary text.

### [[Standardized Credit Risk Assessment Approach (SCRA)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When risk-weighting a bank exposure that has no external rating, the SCRA is the mandatory route: it applies 'to all their unrated bank exposures' and requires classification into Grades A, B or C. This is the direct complement to ECRA within the same chapter 7 of the SAMA credit-risk framework, so the two methods together exhaust the standardized treatment of bank exposures. Conclude that whether a counterparty bank is rated determines which method applies, and that SCRA's 30% preferential weight is conditional (CET1 ≥ 14%, Tier 1 leverage ≥ 5%, plus all Grade A requirements) — verify those thresholds before applying it.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 2 / Contents):** "Exposures to banks 15 ... 8. Standardized approach: the use of external rating 52"
- **Grounding — related node (Page 17 / para 7.17):** "Banks will apply the SCRA to all their unrated bank exposures. The SCRA requires banks to classify bank exposures into one of three risk-weight buckets (i.e. Grades A, B and C)"
