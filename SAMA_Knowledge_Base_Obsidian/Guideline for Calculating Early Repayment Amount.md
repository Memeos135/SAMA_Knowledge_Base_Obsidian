### [[Declining Balance Method]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When calculating an early-repayment settlement or the lender's permissible compensation, apply the declining-balance method as mandated by this Guideline: term cost must be distributed proportionally across installments on the basis of the outstanding principal at the start of each period, and on early repayment the borrower pays the remaining principal plus term cost for only the following three months, computed on the declining balance. This is the unified method SAMA imposes on all finance providers for monthly-installment contracts (personal, auto finance-lease, real estate). You would conclude that lender charges exceeding the three-month declining-balance ceiling (plus limited third-party costs) are non-compliant.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 4 (early repayment conditions)):** "reinvestment cost, not exceeding the term cost of the three months following repayment, calculated on the declining balance basis (paraphrase)"

### [[Implementing Regulation of Finance Companies Control Law]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining a borrower's early-repayment entitlement and the maximum compensation a financier may charge, read the Guideline as a subordinate explanatory instrument built directly on the Implementing Regulation of the Finance Companies Control Law: the Guideline expressly states it was prepared based on Articles 82 and 84 of that Regulation (alongside the Finance Lease Regulation and Consumer Finance rules). You should treat the Guideline's worked examples as interpretive aid but anchor any compliance decision on the primary Regulation text it cites.
