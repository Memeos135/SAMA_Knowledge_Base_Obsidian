# Minimum Capital Requirements for Market Risk

## Connections

### [[Banking Book  Trading Book Boundary|Banking Book / Trading Book Boundary]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining capital treatment of a hedge, the banking book / trading book boundary rules and the market risk capital requirements must be read as one interlocking regime, because whether an exposure sits inside or outside the market risk charge turns on the internal-risk-transfer conditions in the boundary provisions. The boundary section expressly conditions recognition on the external hedge meeting 'the requirements of paragraphs 9.73 to 9.74 and 9.76 9.77 of the SAMA Minimum Capital Requirements for Market Risk,' so the boundary determination directly drives what is included in or excluded from the market risk capital requirement. A reviewer should not classify a position under one document without checking the cross-referenced conditions in the other — e.g. a short banking-book credit position created by an internal risk transfer and not capitalised in the banking book must be capitalised under the market risk rules.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 24 / 5.22):** "both the trading book leg of the internal risk transfer and the external hedge must be included in the market risk capital requirements."

### [[Internal Models Approach (IMA)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining which capital methodology a bank may use for market risk, treat the IMA not as a free choice but as a conditional exception to the default Standardised Approach within the Market Risk framework. The framework requires all banks to compute the standardised charge and makes IMA use contingent on prior SAMA approval, per-trading-desk nomination, and qualitative and backtesting criteria; desks not approved must fall back to the standardised approach. Conclude that reliance on internal models is only defensible for specifically approved, nominated desks, and the standardised calculation remains a mandatory baseline.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 11 / 3.9):** "a bank may choose between two broad methodologies: the standardised approach ... and internal models approach (IMA) ... SAMA approval is required before using the IMA approach."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 90 / 10.4):** "The bank must nominate individual trading desks ... for which the bank seeks model approval in order to use the internal models approach (IMA)."

### [[SAMA Minimum Capital Requirements for Credit Risk]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When scoping capital treatment of internal risk transfers and hedges, do not read the Credit Risk and Market Risk frameworks in isolation: the same instrument (e.g. an external hedge or a banking-book credit position moved to the trading book) can shift between the two regimes depending on whether the [5.21] matching conditions are met. The Market Risk framework expressly conditions capital recognition of CVA/credit-derivative hedges on requirements that straddle both books, so a hedge that reduces a credit-risk capital charge only qualifies if the parallel market-risk treatment is also satisfied. Conclude that eligibility must be checked against both frameworks together, and that failing the market-risk condition removes the credit-risk offset.

### [[Standardized Approach]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining which capital calculation methodology binds a KSA bank, note that the term 'Standardised Approach' is regime-specific and not interchangeable: the credit-risk SA in SAMA_EN_3487 governs credit-RWA and feeds the output floor, while the market-risk framework (SAMA_EN_3553) mandates a distinct SA that all banks, D-SIBs and non-D-SIBs alike, must use to compute the market-risk capital charge. The link reflects that both frameworks deploy 'standardised approach' as a defined calculation basis, but they apply to separate risk classes and separate RWA computations (market-risk RWA is capital requirement × 12.5). For a compliance decision, do not assume a bank's credit-risk SA usage satisfies market-risk obligations; confirm the market-risk SA is applied separately as required under 6.1, and check the output-floor interaction where both feed capital adequacy.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 26 / para 6.1):** "all Banks (D-SIBs and Non D-SIBs) are required to calculate the market risk capital charge by using the Standardised Approach."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 733 / para 5.7):** "The standardized approaches to be used to calculate the base of the output floor ... (1) The standardized approach for credit risk."

### [[Trading Book Policy Statement]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether a bank's market-risk positions are correctly captured, start from the Trading Book Policy Statement (TPS): the Market Risk framework mandates that every bank with market-risk exposures maintain a TPS and defined trading desks, because book boundary and desk structure drive whether instruments and internal risk transfers receive trading-book versus banking-book treatment. The internal-risk-transfer rules in section 5 turn on how positions are assigned to the regulatory books, which the TPS governs. Conclude that TPS documentation and desk definitions are a prerequisite to defending any market-risk capital calculation and to the recognition of internal hedges.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 12 / 3.11):** "All banks with market risk exposures are required to have a Trading Book Policy Statement (TPS)."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 24 / 5.22):** "both the trading book leg of the internal risk transfer and the external hedge must be included in the market risk capital requirements."
