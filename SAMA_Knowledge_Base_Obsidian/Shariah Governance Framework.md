# Shariah Governance Framework

## Connections

### [[Board of Directors]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When allocating accountability for Shariah compliance in an Islamic bank, treat the board as the apex owner of the entire framework, not merely one of several functions. Article 5 makes the board 'primarily responsible for the overall Shariah governance framework' and requires it to approve the framework, exercise continuous oversight, and supervise implementation of committee decisions — so the SGF's requirements ultimately attach to the board as the responsible organ. In practice this means board-level approval and documented oversight of the framework are prerequisites, and gaps in the framework are treated as board-level failures rather than delegated deficiencies.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Art 2):** "It also aims to define the tasks and responsibilities of the board of directors, executive management, Shariah committee, compliance department, risk management department, and internal audit department."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 6 / Art 5(1)):** "The board is primarily responsible for the overall Shariah governance framework of the bank and the compliance of its Islamic banking activities with the Shariah principles and rules."

### [[Development Process of Shariah-Compliant Products]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When assessing whether an Islamic product can lawfully be offered, treat committee approval as a gating requirement under the SGF, not a formality. Article 18 requires that all Shariah issues in product development, design and process be referred to the committee, that committee approval be obtained for every product and subsequent amendment, and that both pre- and post-approval stages be covered. For a compliance decision this means a product offered or materially amended without documented committee approval is non-compliant, and the review must extend to legal contracts and related transaction documents.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 14 / Art 16 First(2)):** "overall Islamic banking operations at the bank, including the development process of Shariah-compliant products... (See Chapter Nine of this framework)."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 16 / Art 18(2)):** "The committee approval shall be obtained for all Islamic banking products to be presented and for any subsequent amendments."

### [[Executive Management]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping who executes (rather than approves) Shariah governance, the SGF assigns executive management the operational duties that make the board-approved framework function: referring Shariah issues to the committee, implementing committee decisions, and embedding a Shariah-compliance culture. Article 6 sets these as mandatory ('shall') obligations distinct from the board's oversight role under Article 5. For a compliance decision, this means implementation failures (e.g. failing to refer a product to the committee, or not enacting a committee decision) fall on management, and the framework should be read as separating board approval/oversight from management execution.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 3 / Art 2):** "to define the tasks and responsibilities of the board of directors, executive management, Shariah committee, compliance department, risk management department, and internal audit department."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 7 / Art 6(1)-(2)):** "The management shall identify and refer any Shariah issues to the Shariah committee for decisions... shall monitor and implement the Shariah decisions issued by the committee."

### [[Internal Shariah Audit]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When designing lines of assurance for an Islamic bank, treat internal Shariah audit as an independent third line whose findings must reach both the board audit committee and the Shariah committee, distinct from the ongoing Shariah compliance function. Chapter 7 frames it as 'independent assessment' providing objective assurance, requires auditors with adequate Shariah knowledge, and mandates dual reporting of findings. For a compliance decision this means Shariah audit cannot be collapsed into the compliance function, and audit findings that are not escalated to both governance bodies indicate a framework gap.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Art 4(3)):** "the committee shall report to the board of directors."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 15 / Art 16 Third):** "The findings and observations of the internal Shariah audit shall be submitted to both the board audit committee and the Shariah committee."

### [[Shariah Committee]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When determining who has final authority over Shariah rulings, treat the Shariah committee as the definitive decision-maker on which the board must rely, subject to the independence protections in the framework. Article 10 makes the committee responsible for all its Shariah decisions and requires the board to rely on it for such rulings, while Article 13 bars modifying or setting aside its decisions without its approval and requires direct reporting to the board. For a compliance decision this means a committee ruling cannot be overridden by management or board, and any product or transaction contradicting a committee decision is non-compliant regardless of internal approvals.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Art 4(5)):** "pillars that include effective tasks and responsibilities carried out by the board and the management, independence of the committee, and qualification of its members."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 11 / Art 10):** "The board must rely on the committee for issuing Shariah decisions related to engaging in Islamic banking activities."

### [[Shariah Compliance Function]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **What this link tells you:** When mapping which internal control obligations a bank running Islamic banking must discharge, treat the Shariah compliance function as a mandatory component of the broader Shariah Governance Framework, not a standalone practice. The framework (Article 4) requires policies, control mechanisms and reporting channels to ensure Islamic activities are Shariah-compliant 'at all times', and the compliance function in Article 16 operationalises that by regularly assessing operations, resolving non-compliances and feeding into product development. You should therefore scope Shariah compliance duties against the framework's stated structure and reporting lines, and not assume compliance is satisfied without the control and non-compliance-risk elements the framework mandates.
- **Grounding — this node (Page 5 / Art 4):** "The bank must establish a control mechanism to ensure that the objectives and operations of its Islamic banking activities are in compliance with Shariah principles and rules at all times."
- **Grounding — related node (Page 14 / Art 16):** "The Shariah compliance function refers to the regular assessment of the bank's Islamic activities and operations to ensure that they are Shariah compliant."
