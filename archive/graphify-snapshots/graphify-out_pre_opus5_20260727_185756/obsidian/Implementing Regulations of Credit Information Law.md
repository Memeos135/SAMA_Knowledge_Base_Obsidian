# Implementing Regulations of Credit Information Law

## Connections

### [[Credit Information Law]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **Why:** The Implementing Regulations are expressly subordinate to and derive their authority from the Credit Information Law (Royal Decree No. M/37); every operative obligation in the Regulations is framed as being 'according to the Law and its Implementing Regulations', confirming the parent-child legal relationship between the two instruments.
- **This node (Page 1 / Article 1):** "Implementing Regulations: Implementing Regulations of the Credit Information Law."
- **Related node (Page 1 / Article 1):** "The Law: The Credit Information Law promulgated by Royal decree No. M/37, dated 5/7/1429H."
- **Implication:** Any compliance gap analysis must trace obligations back to both instruments simultaneously; a control mapped only to the Implementing Regulations may miss a higher-order statutory duty in the Law itself, which an examiner would escalate differently.

### [[Saudi Central Bank (SAMA)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **Why:** The Implementing Regulations repeatedly designate SAMA as the competent authority that approves, supervises, and issues binding working rules for credit information companies, making SAMA an integral regulatory actor named within the Regulations' operative provisions.
- **This node (Page 9 / Article 25):** "gather credit information from members in line with the criteria approved by the company and which include administrative, technical and legal requirements as well as the working rules approved by SAMA"
- **Related node (Page 1 / Article 1):** "SAMA: Saudi Central Bank (SAMA)*. Governor: Governor of Saudi Central Bank."
- **Implication:** Credit information companies must be able to evidence that their data-collection criteria and working rules have received SAMA approval; an audit trail of SAMA-approved rule versions and the dates of approval is a mandatory supervisory artefact.
