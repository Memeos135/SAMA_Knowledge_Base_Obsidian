# Fraud Response Plan

- **Why:** The AML Law establishes the predicate-offence framework and legal-person liability that underlies fraud as a crime generating proceeds subject to AML obligations; the Fraud Response Plan operationalises the detection-and-reporting duty that arises once fraud (a predicate offence) is suspected, creating a procedural bridge between the criminal law and the institutional response regime.

- **Implication:** A Fraud Response Plan must be designed to trigger AML/STR reporting workflows when fraud is detected, since fraud proceeds constitute predicate-offence funds under the AML Law; the plan and the STR process should be integrated in system design and tested jointly.
- **Caveat:** The Fraud Response Plan document (SAMA_EN_2217_VER1) does not explicitly cite the AML Law by article; the link is inferred from the predicate-offence nexus and SAMA supervisory authority over both regimes.
