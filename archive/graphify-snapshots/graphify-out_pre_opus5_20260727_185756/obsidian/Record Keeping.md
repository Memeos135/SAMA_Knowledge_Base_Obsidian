# Record Keeping

## Connections

### [[Anti-Money Laundering Law]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **Why:** Article 12 of the AML Law expressly governs the manner of record keeping and the minimum retention period, making the Record Keeping section of the Guide a direct operational elaboration of that statutory obligation.
- **This node (Page 45 / Section 6):** "Article (12) of the Anti-Money Laundering Law and Article (65) of the Law on Combating Terrorism Crimes and Financing state the financial institution's obligations relating to the manner of record keeping, the minimum record-keeping period, and the possibility for extending that…"
- **Related node (Page 5 / Chapter IV Definitions):** "Anti-Money Laundering Law: The Anti-Money Laundering Law issued by Royal Decree No. (M/20) dated 05/02/1439H."
- **Implication:** Electronic archiving systems must retain CDD records, transaction data, SAR-related investigation files, and unsuccessful-measure documentation for a minimum of ten years in a retrievable and auditable format, subject to periodic effectiveness testing (at least annually) and on-demand production to SAMA.

### [[Enhanced Due Diligence Measures]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **Why:** The EDD section cross-references the Record Keeping section directly, making documentation of enhanced measures a mandatory output of every EDD action; the record-keeping obligations (minimum 10-year retention, retrievable/auditable format) therefore apply with full force to all EDD evidence collected.
- **This node (Page 45 / Para 6.1):** "The financial institution shall keep records for a period of no less than ten years from the date of the end of the business relationship or contract, conclusion of the transaction, or closure of the account."
- **Related node (Page 39 / Para 4.8):** "When taking enhanced customer due diligence measures, the financial institution shall properly document these measures in accordance with Paragraph (6.1) in the Record Keeping Section."
- **Implication:** Every EDD action (senior management approval, source-of-wealth evidence, enhanced monitoring output) must be captured in the record-keeping system with a minimum 10-year retention tag and must be retrievable for SAMA examination on demand.

### [[Law on Combating Terrorism Crimes and Financing]] — `cites` [EXTRACTED]
- **Why:** Article 65 of the Law on Combating Terrorism Crimes and Financing is directly cited in the AML/CTF Guide's Record Keeping section as one of the two statutory bases defining the manner of record keeping, minimum retention periods, and the authority to extend those periods, making the CTF Law a co-equal legal source for the record-keeping obligation alongside Article 12 of the AML Law.
- **This node (Page 45 / Section 6):** "Article (12) of the Anti-Money Laundering Law and Article (65) of the Law on Combating Terrorism Crimes and Financing state the financial institution's obligations relating to the manner of record keeping, the minimum record-keeping period, and the possibility for extending that…"
- **Related node (Page 5 / Chapter IV Definitions):** "Paragraph (4) of the Implementing Regulations of the Law on Combating Terrorism Crimes and their Financing stipulates that 'The supervisory authorities… referred to in Article 1/22 of the Law shall include (4) Saudi Central Bank.'"
- **Implication:** Record-keeping systems must satisfy dual statutory authority (AML Law Art. 12 and CTF Law Art. 65) simultaneously; a SAMA examiner will check that retention parameters, access controls, and extension procedures are configured to meet both laws, not just the AML Law alone.

### [[SAMA (Saudi Central Bank)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **Why:** The Record Keeping section imposes a direct regulatory obligation on financial institutions to make records available to SAMA upon request, and constrains outsourcing arrangements so that SAMA retains supervisory access rights to underlying records at all times.
- **This node (Page 46 / Art 6.4):** "the financial institution provides SAMA with the necessary records as requested"
- **Related node (Page 1 / Title page):** "The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing (AML/CTF) Guide … Saudi Central Bank"
- **Implication:** Record management systems — including third-party outsourced archives — must contractually preserve SAMA's right of direct access to AML/CTF records; the institution must be able to retrieve and produce any record on demand, with periodic tests (at least annually) evidencing system effectiveness.

### [[Wire Transfer]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **Why:** The Wire Transfer section explicitly cross-references the Record Keeping section as the governing retention standard for beneficiary identity verification data collected during wire transfers, and record keeping para 6.8 specifically addresses wire transfer record obligations, creating a direct normative cross-reference between the two regimes.
- **This node (Page 47 / Art 6.8):** "6.8 When conducting wire trans[fers] … [record keeping obligations apply]"
- **Related node (Page 67 / Art 14.6 area):** "the financial institution receiving a wire transfer shall verify the beneficiary's identity and keep this information as stated under the Record Keeping Section."
- **Implication:** Wire transfer processing systems must retain originator and beneficiary data fields (name, account/reference number, address/ID, purpose, relationship) for a minimum of ten years in a retrievable and auditable format, with records available to SAMA and competent authorities upon request — this data retention requirement must be built into the payment messaging and archiving infrastructure.
