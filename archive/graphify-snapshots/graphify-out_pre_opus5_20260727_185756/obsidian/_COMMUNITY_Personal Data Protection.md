# Personal Data Protection

**Cohesion:** 0.24 - loosely connected
**Members:** 11 nodes

## Why this community

This community maps the intersection of the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) and the Credit Information Law as they apply to credit data processing by Credit Information Companies operating under SAMA supervision. It identifies where the PDPL's Controller/Processor/Data Subject framework overlays the credit-specific consent and record-handling obligations, creating a dual-regime compliance burden for entities processing credit data.

## How members connect

- Credit data as a PDPL-regulated personal data category: Credit Data Processing is explicitly referenced under the PDPL, treating credit records as personal data subject to PDPL obligations in addition to the Credit Information Law's sector-specific rules.
- Dual supervisory authority: SAMA (Saudi Central Bank) is the sector regulator under the Credit Information Law; the Competent Authority (PDPL) (the SDAIA/NDMO framework) supervises PDPL compliance — Credit Information Companies face oversight from both, with potential for divergent enforcement.
- Controller/Processor distinction applied to credit data flows: Credit Information Companies acting as data aggregators may be Controllers; member lenders submitting data may be joint Controllers or Processors depending on the arrangement — the PDPL's definitional framework must be mapped onto the Membership Agreement structure.
- Data Subject rights as an overlay on Consumer rights: PDPL Data Subject rights (access, correction, erasure) run alongside the Credit Information Law's Consumer objection and consent rights — entities must satisfy both regimes when a Consumer/Data Subject challenges their Credit Record.
- Credit Information Law as lex specialis: The PDPL references the Credit Information Law as a domain-specific instrument, suggesting the Credit Information Law's consent and disclosure rules are the primary standard for credit data, with the PDPL filling residual gaps.
- SAMA's dual role: SAMA (Saudi Central Bank) appears both as the Credit Information Law regulator and as a Saudi Central Bank (PDPL) reference node, indicating SAMA's internal data processing activities are themselves subject to PDPL obligations.
