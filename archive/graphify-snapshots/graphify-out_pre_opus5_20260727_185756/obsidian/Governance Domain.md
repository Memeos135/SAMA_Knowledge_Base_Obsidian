# Governance Domain

## Connections

### [[Counter-Fraud Framework]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **Why:** The Governance Domain is the first and foundational domain of the Counter-Fraud Framework; the Framework explicitly positions Board and Executive Leadership accountability, the Counter-Fraud Governance Committee (CFGC), and the Counter-Fraud Programme as prerequisite structural requirements that enable all other domains.
- **This node (Page 14 / Section 3):** "The Board and Executive Leadership of the Member Organisation is ultimately responsible for creation of a Counter-Fraud Programme; providing leadership and direction; and projecting a Counter-Fraud culture inside and outside the organisation."
- **Related node (Page 9 / Section 2.1):** "The Framework is structured around four main domains, namely: Governance, Prevent, Detect, Respond… The Control Requirements reflect the mandated Counter-Fraud controls that should be considered by Member Organisations when designing and implementing a Counter-Fraud Programme."
- **Implication:** Compliance officers must ensure that CFGC establishment, Board endorsement of the Counter-Fraud Strategy/Policy/Risk Appetite, and appointment of a Head of Counter-Fraud are evidenced before the organisation can claim any maturity level above 0 in any Framework domain.

### [[Counter-Fraud Governance Committee (CFGC)]] — `references` [EXTRACTED]
- **Why:** The CFGC is a mandatory structural element of the Governance domain: the Governance sub-domain 3.1 requires Member Organisations to establish and maintain the CFGC, and the CFGC is the formal approval and oversight body for all key governance artefacts (strategy, policy, fraud risk appetite) described throughout Section 3.
- **This node (Page 14 / Section 3.1):** "Member Organisations should establish and maintain a dedicated Counter-Fraud Governance Committee (CFGC)... headed by a member of the Executive Committee (e.g., CEO, CRO or equivalent)."
- **Related node (Page 18 / Section 3.4):** "Approving the CFGC charter... Endorsing (after being approved by the CFGC): the Counter-Fraud Strategy... Counter-Fraud Policy... output of the Fraud Risk Assessment... Fraud Risk Appetite."
- **Implication:** Examiners will expect to see a formally constituted CFGC with a board-endorsed charter, documented membership at executive level, and evidence of CFGC approval for each of the five endorsed artefacts (strategy, policy, FRA output, risk appetite, roles); absence of any one constitutes a Governance domain control gap.
